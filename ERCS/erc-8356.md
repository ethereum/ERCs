## Abstract

This standard defines a consent grant in which three principals are distinguished: a **subject** who
consents, a **grantee** who receives access, and an optional **agent** that acts on the grantee's
behalf. The subject is not the beneficiary. The grant is bound to a declared purpose drawn from a
versioned code registry, carries a validity window and a usage cap, may be attenuated into sub-grants
that can never exceed their parent, and can be revoked either in whole by the subject or in part by
terminating only the agent leg. Grants are minted against an existing asset token so that consent
appears in that asset's provenance.

The on-chain record is a **status anchor**, not the instrument. The instrument, the human-readable
authorization the subject actually assented to, remains off-chain and is referenced by hash. What the
chain contributes is public, non-repudiable, consensus-ordered revocation, and the ability to prove
that a grant existed before a given access.

## Motivation

Delegated access to regulated data is now routinely exercised by software agents acting for a human
principal. Existing standards cannot express this safely, for three reasons.

A datum about a person is rarely a single object. It is a chain of derived artifacts, each produced
by a different custodian and each further from the person than the last. A biological sample is the
clearest case:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  subgraph Person["The person"]
    Sample["Biosample"]
  end
  subgraph Lab["Sequencing laboratory"]
    FASTQ["FASTQ"]
    VCF["VCF"]
  end
  subgraph Annot["Annotation service"]
    AVCF["Annotated VCF"]
    Store["Queryable record"]
  end
  subgraph Model["Model provider"]
    Extract["Training extract"]
    Vectors["Embeddings"]
    Weights["Model weights"]
  end
  Sample -- "sequencing" --> FASTQ
  FASTQ -- "variant calling" --> VCF
  VCF -- "annotation" --> AVCF
  AVCF -- "indexing" --> Store
  Store -- "extraction" --> Extract
  Extract -- "embedding" --> Vectors
  Vectors -- "training" --> Weights
```

One consent decision at the left governs seven transformations across four custodians, and the person
is asked again at none of them. Two properties follow, and no existing standard provides either. The
permission must compose along the chain, narrowing at each hop and never widening. And withdrawal
must run the chain in reverse, which it can do until it reaches model weights, where deletion is not
available and only measurement and accounting remain.

**Every existing delegation standard is two-party.** In [ERC-4907](./eip-4907.md) and [ERC-5006](./eip-5006.md) a token owner grants a
time-boxed `user` role over their own token. In [ERC-8226](./eip-8226.md) a principal delegates scoped, capped
authority to an agent over the principal's own asset, with the mandate keyed `(agent, principal, target)`.
In every case the party who consents is the party who benefits, and the party at risk is the party
who signed. The case this standard addresses is structurally different: a subject consents to a
grantee's access to data *about the subject*, and the harm from an unauthorized act falls on someone
who is not a party to the transaction at all.

Other standards do model a subject. [ERC-8328](./eip-8328.md) defines `subjectId`, `ROLE_SUBJECT`
and `ROLE_BENEFICIARY`; World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) Verifiable Credentials (VC) 2.0 separates `credentialSubject` from `holder` and `issuer`. What
neither does is make that non-party subject's withdrawal **operative for a relying party at
access time**. ERC-8328 says so itself: it "does not define compliance policy... transfer
restrictions", and its `OUTCOME_REVOKED` is a recorded assertion that nothing consults. That is the
gap this specification closes, and it is the whole of the claim.

**The agent cannot be revoked separately from the human.** Where an agent is modeled, it is modeled
as an extension of the principal. There is no way to say "this researcher retains access, but the
tool they were using does not," which is precisely the action an incident response requires when a
model provider, a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server, or a session key is compromised.

**Purpose is not enforced anywhere.** Regulated-data authorizations are purpose-limited by
construction, and no EIP enforces purpose at all. Every regulatory-compliance ERC to date
([ERC-3643](./eip-3643.md), [ERC-7518](./eip-7518.md), [ERC-8106](./eip-8106.md), ERC-8226, [ERC-8320](./eip-8320.md)) encodes securities and real-world-asset
vocabulary: accreditation, jurisdiction blocks, anti-money laundering (AML) flags, transfer caps. A fund unit has no purpose
limitation, so the vocabulary was never needed. Data has one.

There is also a concrete gap in the incumbent non-blockchain practice this standard is meant to
interoperate with rather than replace. The Global Alliance for Genomics and Health (GA4GH) Authentication and Authorization Infrastructure
profile, the incumbent mechanism for credentialed access to controlled genomic datasets, allows a Visa Issuer
to provide tokens of this type without any revocation process; it defines `exp` as explicitly not
a hard cutoff, noting that access is not necessarily removed when the `exp` timestamp passes; and it
rate-limits the only active validity check, allowing polling at most once per hour per Passport
Clearinghouse. There is no revocation list and no push mechanism. Worst-case
propagation of a withdrawn consent is therefore bounded only by token lifetime and an hourly poll.

A withdrawal that takes an hour to propagate is a poor fit for legal regimes whose operative triggers
are knowledge and ease. This standard's contribution is to make the revocation itself public,
timestamped, non-repudiable, and immediately readable by any relying party, without moving the
authorization document, the identity, or the data on-chain.

## Specification

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT",
"RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as
described in RFC 2119 and RFC 8174.

### Definitions

- **Subject**: the natural or legal person whose data the grant concerns, and whose assent the grant
  records. The subject is not required to be the owner of the asset token.
- **Grantee**: the party authorized to exercise the grant.
- **Agent**: an optional principal that exercises the grant on the grantee's behalf.
- **Instrument**: the off-chain authorization document the subject assented to, referenced on-chain
  by `termsRef` and never stored on-chain.
- **Agent operator**: the party accountable for the agent, typically the organization running the
  software the agent is. Named separately from the agent because containing a compromised agent must
  not require the agent itself to cooperate. The grant records it in `agentOperator`, and the subject
  signs it together with the agent.
- **Issuer**: the party that runs the programme or study under which grants are made, identified by
  the contract's `isIssuer` function. It is not a party to any individual grant.
- **Subject key**: the address whose signatures authorize every subject-authored act for one grant.

### Data model

```solidity
enum Status {
    NONE,                  // 0  unset
    ACTIVE,                // 1
    REVOKED_BY_SUBJECT,    // 2  withdrawal by the subject
    RENOUNCED_BY_GRANTEE,  // 3  handed back by the grantee
    TERMINATED_BY_ISSUER,  // 4  programme or study closed
    SUSPENDED,             // 5  temporary hold; resume is the only non-terminal return to ACTIVE
    EXHAUSTED              // 6  usesMax reached
}

enum AgentKind {
    NONE,                  // 0  no agent leg
    SESSION_KEY,           // 1  ephemeral key that calls as msg.sender
    EXTERNAL_REGISTRY,     // 2  reference into an agent identity registry (e.g. ERC-8004)
    TOKEN_BOUND_ACCOUNT,   // 3  ERC-6551 account
    ASSERTED_UNVERIFIED    // 4  claimed by the grantee, not proven
}

enum AgentStatus {
    NONE,                  // 0  no agent leg
    ACTIVE,                // 1
    REVOKED                // 2  agent leg ended; the grant itself is unaffected
}

enum SuspendedBy {
    NONE,                  // 0  not suspended
    GRANTEE,               // 1
    AGENT_OPERATOR         // 2
}
```

A conforming implementation MUST store, for each grant, the following fields with these types and
meanings:

```solidity
struct ConsentGrant {
    // slot 0
    bytes32 scopeRef;           // resource hash (the Merkle leaf), or Merkle root if FLAG_SCOPE_IS_ROOT
    // slot 1
    bytes32 purposeProfileRef;  // hash of the canonical purpose profile document
    // slot 2
    bytes32 termsRef;           // hash of the versioned template instrument, never a per-subject copy
    // slot 3
    bytes32 parentGrantId;      // 0 for a root grant
    // slot 4
    uint256 assetTokenId;       // the token, in assetContract, this grant is recorded against
    // slot 5
    bytes32 subjectCommit;      // commitment to a per-grant subject key; never an address
    // slot 6
    address grantee;
    uint48  notBefore;
    uint48  expiresAt;
    // slot 7
    address agent;
    uint32  ops;                // permission bitmask
    uint32  purposeCode;        // index into the purpose registry
    uint8   status;             // Status
    uint8   flags;
    uint16  depth;              // delegation depth, 0 for a root grant
    // slot 8
    uint8   agentKind;          // AgentKind
    uint8   agentStatus;        // AgentStatus; revocable independently of `status`
    uint8   suspendedBy;        // SuspendedBy; nonzero only while status is SUSPENDED
    uint48  lastUsedAt;         // block timestamp of the last accepted commitUsage
    uint16  useCount;           // cumulative committed uses across epochs; saturates at its maximum
    uint16  usesMax;            // 0 means unlimited
    uint64  usageEpoch;         // epoch of the last accepted commitUsage
    uint32  usageCount;         // count of the last accepted commitUsage within usageEpoch
    // slot 9
    address agentOperator;      // zero when there is no agent leg
    // slot 10
    address assetContract;      // the token contract of assetTokenId; two contracts can share a token id
}
```

The stored fields, their types and their meanings are normative. Field order and slot packing are
informative: the layout above occupies eleven storage slots (0 to 10), and reordering costs gas on every
grant without changing behaviour. `purposeProfileRef`, `termsRef` and the `releaseHash` of the
purpose registry are each `keccak256` of the exact bytes of the document they reference; for a
purpose profile those bytes are its canonical form (see Purpose profile).

**Calldata encoding.** `grant` and `subGrant` take the narrow input struct `GrantInput`, never
`ConsentGrant`: a struct of the width of `ConsentGrant` in an external function signature generates
an application binary interface (ABI) decoder that exceeds the Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM)
stack, and does not compile, even with the intermediate representation (IR) pipeline enabled. In
`GrantInput` the seven small scalars (`notBefore`, `expiresAt`, `ops`, `purposeCode`,
`usesMax`, `agentKind`, `flags`) are packed into the single word `policy`, laid out as in the policy
table below. The helper `encodePolicy(notBefore, expiresAt, ops, purposeCode, usesMax, agentKind,
flags)` returns that word and `decodePolicy(policy)` returns the seven scalars. They are conveniences
and not part of the interface. An input struct MUST NOT expose the contract-controlled fields (`status`, `depth`, `parentGrantId`,
`agentStatus`, `suspendedBy`, `useCount`, `lastUsedAt`, `usageEpoch`, `usageCount`): a caller able to
set its own `status` could forge an active grant, and one able to set `depth` could escape the
delegation bound.

`flags` bits: `FLAG_SCOPE_IS_ROOT = 0x01`, `FLAG_DELEGABLE = 0x02`,
`FLAG_DESTROY_DERIVATIVES = 0x04`. Bits `0x08` through `0x80` are reserved and MUST be zero.

`ops` bits, normative. Without fixed values, two parties assign different meanings to the same
bit position: a grant minted to permit evaluation is read by the data holder as permitting export,
and the failure direction is disclosure:

| bit | name | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `0x01` | `READ` | retrieve the resource |
| `0x02` | `QUERY` | evaluate over it without retrieving it |
| `0x04` | `DERIVE` | produce a new artifact from it |
| `0x08` | `EXPORT` | move it outside the enforcing boundary |
| `0x10` | `TRAIN` | use it to fit model parameters |

Bits `0x20` and above are reserved. **An unrecognized bit MUST NOT be treated as granting
anything.** Implementations MAY additionally expose these as [ERC-6617](./eip-6617.md) permissions;
the values are fixed here because a grant minted by one party is evaluated by another, and indices
assigned independently collide.

### Interface

```solidity
interface IConsentGrant /* is IERC165 */ {

    event ConsentGranted(
        bytes32 indexed grantId, address indexed assetContract, address indexed grantee,
        uint256 assetTokenId, uint32 purposeCode, uint48 expiresAt);
    event ConsentSubGranted(bytes32 indexed grantId, bytes32 indexed parentGrantId, uint16 depth);
    event ConsentRevoked(bytes32 indexed grantId, bytes32 indexed by, uint8 status, bytes32 reasonCode);
    event AgentRevoked(bytes32 indexed grantId, address indexed agent, bytes32 reasonCode);
    event ScopeLeafRevoked(bytes32 indexed grantId, bytes32 leaf);
    event GrantSuspended(bytes32 indexed grantId, address indexed by, bytes32 reasonCode);
    event GrantResumed(bytes32 indexed grantId, address indexed by);
    event UsageCommitted(bytes32 indexed grantId, uint64 epoch, bytes32 headHash, uint32 count);

    /// Subject-authored acts take the subject salt: the contract opens `subjectCommit` as
    /// keccak256(abi.encode(subjectSalt, subjectKey)) with subjectKey equal to msg.sender here,
    /// or to the `subjectKey` argument whose signature is checked in the `WithSig` variants.
    function revokeBySubject(bytes32 grantId, bytes32 reasonCode, bytes32 subjectSalt) external;
    function renounceByGrantee(bytes32 grantId) external;
    function revokeAgent(bytes32 grantId, bytes32 reasonCode) external;
    function revokeScopeLeaf(bytes32 grantId, bytes32 leaf, bytes32 subjectSalt) external;

    /// Relayed equivalents. A subject holding no fee token MUST still be able to withdraw,
    /// so every subject-authored act has a signed path a third party can submit.
    function revokeBySubjectWithSig(
        bytes32 grantId, bytes32 reasonCode, bytes32 subjectSalt,
        address subjectKey, bytes calldata subjectSig) external;
    function revokeScopeLeafWithSig(
        bytes32 grantId, bytes32 leaf, bytes32 subjectSalt,
        address subjectKey, bytes calldata subjectSig) external;

    /// Incident-response hold. Only the grantee or the agent operator. See Status transitions.
    function suspend(bytes32 grantId, bytes32 reasonCode) external;
    /// Clears SUSPENDED back to ACTIVE. Only the party recorded in `suspendedBy`, never an operator
    /// undoing a subject withdrawal.
    function resume(bytes32 grantId) external;
    /// Programme or study closure. Only an account for which `isIssuer` returns true.
    function terminateByIssuer(bytes32 grantId, bytes32 reasonCode) external;

    function commitUsage(bytes32 grantId, uint64 epoch, bytes32 headHash, uint32 count) external;

    function checkAccess(
        bytes32 grantId, uint32 purposeCode, uint32 op,
        bytes32 resourceHash, bytes32[] calldata proof
    ) external view returns (bool);

    function isGrantActive(bytes32 grantId) external view returns (bool);

    /// The status to show: that of the nearest grant, from this one up to the root, whose
    /// status is not ACTIVE (see Effective status). `isGrantActive` remains the authorization answer.
    function effectiveStatus(bytes32 grantId) external view returns (uint8);

    /// Whether `leaf` has been withdrawn via `revokeScopeLeaf` on this grant or any ancestor.
    function isLeafRevoked(bytes32 grantId, bytes32 leaf) external view returns (bool);

    function subjectOf(bytes32 grantId) external view returns (bytes32 subjectCommit);

    /// Monotonic per-subject nonce for signed grants. Without it a relayed grant is
    /// replayable against the same contract.
    function nonces(bytes32 subjectCommit) external view returns (uint256);

    function revocationEpoch() external view returns (uint256);

    /// The purpose registry (IPurposeRegistry); the zero address means no registry is set.
    function purposeRegistry() external view returns (address);

    /// The maximum delegation depth this implementation accepts.
    function maxDepth() external view returns (uint16);

    /// Whether `account` holds the issuer role used by `terminateByIssuer`.
    function isIssuer(address account) external view returns (bool);

    /// Mint a root grant. Under path (a) of Authorship of consent, `subjectKey` is msg.sender and
    /// `subjectSig` is empty; under path (b), `subjectSig` is the subject's signature of the
    /// `Grant` typed data, checked against `subjectKey`.
    function grant(
        GrantInput calldata g, bytes32 subjectSalt, address subjectKey, bytes calldata subjectSig
    ) external returns (bytes32 grantId);

    /// Mint an attenuated child of `parentGrantId`. See Attenuation.
    function subGrant(
        bytes32 parentGrantId, GrantInput calldata g, bytes32[] calldata scopeProof
    ) external returns (bytes32 grantId);

    /// The stored fields of a grant; a grant that does not exist returns an all-zero struct.
    function getGrant(bytes32 grantId) external view returns (ConsentGrant memory);
}
```

The [ERC-165](./eip-165.md) interface identifier of `IConsentGrant` is `0x85dfe863`, the XOR of the selectors of the
functions above.

`GrantInput` MUST carry exactly the fields of the signed `Grant` typed data below, in that order, and
no contract-controlled field. Its field types, and so the selectors of `grant` and `subGrant`, are
normative:

```solidity
struct GrantInput {
    bytes32 scopeRef;
    bytes32 purposeProfileRef;
    bytes32 termsRef;
    address assetContract;
    uint256 assetTokenId;
    bytes32 subjectCommit;
    address grantee;
    address agent;
    address agentOperator;
    uint256 policy;             // packed scalars; layout in the policy table
}
```

### Required behaviour

**Subject commitment.** `subjectCommit` is the only on-chain reference to the subject. It MUST
be formed as:

```
subjectCommit = keccak256(abi.encode(subjectSalt, subjectKey))
```

where `subjectSalt` is drawn from at least 128 bits of entropy, is unique to the grant, and is
**never written to contract storage or events**, and `subjectKey` is the address whose signatures authorize every
subject-authored act for that grant: an externally owned account (EOA), whose signature is checked
by recovery, or an [ERC-1271](./eip-1271.md) contract account, whose signature is checked by calling
`isValidSignature`. A signature does not reveal which contract account produced it, so every signed
path takes `subjectKey` as an explicit argument. A bare `keccak256(subjectKey)` without a salt MUST NOT be used: an
address space is enumerable, and Privacy rule 2 forbids unsalted hashes over enumerable spaces. A
fresh `subjectKey` MUST be generated for each grant (Privacy rule 7), so that a long-lived wallet
address is not the opening.

**Authorship of consent.** `grant` takes `subjectKey` as an argument, MUST revert when
`g.subjectCommit` is zero, and MUST require either (a) `msg.sender == subjectKey`, with an empty
`subjectSig`, for an opening of `g.subjectCommit`, or (b) a valid [EIP-712](./eip-712.md) signature
by `subjectKey`, verified with ERC-1271 support for contract accounts, over the normative typed
data below. An implementation MUST NOT permit an operator, issuer, or minter to create a grant on
a subject's behalf without such authorship. Every call path that accepts `subjectSalt` (`grant`,
`revokeBySubject`, `revokeScopeLeaf`, both `WithSig` variants and `bindContent` of the Content
Credentials profile) MUST treat it as authentication
material, not as chain state, and MUST NOT store it or emit it in an event.

**Opening the commitment.** Every subject-authored call takes `subjectSalt`, and the contract MUST
check that `keccak256(abi.encode(subjectSalt, subjectKey))` equals the grant's `subjectCommit`, where
`subjectKey` is `msg.sender` on the direct paths and the `subjectKey` argument on the `WithSig`
paths and on `grant` path (b), and only then verify the signature against that address. The salt
travels in the calldata of that call, which is public. From the first subject-authored call of a
grant, `grant` included, the subject key of that one grant is therefore public. It discloses
nothing about any other grant, because a fresh subject key is used for each grant (Privacy
rule 7). Where the subject key is a contract account, deploying one account per grant can link the
grants through the deployer or the funder; avoiding that linkage is the subject's responsibility
and outside this standard.

The signed payload is normative, because a signature that is not portable across implementations
defeats the purpose of naming EIP-712 at all. The domain is `name: "ConsentGrant"`, `version: "1"`,
with the chain id and the verifying contract address. The struct is:

```
Grant(bytes32 scopeRef,bytes32 purposeProfileRef,bytes32 termsRef,address assetContract,uint256 assetTokenId,bytes32 subjectCommit,address grantee,address agent,address agentOperator,uint256 policy,uint256 nonce)
```

`nonce` is the current value of `nonces(subjectCommit)`, and the contract consumes it when it
accepts the signature.

`policy` packs the seven small scalars, and its layout is normative here even though the
calldata encoding is not. A signed payload that referenced an implementation-defined encoding
would produce a different digest per implementation, which is the portability failure this
paragraph exists to prevent:

| bits | field | type |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 47 | `notBefore` | `uint48` |
| 48 to 95 | `expiresAt` | `uint48` |
| 96 to 127 | `ops` | `uint32` |
| 128 to 159 | `purposeCode` | `uint32` |
| 160 to 175 | `usesMax` | `uint16` |
| 176 to 183 | `agentKind` | `uint8` |
| 184 to 191 | `flags` | `uint8` |

Bits 192 and above are reserved and MUST be zero. A known limitation: a wallet cannot render
seven packed fields to the person being asked to consent, which is the harm EIP-712 exists to
prevent. The `Grant` type above is the only signed form: an implementation MUST NOT accept the same
grant under a different typed-data type, because a wallet or verifier could not tell which type a
contract expects, and that is the portability failure the packed layout prevents.

**Revocation typed data.** Subject-signed withdrawals use the same domain, with these types, which
carry no nonce:

```
RevokeBySubject(bytes32 grantId,bytes32 reasonCode)
RevokeScopeLeaf(bytes32 grantId,bytes32 leaf)
```

A signed withdrawal names one grant, and both withdrawals are terminal: `revokeBySubject` reverts
once the grant has left `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`, and `revokeScopeLeaf` reverts once the leaf is
withdrawn. A replayed signature therefore reinstates nothing and cannot advance `revocationEpoch` a
second time. A nonce shared with `grant` would let a party holding two signatures for the same nonce
spend one to invalidate the other, which a withdrawal must never allow.

**Replay.** Domain separation alone stops cross-chain and cross-contract replay and does nothing
about replay against the same contract. Without a nonce a relayer holding one `subjectSig` can call
`grant` again after the subject has revoked, minting a fresh identical ACTIVE grant. That is
reinstatement by another name, and it defeats two MUSTs in this document. Implementations therefore
MUST maintain a monotonic per-subject nonce keyed by `subjectCommit`, MUST expose it as
`nonces(bytes32)`, and MUST consume it whenever a `grant` signature is accepted. Signed
revocations are replay-safe without one, as stated under Revocation typed data.

**Grant identifiers.** `grant` MUST revert if the identifier already exists. Without that rule an
implementation whose derivation collides silently overwrites a live grant's `status`, which is a back
door around no-reinstatement. This specification does not mandate a particular preimage.

**Relayed withdrawal.** An implementation MUST accept a subject-signed revocation submitted by a
third party (`revokeBySubjectWithSig`, `revokeScopeLeafWithSig`), so that a subject holding no fee
token can withdraw. A design where granting accepts a relayed signature and withdrawal does not is
non-conforming, because it makes withdrawal strictly harder than granting.

**Attenuation.** `subGrant` MUST be called by the parent's `grantee`, and MUST revert unless
`isGrantActive(parentGrantId)` is true, the parent has `FLAG_DELEGABLE` set, and all of the following
hold: child `subjectCommit`, `assetContract` and `assetTokenId` equal the parent's; child `ops` is a
subset of parent `ops`; child `expiresAt` is not later than the parent's; child `notBefore` is not
earlier than the parent's; child `purposeCode` is the parent's or a registered narrowing of it;
child `usesMax` meets the rule below; and `depth` does not exceed the implementation's `maxDepth`.
The child names its own `grantee`, `agent` and `agentOperator`, under the same mint-time rules as
`grant` (see Agent leg at mint).

Because 0 means unlimited, `usesMax` is attenuated by a rule and not by integer comparison: when the
parent's `usesMax` is non-zero, the child's MUST be non-zero and not greater than it; when the
parent's is zero, the child's may be any value.

`usesMax` bounds the uses committed on one grant. Each grant keeps its own `useCount`, so a grantee
holding a delegable grant can divide or repeat its allowance across sub-grants. A subject who needs
an aggregate ceiling MUST NOT set `FLAG_DELEGABLE`, or MUST meter access outside this mechanism (see
"`usesMax` is not a cryptographic bound").

**Scope containment MUST be proven, not assumed.** Subtree membership is not decidable from two
32-byte roots, so an implementation cannot satisfy "child scope is within parent scope" by inspection.
Either the child names the same `scopeRef` and the same `FLAG_SCOPE_IS_ROOT` value as the parent, or
the parent is root-scoped, the child is leaf-scoped, and `scopeProof` proves the child's `scopeRef`
is a leaf under the parent's root. An
implementation MUST reject a child whose membership is neither of those. Accepting an unproven child
scope voids attenuation entirely for Merkle-scoped grants: the grantee can mint a child naming any
resource at all, and a later `checkAccess` on that child consults only the child's own `scopeRef`.

**Agent leg at mint.** `grant` and `subGrant` MUST revert unless the agent fields agree. When
`agentKind` is `NONE`, `agent` and `agentOperator` MUST be zero, and `agentStatus` is set to `NONE`.
For any other `agentKind`, `agent` and `agentOperator` MUST be non-zero, and `agentStatus` is set to
`ACTIVE`. For `EXTERNAL_REGISTRY`, `agent` is the address through which the registered identity
acts (for an [ERC-8004](./eip-8004.md) identity, its `agentWallet`), as it stood at the grant. This
standard does not resolve a registry on chain; a relying party that needs the live registry state
reads it off chain.

**Agent verification.** `checkAccess` MUST accept exactly these callers: (a) `msg.sender == agent`,
when `agentKind` is `SESSION_KEY`, `EXTERNAL_REGISTRY` or `TOKEN_BOUND_ACCOUNT` and `agentStatus` is
`ACTIVE`; or (b) `msg.sender == grantee`, when `agentKind` is `NONE` or `ASSERTED_UNVERIFIED`, or
when `agentStatus` is `REVOKED`. After `revokeAgent` the agent therefore fails and the grantee keeps
direct access, which is the incident-response outcome the agent leg exists for. An implementation
MUST NOT accept a caller-supplied agent identity as satisfying an agent constraint.
`ASSERTED_UNVERIFIED` is recorded for audit only and never makes the agent a permitted caller. An implementation SHOULD take the agent of an
`EXTERNAL_REGISTRY` grant from an ERC-8004 identity registry rather than defining a parallel agent
model.

**`checkAccess` authenticates only an on-chain caller.** The function reads `msg.sender`. An
off-chain relying party that invokes it through `eth_call` (or any `eth_call` equivalent) supplies
`from` itself and obtains **no authentication whatsoever**: a client can set `from` to the agent
address and receive `true` for a grant that agent was never authorized to exercise from that client.
For off-chain enforcement, a relying party MUST NOT treat a lone `checkAccess` `eth_call` as proof of
caller identity. It MUST combine `isGrantActive` / `effectiveStatus` (which do not depend on
`msg.sender`) with an independent authentication of the agent or grantee (for example a signature
over the access request, a signature by the `agentWallet` registered in an [ERC-8004](./eip-8004.md) identity, or a transport-level credential).
On-chain callers that gate a resource behind `checkAccess` in the same transaction remain sound,
because `msg.sender` is then the EVM caller.

**`checkAccess` evaluation.** `checkAccess` MUST return true only if all of the following hold: the
caller is permitted by Agent verification; `isGrantActive(grantId)` is true; `purposeCode` equals the
grant's `purposeCode` (exact equality over registered codes, since narrowing applies only to
`subGrant`); `op` has exactly one bit set, that bit is a recognized `ops` bit, and the grant's `ops`
has that bit set; and the scope proof below holds. It MUST return false for an unknown grant, and for
an `op` with zero bits or more than one bit set.

**Scope proof.** `checkAccess` MUST return false if `resourceHash` has been withdrawn via
`revokeScopeLeaf` on **this grant or any ancestor**, whether or not this grant is root-scoped. When
`FLAG_SCOPE_IS_ROOT` is set, it MUST also verify `proof` as a Merkle inclusion proof of
`resourceHash` under `scopeRef`. When the flag is clear, `scopeRef` MUST equal `resourceHash`. In both cases `resourceHash` is the Merkle leaf defined below,
`keccak256(bytes.concat(keccak256(resourceId)))`, and the `scopeRef` of a single-resource grant,
including a leaf-scoped child, is that same leaf.

**Merkle construction is normative.** Without a fixed leaf and pair rule, two conforming
implementations can disagree on whether a proof is valid. Implementations MUST use:

1. **Leaf.** `leaf = keccak256(bytes.concat(keccak256(resourceId)))`, where `resourceId` is the
   canonical byte encoding of the resource identifier agreed off-chain for that asset. The identifier MUST carry no meaning outside this protocol or MUST
   include a salt of at least 128 bits, because the leaf is an unsalted hash of it and Privacy rules 1
   and 2 apply to it. Double-hashing
   matches the OpenZeppelin `MerkleProof` / StandardMerkleTree leaf form and blocks second-preimage
   attacks on interior nodes presented as leaves.
2. **Interior node.** `parent = keccak256(bytes.concat(min(a, b), max(a, b)))` over the two
   32-byte children, i.e. **sorted-pair** hashing. Proof elements are sibling hashes from leaf to
   root; at each level the implementation sorts the current hash with the sibling before hashing.
3. **Empty proof.** A single-leaf tree has `scopeRef == leaf` and an empty `proof` array.

`revokeScopeLeaf` withdrawals MUST be consulted on every ancestor when evaluating a descendant;
`isLeafRevoked(grantId, leaf)` reports exactly that evaluation.
A per-datum withdrawal that stops at the grant that recorded it does not survive one delegation hop,
and would let a grantee recover a withdrawn leaf by minting a child that re-lists it.

**Purpose registry.** `purposeCode` indexes a registry that maps a code to an ontology term and pins
the ontology release, so a code's meaning cannot drift. An implementation MUST expose
`purposeRegistry()` returning its registry address, and the registry MUST implement:

```solidity
interface IPurposeRegistry /* is IERC165 */ {
    /// The ontology term and the hash of the pinned ontology release for a registered code.
    function termOf(uint32 code) external view returns (string memory iri, bytes32 releaseHash);
    function isNarrowing(uint32 childCode, uint32 parentCode) external view returns (bool);
}
```

`termOf` returns an Internationalized Resource Identifier (IRI) naming the ontology term, and the
hash of the pinned ontology release, so a reader can resolve the mapping and the release. It returns
an empty string for an unregistered code. Once a code is registered, its term and release MUST NOT
change. Who may register codes and narrowings is implementation-defined. The ERC-165 interface
identifier of `IPurposeRegistry` is `0xea84a472`.

Where `purposeRegistry()` returns the zero address, `subGrant` MUST require exact purpose equality;
that is, an unset registry MUST fail closed. On-chain purpose matching is equality over registered
codes. Ontology subsumption reasoning, where a broad permitted use implies a narrower one, is
explicitly out of scope and is performed off-chain by the relying party.

**Purpose profile.** `purposeProfileRef` is the hash of the canonical purpose profile document,
which carries the full purpose including any parameters. Parameters need a defined place because
several widely used modifiers are parameterized (a disease-specific permission needs a disease, a
geographic restriction needs a region, a time limit needs a duration) and no published vocabulary
specifies how a parameter attaches. The profile document MUST be a JavaScript Object Notation
(JSON) object, canonical per [RFC 8785](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8785) (JSON
Canonicalization Scheme), with exactly one member, `terms`, an array of `{term, value}` pairs where
`term` is an ontology IRI and `value` is absent or an IRI or literal. RFC 8785 does **not** reorder
array elements, so two profiles with the same pairs in different array orders would hash
differently. Implementations MUST sort the array by `term` ascending as UTF-8 byte order, ties
broken by the RFC 8785 serialization of the pair, before canonicalization, MUST reject profiles
whose array is not so sorted, and MUST reject two identical pairs. That is a choice made here, not
a convention inherited from elsewhere.

**Revocation authority.**

| Action | Authorized caller | Resulting status |
|---|---|---|
| `revokeBySubject` | the subject only | `REVOKED_BY_SUBJECT` |
| `renounceByGrantee` | the grantee | `RENOUNCED_BY_GRANTEE` |
| `revokeAgent` | grantee, agent, or agent operator | `agentStatus` only; grant survives |
| `revokeScopeLeaf` | the subject | grant survives, leaf withdrawn |

`revokeBySubject` MUST be unconditional, MUST take effect immediately, MUST NOT be blocked by the
grantee, the issuer, or the contract operator, and MUST cause `isGrantActive` to return false for
every descendant grant. It MUST NOT require unbounded work in the revoking transaction; lazy
evaluation is permitted and RECOMMENDED. An eager per-descendant write lets a grantee mint enough
sub-grants to push the subject's withdrawal past the block gas limit, which makes the withdrawal
permanently impossible to execute and defeats the "MUST NOT be blocked" rule in the same sentence that
states it.

`revokeAgent` MUST revert unless `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, `agentKind` is not `NONE` and
`agentStatus` is `ACTIVE`. `revokeScopeLeaf` MUST revert unless `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`
and the leaf is not already withdrawn on this grant; it MAY be called on any grant, root-scoped or
not.

**Status transitions.** Every non-`NONE` value MUST have exactly one writing path. Values with no
path are dead weight and invite non-conforming custom setters.

| From | To | Writer | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `NONE` | `ACTIVE` | `grant` / `subGrant` | only mint path |
| `ACTIVE` | `REVOKED_BY_SUBJECT` | `revokeBySubject` / `WithSig` | terminal |
| `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` | `RENOUNCED_BY_GRANTEE` | `renounceByGrantee` | terminal; the grantee can always hand a grant back |
| `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` | `TERMINATED_BY_ISSUER` | `terminateByIssuer` | terminal; caller satisfies `isIssuer` |
| `ACTIVE` | `SUSPENDED` | `suspend` | grantee or non-zero agent operator only |
| `SUSPENDED` | `ACTIVE` | `resume` | only the party recorded in `suspendedBy` |
| `SUSPENDED` | `REVOKED_BY_SUBJECT` | `revokeBySubject` / `WithSig` | subject always wins |
| `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` | `EXHAUSTED` | `commitUsage` | when cumulative uses reach `usesMax != 0` |

Every transition out of `SUSPENDED` sets `suspendedBy` to `NONE`, so the field is non-zero only
while the status is `SUSPENDED`. `suspend` MUST revert unless `status` is `ACTIVE` and the caller is
the grantee or a non-zero `agentOperator`. A suspension by the agent operator therefore blocks
neither the subject's withdrawal, nor the grantee's `renounceByGrantee`, nor `terminateByIssuer`.

`REVOKED_BY_SUBJECT`, `RENOUNCED_BY_GRANTEE`, `TERMINATED_BY_ISSUER`, and `EXHAUSTED`
are **terminal**. An implementation MUST NOT provide any function that returns a terminal grant to
`ACTIVE`. Re-granting MUST mint a new grant with a new identifier. The sole exception to
no-reinstatement is `resume` of `SUSPENDED` → `ACTIVE`, and it exists only so incident response can
lift a temporary hold without erasing a later subject withdrawal. `resume` MUST revert if the
caller is not the party recorded in `suspendedBy`, and MUST revert if status is anything other than
`SUSPENDED`. A grant is replaced by revoking it and minting a new one.

**Roles.** The callers named in this section are identified as follows. The subject is whoever opens
`subjectCommit` (Subject commitment). The grantee is `grantee`, the agent is `agent`, and the agent
operator is `agentOperator`, which is zero when there is no agent leg, so no operator can act on such
a grant. The issuer is any account for which `isIssuer` returns true. `suspend` records its caller in
`suspendedBy` (`GRANTEE` when the caller is the grantee, `AGENT_OPERATOR` otherwise), and `resume`
requires the grantee or the agent operator respectively.

**Interface detection.** A conforming implementation MUST return true from
[ERC-165](./eip-165.md) `supportsInterface` for the identifier of `IConsentGrant`, `0x85dfe863`. The
identifier includes `grant` and `subGrant`, whose selectors are fixed because `GrantInput` is
normative.

**Activity.** `isGrantActive` MUST evaluate the grant and every ancestor up to `maxDepth`, and MUST
return false if any is not `ACTIVE`, if the current time is outside `[notBefore, expiresAt)`, or if
`usesMax != 0 && useCount >= usesMax`. It does not consider `agentStatus`: the agent leg changes
who may call `checkAccess`, not whether the grant is active.

**Effective status.** `effectiveStatus(grantId)` MUST return `NONE` for an unknown grant. Otherwise it
MUST return the stored `status` of the nearest grant, walking from `grantId` up through its
ancestors, whose status is not `ACTIVE`, and `ACTIVE` when there is none. It therefore reports an
ancestor's `SUSPENDED` or terminal status, and the nearest one when several exist. It does not
reflect `[notBefore, expiresAt)` or `usesMax`: only `isGrantActive` does, so `effectiveStatus` is
for display and never replaces `isGrantActive`.

**Revocation epoch.** `revocationEpoch` MUST increase by at least one on every call that can turn a
previously returned authorization into a refusal: `revokeBySubject` and `revokeBySubjectWithSig`,
`renounceByGrantee`, `terminateByIssuer`, `revokeAgent`, `revokeScopeLeaf` and its `WithSig`
variant, `suspend`, and any transition to `EXHAUSTED`. `resume` does not change it. Relying
parties that cache authorization decisions SHOULD re-validate when it changes; this bounds revocation
latency without requiring per-access reads.

**Events.** Each state change emits exactly the event named, in the same transaction. `grant` emits
`ConsentGranted`; `subGrant` emits `ConsentGranted` followed by `ConsentSubGranted`; every
transition to a terminal status emits `ConsentRevoked` with the new `status`; `revokeAgent` emits
`AgentRevoked`; `revokeScopeLeaf` emits `ScopeLeafRevoked`; `suspend` emits `GrantSuspended` and
`resume` emits `GrantResumed`, each with the caller in `by`; and `commitUsage` emits
`UsageCommitted`. In `ConsentRevoked`, `by` is `subjectCommit` for `REVOKED_BY_SUBJECT`, the caller's
address left-padded to 32 bytes for `RENOUNCED_BY_GRANTEE` and `TERMINATED_BY_ISSUER`, and zero for
`EXHAUSTED`, which has no caller to name. `reasonCode` is the code the caller supplied, or zero where
the function takes none. No event carries the subject key or the salt.

**Derivative destruction.** `FLAG_DESTROY_DERIVATIVES` records an instruction of the subject, not an
on-chain mechanism, because a contract cannot delete anything off-chain. When the flag is set on a
grant and that grant or any ancestor reaches `REVOKED_BY_SUBJECT`, the grantee and the agent MUST
destroy every derivative they produced under the grant (an artifact created through the `DERIVE` or
`TRAIN` operations), and a relying party MAY treat continued possession as non-compliance with the
grant. Where destruction is not technically available, as for trained model weights, the grantee
SHOULD document the measurement and accounting done in its place.

**Usage accounting.** Implementations MUST NOT emit a per-access event. Usage is recorded off-chain
as an append-only hash chain whose head is committed periodically via `commitUsage`. The chain is
normative so that two parties can recompute the same head:

1. **Hash function.** `H` is `keccak256`. `h_0 = bytes32(0)`.
2. **Step.** `h_i = keccak256(abi.encodePacked(h_{i-1}, record_i))` for `i = 1..n`.
3. **Record.** Each `record_i` is 32 bytes: `record_i = keccak256(abi.encode(
   bytes32 grantId, uint64 accessedAt, uint32 op, bytes32 resourceHash, bytes32 requestId))`.
   `accessedAt` is Unix seconds; `requestId` is a caller-chosen unique id for the access so that two
   identical accesses do not collide into one record. Fields finer than needed for enforcement
   (identity of a human user, session tokens) MUST NOT appear in `record_i`.
4. **Commitment.** `commitUsage(grantId, epoch, headHash, count)` asserts that after `count`
   records in `epoch`, the chain head is `headHash`. An `epoch` is one off-chain hash chain for the
   grant: epoch 0 is the first, and the grantee or agent starts a fresh chain, with `h_0 = bytes32(0)`
   again, by using the next epoch number. `epoch` MUST equal `usageEpoch`, or `usageEpoch + 1` once at least one use has been
   committed (`usageCount > 0`), so that epoch 0 cannot be skipped empty. Within an epoch `count`
   MUST be greater than `usageCount`, in a new epoch `count` MUST be greater than zero, and
   `headHash` MUST NOT be zero. A call that adds no uses, or that repeats an `(epoch, count)` under
   another `headHash`, therefore reverts, so a committed head cannot be restated. The contract MUST
   then update
   the stored fields: `useCount` increases by `count - usageCount` for the same epoch, or by `count`
   for a new epoch, and saturates at its maximum; `usageEpoch` and `usageCount` take the new `epoch`
   and `count`; and `lastUsedAt` takes `block.timestamp`, the time of the commitment and not of any
   access. `revocationEpoch` is unrelated to `epoch`. When `usesMax != 0` and `useCount >= usesMax`,
   status MUST become `EXHAUSTED` and MUST NOT overwrite a terminal subject or issuer status. Only the
   grantee, or the agent while `agentStatus` is `ACTIVE`, may call `commitUsage`; it MUST revert for
   any other caller, and for a grant whose status is neither `ACTIVE` nor `SUSPENDED`.

**`usesMax` is not a cryptographic bound.** Only the grantee or agent can advance the off-chain chain
and call `commitUsage`, so the party constrained by `usesMax` is also the party that reports use.
The field is a **contractual** ceiling with on-chain accounting the constrained party self-declares.
A relying party that needs a bound the grantee cannot under-report MUST meter access outside this
mechanism (for example by serving bytes only through a gate that increments its own counter, or by
requiring a usage head signed by a third-party notary). Implementations MUST document this honesty
assumption wherever they surface `usesMax` to a subject.

What the chain provides is tamper-evidence and ordering over the records that were appended. It does
**not** provide completeness: a party that declines to append a record leaves no gap a third party
can detect from the head alone. See Privacy and Data Protection under Security Considerations.

### Privacy rules

These rules protect the data subject rather than the protocol. An implementation can satisfy every
other requirement, function correctly, and still place its operator in breach of data protection
law. The chain records authorization state, never facts about the person: anything that describes
the subject rather than the permission belongs off-chain.

The following MUST NOT be written to chain state or events:

1. Any identifier that carries meaning outside this protocol, in any form, **including hashed and
   including encrypted**. This covers an account address, a name, a medical record number, a date of
   birth, an email address and a biological sequence. A hash of such an identifier remains personal
   data, and encryption does not remove that status. The single permitted exception is the subject
   commitment of rule 7, which is generated for one grant and denotes nothing outside it.
2. An unsalted hash of any identifier drawn from an enumerable space. An address space is enumerable,
   so a bare hash of an address can be reversed by brute force and yields a pseudonym at best.
3. Data from which the underlying record can be reconstructed, including partial or aggregate
   derivatives where the population is small.
4. Date elements finer than a year, and geography finer than a coarse region, that describe the
   subject or the subject's data. The grant's own timestamps (`notBefore`, `expiresAt`,
   `lastUsedAt`) describe the authorization and not the person, and are permitted.
5. The plain-language instrument text. `termsRef` MUST hash a **versioned template** shared across
   many subjects, never a per-subject executed document, which would be a commitment to personal data.
6. Free-text purpose or condition strings. Use registered codes and the profile hash.
7. A stable subject identifier reused across grants. The subject MUST be referenced on-chain only as
   `subjectCommit`, a commitment to a subject key that is generated for that grant, drawn from at
   least 128 bits of entropy, and derived from no pre-existing identifier. A fresh key MUST be used
   for each grant, so that two grants concerning the same person are not linkable by an observer.
8. Session identifiers or other behavioral identifiers. A session identifier has no on-chain
   enforcement value, because a contract cannot verify that a session existed, and publishing one
   makes an individual's interactions linkable.

### Zero-subject profile

An implementation MAY support a zero-subject profile, in which a `Bind` carries no on-chain subject
authentication and revocation happens only by an off-chain process. The profile applies to `Bind`
alone: `grant` MUST revert when `subjectCommit` is zero, so every grant has a subject who can
withdraw it. Where a `Bind` carries a zero `subjectCommit`, `grantId` MUST be zero and the
implementation MUST reject every on-chain subject-signed path for it. The Content Credentials profile
below uses it for a public work.

### Grant tokens

An implementation MAY additionally represent grants as soulbound [ERC-1155](./eip-1155.md) tokens in a reserved
identifier range. Where it does, the grant token MUST be minted to the grantee only. A subject-side
mint MUST NOT be performed, because it would write the subject's account address into transfer state
and events and so republish exactly the identifier that `subjectCommit` exists to keep off-chain.

### Content Credentials profile

This subsection is OPTIONAL. It is not a separate standard. A deployment MAY bind a grant, or a
subject-less authorship claim, to a Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA) Content Credential so that a viewer can
establish who committed to a piece of content and, when the content carries someone's data, whether
that person's consent still stands. A conforming core implementation need not implement it, and an
implementation that does implement it remains a conforming ERC-8356 implementation.

**Referenced specifications.** The terms manifest, claim, hard binding, ingredient and X.509 claim
signature are those of the C2PA Technical Specification, version 2.5. An identity assertion is the
one defined by the Creator Assertions Working Group (CAWG) Identity Assertion specification, version
1.3. The verifier's trust list is the list of trust anchors the verifier applies to X.509 claim
signatures, for example the C2PA trust list, and the verifier declares which list it uses.

**Two graphs stay two graphs.** A C2PA manifest already carries an ingredient graph: the assets a
derivative was made from. This ERC carries a consent graph: `parentGrantId` attenuation and `scopeRef`
leaves. The profile MUST NOT collapse one into the other. Revoking a parent asset orphans its
derivatives through the C2PA ingredient chain, and `FLAG_DESTROY_DERIVATIVES` carries the subject's
instruction to destroy them (see Derivative destruction); revoking a grant does not rewrite any C2PA
manifest. Withdrawal of consent is not erasure of authorship, and the two graphs
answer different questions.

**Content commitment.** The content is committed as
`keccak256(abi.encodePacked(salt, sha256(content)))`, where `salt` is a fresh 32-byte value and
`sha256(content)` is the SHA-256 of the exact bytes. `abi.encodePacked` of two `bytes32` values is
their concatenation, so this is `keccak256(salt || sha256(content))`. The salt is off-chain content,
not on-chain state; it MUST carry at least 128 bits of entropy (Privacy rule 7) and MUST NOT be
published on chain. A manifest commitment over the finalized C2PA bytes is formed the same way,
`keccak256(salt || sha256(manifest))`, with an independent salt.

**Field definitions.** `biocid` is a uniform resource identifier (URI) per
[RFC 3986](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3986) that names the content and resolves only through
an access-controlled resolver under the owner's authority; resolution happens off chain and the string
carries no meaning on chain. The profile is written for the scheme `biocid`, of the form
`biocid://<authority>/<path>`, which is this profile's own name for such an identifier and is not
registered with the Internet Assigned Numbers Authority (IANA); another scheme that meets the same
resolver rule is permitted. `ingredientsRoot` is the root of a sorted-pair Merkle tree, built as in
the Merkle construction above, whose leaves are `keccak256(bytes.concat(keccak256(h)))` for each
ingredient hash `h`, the SHA-256 value the C2PA manifest records for that ingredient; it is zero when
the content has no ingredients. `snpBloom` is a 32-byte commitment binding the work to material derived from
deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA), and is zero when the work is not DNA-bound; a deployment commonly builds it as a Bloom
filter over single-nucleotide-polymorphism (SNP) positions, and this profile fixes only its width and
its inclusion in `Bind`, not its construction. The binding pointer carried in the C2PA claim is the
tuple (chain id, verifying contract address, `attestor`, `grantId`, `biocid`) in a manifest
assertion, with `grantId` zero for a public work. Those five fields are normative. The assertion
label is implementation-defined and published by the implementation, and the pointer carries no
subject information.

**Binding ceremony.** The order is normative. `Bind` is an on-chain attestation *of* the sealed C2PA
claim, not a field inside it. Putting `Bind` inside the hashed claim would make `manifestCommitment`
unknowable at signing time and would let a relayer swap the sealed bytes after the author has
signed. A C2PA X.509 author assertion or a CAWG identity assertion, if present, is a separate
signature and is not this `Bind`.

1. The attestor computes `contentCommitment` as above.
2. The C2PA claim is assembled (content hash, ingredients, subject, grant pointer, `biocid`) without
   the EIP-712 `Bind`, and the claim generator produces its X.509 signature over those finalized
   bytes.
3. The `manifestCommitment` is computed over those finalized bytes, `keccak256(salt || sha256(manifest))`,
   with an independent salt.
4. The author (for a public work) or the subject (for a work carrying their data) signs the EIP-712
   `Bind` typed data below. The signature covers the `contentCommitment`, the `manifestCommitment`,
   the `biocid`, the `revocable` flag, the C2PA `ingredientsRoot`, the `subjectCommit`, and the
   `snpBloom`, so none of those can change after signing without invalidating the signature.
   The lab's X.509 claim is separate: `Bind` is always authored by the subject or the public author.
5. For a work carrying a subject's data, `grant` is called with `scopeRef` equal to the
   `contentCommitment` (or a Merkle root containing it), and `bindContent` records the binding against
   that grant. For a public work, no grant is created (see Permanence, below).

**The `Bind` typed data.** The profile reuses this ERC's EIP-712 domain (`name: "ConsentGrant"`,
`version: "1"`, with the chain id and verifying contract). It introduces no [ERC-191](./eip-191.md) scheme. The domain
already carries the chain id and verifying contract, so the message carries only the content and policy
tuple:

```
Bind(bytes32 contentCommitment,bytes32 manifestCommitment,string biocid,bool revocable,bytes32 ingredientsRoot,bytes32 subjectCommit,bytes32 snpBloom,uint256 nonce)
```

`manifestCommitment` is inside `Bind` so a relayer cannot substitute a different sealed C2PA after the
author has signed. `biocid` is a `string`, so EIP-712 includes `keccak256(bytes(biocid))` in the
digest, and the contract needs only that hash: `bindContent` takes `biocidHash`, equal to
`keccak256(bytes(biocid))`, and rebuilds the same digest. That is what stops an attestor from
swapping the URI after the author has signed. The raw URI, which may embed an identifier that carries
meaning outside this protocol, never appears in state, events or calldata, satisfying Privacy rules 1
and 2. `snpBloom` is a commitment, never raw genotype calls. For a public work `revocable` is `false`
and `subjectCommit` is zero (the zero-subject profile). For a work carrying a subject's data
`revocable` is `true` and `subjectCommit` is the grant's `subjectCommit`.

An implementation MUST verify the `Bind` signature with [ERC-1271](./eip-1271.md) support
(`SignatureChecker` or equivalent) against an explicit `attestor` address, so a smart-account
subject can author `Bind` and a signature over different fields cannot bind as a recovered
stranger. For a grant-bound work the attestor MUST open `subjectCommit`. The `Bind` nonce is the
current value of `bindNonces(attestor)`, a counter per attestor that is independent of
`nonces(bytes32)`, because a `Bind` may be signed before `grant` consumes that counter and a public
work has no `subjectCommit` to key a counter by. `bindContent` MUST reject a zero `manifestCommitment`.

**On-chain binding.** A conforming binding implementation SHOULD expose `IContentBinding`:

```solidity
interface IContentBinding /* is IERC165 */ {
    struct Binding {
        address attestor;
        bytes32 contentCommitment;
        bytes32 manifestCommitment;
        bytes32 ingredientsRoot;
        bytes32 snpBloom;
        bytes32 biocidHash;
        bytes32 grantId;          // zero for a public work
        bytes32 subjectCommit;    // zero for a public work
        bool    revocable;
    }

    event ContentBound(
        bytes32 indexed bindingId, address indexed attestor,
        bytes32 indexed contentCommitment, bytes32 grantId);

    /// bindingId = keccak256(abi.encode(attestor, contentCommitment)).
    function bindContent(
        bytes32 contentCommitment, bytes32 manifestCommitment, bytes32 ingredientsRoot,
        bytes32 subjectCommit, bytes32 snpBloom, bool revocable, bytes32 biocidHash,
        uint256 nonce, address attestor, bytes calldata bindSig,
        bytes32 grantId, bytes32[] calldata scopeProof, bytes32 subjectSalt
    ) external returns (bytes32 bindingId);

    /// Recompute a commitment from a disclosed salt and hash.
    function verifyContentDisclosure(bytes32 salt, bytes32 contentSha256, bytes32 contentCommitment)
        external pure returns (bool);
    function verifyManifestDisclosure(bytes32 bindingId, bytes32 salt, bytes32 manifestSha256)
        external view returns (bool);

    /// The stored binding; a `bindingId` that is not bound returns an all-zero struct.
    function getBinding(bytes32 bindingId) external view returns (Binding memory);

    /// The nonce the next `Bind` signed by `attestor` must carry.
    function bindNonces(address attestor) external view returns (uint256);

    /// True for a bound public work; for a bound work carrying a subject's data, true only if
    /// `isGrantActive` is true for its grant and `isLeafRevoked(grantId, contentCommitment)` is false.
    function isBindingActive(bytes32 bindingId) external view returns (bool);
}
```

The ERC-165 interface identifier of `IContentBinding` is `0x70304dcd`, and an implementation that
exposes it MUST return true for that identifier from `supportsInterface`.

A binding is identified by `bindingId = keccak256(abi.encode(attestor, contentCommitment))`. Keying
it by `contentCommitment` alone would let anyone who sees a pending `bindContent` copy its
`contentCommitment`, sign a `Bind` of their own as another attestor and bind it first, so that the
author's binding reverts as already bound. Anyone may submit `bindContent`: the signature, not the
caller, authorizes it. `bindContent` MUST accept the `Bind` signature via ERC-1271 against
`attestor`, MUST consume `bindNonces(attestor)`, MUST reject a `bindingId` that is already bound,
MUST emit `ContentBound`, and MUST, when `grantId` is
non-zero, require the grant be active, its `subjectCommit` match, the attestor open that commitment,
and `contentCommitment` be the grant's scope leaf (equality for a single-leaf grant; the same Merkle
inclusion `checkAccess` uses for a root-scoped grant). When `grantId` is zero it MUST require
`revocable` be `false`. `verifyManifestDisclosure` recomputes the manifest commitment from the
disclosed salt and compares it with the one stored for `bindingId`.

The binding stores commitments only. It MUST NOT store the `biocid`, the salt, the raw content hash,
or any storage Uniform Resource Locator (URL). Following Privacy rules 1 and 3, the binding does not store the C2PA bytes; those
are resolved off chain through the `manifestCommitment`. The calldata of `bindContent` carries
`subjectSalt` when `grantId` is non-zero, which is public (see Opening the commitment), and it
carries `biocidHash`, never the URI.

**URI profile.** In the Content Credentials profile the `biocid` field MUST hold a `biocid` URI or another URI that
resolves only through an access-controlled resolver under the owner's authority. It MUST NOT be a raw
cloud-storage path (for example `gs://` or `s3://`), a direct object-storage URL, or any signed or
presigned URL. A
signed URL is an ungated, unrevocable, unaudited bearer capability that survives consent withdrawal and
cannot be erased, which contradicts this ERC's revocability and erasure guarantees.

**Verifier requirements.** A conforming verifier, given content, the disclosed salts, and the manifest,
from which it reads the binding pointer (chain id, verifying contract address, `attestor`,
`grantId`, `biocid`), MUST treat the failure of any of the following as no valid provenance claim.
`bindingId` below is `keccak256(abi.encode(attestor, contentCommitment))` for the pointer's
`attestor`:

1. if the pointer's `grantId` is non-zero, `isGrantActive(grantId)` is true and
   `isLeafRevoked(grantId, contentCommitment)` is false, which is `isBindingActive(bindingId)`; for
   a public work, with `grantId` zero, this check is skipped;
2. `verifyContentDisclosure` and `verifyManifestDisclosure` both return true;
3. the C2PA hard binding matches the content, and the X.509 signature validates against the verifier's
   trust list (see Referenced specifications), including certificate revocation;
4. `getBinding(bindingId)` returns a binding whose `grantId`, `subjectCommit`, `revocable`,
   `ingredientsRoot` and `snpBloom` equal the values in the manifest, and whose `biocidHash` equals
   `keccak256(bytes(biocid))` for the pointer's `biocid`; the contract verified the attestor's `Bind`
   signature when it accepted the binding.

The on-chain status and the X.509 result combine restrictive-wins. This lattice is normative in
this ERC. It is not a companion note. A verifier that implements the profile MUST apply it:

| Grant state | X.509 / C2PA result | Verifier output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `isBindingActive` is true, or no grant (public work) | valid | valid provenance |
| any | untrusted, revoked, or expired | no valid claim |
| the grant or an ancestor is `SUSPENDED` | valid | no valid claim, until `resume` |
| the grant or an ancestor is terminal (`REVOKED_BY_SUBJECT`, `RENOUNCED_BY_GRANTEE`, `TERMINATED_BY_ISSUER`, `EXHAUSTED`), the content's leaf is withdrawn, the current time is outside `[notBefore, expiresAt)`, or `useCount` has reached `usesMax` | valid | no valid claim |

`SUSPENDED` withholds the claim only while the hold lasts, and the claim is valid again after
`resume`. A grant before `notBefore` withholds it until then; after `expiresAt` it does not return.
`effectiveStatus` identifies a suspended or terminal ancestor; it never reports the time window. Every terminal status is as restrictive as `REVOKED_BY_SUBJECT`. `agentStatus` does not
enter the lattice: a revoked agent leg changes who may exercise the grant, not whether the subject's
consent to the content stands. Untrusted and revoked are both invalid for this lattice. A public work has no on-chain row; only the X.509 and disclosure checks apply, and its claim
is permanent.

**Permanence.** A grant that cannot be revoked would contradict this ERC, whose premise is that
the subject can always withdraw. So a permanent work is expressed by the absence of a grant, not by a
grant that cannot be revoked. A public paper, blog post, or EIP carries a `Bind` signed with
`revocable = false` and a zero subject and mints no grant; a work carrying a subject's data always
carries a revocable grant. The Zero-subject profile defined above is the vehicle for the first case.

## Rationale

**Why the token is a status anchor and not the instrument.** A signature proves control of a key, not
that a particular human assented. Whether an authorization is valid also turns on facts a contract
cannot observe, including whether the signer had authority to act for the subject and whether any
material representation in the instrument was false. Adjacent work reached the same conclusion:
ERC-8328 states that its stored records are "attributable assertions, not proof that the reported
action occurred or was lawful," and ERC-8226 states that "The content of `metadata`, such as off-chain legal
text, cannot be verified on-chain." This standard therefore claims weight as evidence
and ordering, not legal operative effect, with one exception: revocation status, which relying parties
read directly and which is operative for them.

**Why not a profile of ERC-8226.** ERC-8226 is a two-party delegation keyed `(agent, principal, target)`, in
which the principal owns the asset and the caps are denominated in transfer quantity. This standard
is irreducibly three-party, and its subject holds no asset and signs no transaction at exercise time.
ERC-8226 checks at each use that a mandate has not been revoked, but it reads the principal's
eligibility at the compliance provider only when a mandate is granted or extended, and does not
re-read it while the mandate is live; a freeze applied by an enforcer halts evaluation without
revoking. For an irreversible disclosure, and under regimes whose triggers are knowledge and ease of
withdrawal, an eligibility checked at grant time only is not sufficient. Finally, ERC-8226's `metadata`
field is a 32-byte pointer that it forbids using for enforcement decisions, so the one place a
purpose or an instrument could live carries no force of its own. The two compose: an agent may hold
an ERC-8226 mandate over a payment asset and a grant under this standard over a data asset.

**Why not simply a Verifiable Credential with a status list.** For the instrument itself, a W3C
Verifiable Credential is the better carrier, and this standard says so. `termsRef` hashes the
**versioned template** referenced from the credential's `termsOfUse`, never the per-subject
credential instance: hashing the instance would be a commitment to personal data, which Privacy
rule 5 forbids. What a VC status list cannot provide is a revocation that is public,
consensus-ordered, and not retractable by the issuer. A Bitstring Status List is fetched from an
issuer-controlled endpoint that can be rewritten or taken offline. The on-chain revocation is the
part that must not be under the issuer's unilateral control, and it is the only part this standard
puts on-chain.

**Why purpose is a registry code and not a free hash.** Purpose vocabularies used in practice are
classification systems with parent and child terms, so equality over a hash produces false denials: a grant for
a broad permitted use will not match a request labelled with a narrower one that the ontology says it
permits. This standard therefore specifies coarse equality over registered codes on-chain, with
hierarchy reasoning performed off-chain by the relying party, and requires the registry entry to pin
the ontology release so a code's meaning cannot drift. `purposeProfileRef` carries the hash of the
full canonical profile, including any parameters, because several widely used modifiers are
parameterized (a disease-specific permission needs a disease, a geographic restriction needs a
region, a time limit needs a duration) and no published vocabulary specifies how the parameter
attaches. This standard specifies it in the Purpose profile paragraph of the Specification, where
the sorted-array rule is a choice made by this standard, not a convention inherited from elsewhere.

**Why grants are soulbound.** A transferable consent grant is a right of access that can be sold to a
person's data. Consent runs to a named recipient; non-assignment is the property, not a
limitation. Delegation is expressed by attenuated `subGrant`, which cannot exceed its parent and
cascades on revocation.

**Why no per-access event.** See Privacy and Data Protection under Security Considerations.

### Prior Art

- **[ERC-5006](./eip-5006.md)** gives an [ERC-1155](./eip-1155.md) token a time-bounded `user` role.
  Two-party: the owner grants over their own token. This specification adds the third principal and
  the purpose.
- **[ERC-4907](./eip-4907.md)** is the [ERC-721](./eip-721.md) sibling of the above.
- **[ERC-8226](./eip-8226.md)** delegates scoped, time-bounded, financially capped authority to an
  agent, keyed `(agent, principal, target)`. Its caps are denominated in transfer quantity and its
  `metadata` field is expressly non-normative, so it has no place to put an instrument or a purpose.
- **[ERC-8328](./eip-8328.md)** logs subject-linked compliance events and models a subject, a
  beneficiary and an actor. It is a reporting interface: its records are "attributable assertions,
  not proof that the reported action occurred or was lawful", and nothing consults its outcomes.
- **Entriken and Uribe (2020), "Biosample permission token with non-fungible tokens"** set out the
  `permitter`, `permitee` and permit model this specification builds on, using ERC-721 to publish
  permit status and to resolve a permit recursively back to the property owner. It establishes the
  three-role framing and the public-status requirement. What it does not do is bind a permit to a
  declared purpose, give the agent an independently revocable leg, or make the `permitter`'s
  withdrawal operative for a relying party at access time.
- **[ERC-8004](./eip-8004.md)** supplies agent identity. Its `agentId` is an ERC-721 token id and
  `agentWallet` is a reserved, signature-verified field. The Specification recommends resolving an
  `EXTERNAL_REGISTRY` agent through it rather than defining a parallel agent model.
- **[ERC-6617](./eip-6617.md)** defines bit-based permissions. The `ops` values here are fixed
  rather than delegated to it because a grant minted by one party is evaluated by another.
- **World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) Verifiable Credentials 2.0** is the better carrier for the
  instrument itself, and this specification treats it as such: `termsRef` and `purposeProfileRef` are
  hashes of the **versioned templates** referenced from a credential's normative `termsOfUse`, not
  hashes of the per-subject credential and not replacements for it. Only revocation status is placed
  on-chain, because only revocation must be outside the issuer's unilateral control. A status list is
  fetched from an issuer-controlled endpoint that can be rewritten or taken offline; a
  consensus-ordered revocation cannot.
- **[ERC-7053](./eip-7053.md)** indexes content provenance, and the **Ethereum Attestation Service
  (EAS)** attests arbitrary claims, but neither carries a revocable consent that a verifier is
  required to honor restrictive-wins, and neither reuses a purpose-bound consent domain as the
  authorship domain.
- **C2PA and CAWG.** The C2PA and CAWG identity-assertion work carries an author identity into a
  manifest but has no on-chain revocation a verifier must observe. The novelty the Content
  Credentials profile adds over that body of work is narrow and specific: a mutual, salted, joint
  content-and-consent binding whose runtime validity is the restrictive-wins combination of an X.509
  credential and a revocable on-chain status.

## Backwards Compatibility

This standard introduces no changes to existing token behaviour. It is asset-standard agnostic:
`assetContract` and `assetTokenId` may name an ERC-721 or ERC-1155 token, and an implementation can additionally
represent grants as soulbound [ERC-1155](./eip-1155.md) tokens in a reserved identifier range; the Grant tokens
paragraph of the Specification states the minting rule for that case. Where a time-boxed user
role over an ERC-1155 asset is all that is required, ERC-5006 is sufficient and this standard is
unnecessary. Where an agent identity and payment account are required, taking `agent` from an
[ERC-8004](./eip-8004.md) identity registry avoids defining a parallel agent model.

## Security Considerations

**Enforcement is advisory unless the caller is verified.** `checkAccess` is only as strong as the
implementation's verification of `msg.sender`. An implementation that accepts an agent address as a
parameter provides no agent authorization at all. The same failure mode appears off-chain: an
`eth_call` with a client-chosen `from` does not authenticate the agent. This is stated as a
normative requirement above because it is the most likely implementation error, and it is the
deployment shape the Motivation describes (a data holder checking a grant before release).

**A claimed agent identity is not an authenticated one.** `ASSERTED_UNVERIFIED` exists so that an
unproven claim can be recorded honestly rather than disguised as a principal. It does not
satisfy an agent constraint (see Agent verification in the Specification).

**Compromised session keys.** Where `agentKind == SESSION_KEY`, the key authenticates a session, not
the software behind it. `revokeAgent` is the containment primitive, and it is deliberately callable
by the agent and its operator as well as the grantee so that containment does not require the
grantee to be reachable.

**Unbounded delegation.** `maxDepth` and the attenuation invariants exist because a delegation chain
without them is privilege escalation. A small `maxDepth` keeps
`isGrantActive` cheap.

**No reinstatement.** A function that flips a revoked grant back to active destroys the property that
a revocation is final, and would let an operator undo a withdrawal. Re-granting mints a new
identifier so that the history distinguishes the two.

### Privacy and Data Protection

The binding privacy requirements are the Privacy rules in the Specification. This subsection
explains why they exist and what they leave open.

A hash of a personal identifier remains personal data, and encryption does not remove that status,
so the only on-chain reference to the subject is a commitment generated for one grant. The subject
commitment is a pseudonym and nothing stronger: it remains personal data while it can be linked to a
person, and the erasure argument rests on destroying the key, not on the commitment being free of
linkage.

**Per-access events are left out for this reason.** An event stream keyed to a subject publishes an
access-pattern timeline. Access frequency and timing are themselves revealing, and on a permissioned
chain with a known participant set they are attributable. The hash-chain-plus-commitment design
preserves tamper-evidence and ordering over the records that were appended, while publishing only a
periodic head and a count. It does not prove that every access was recorded.

**Deployment.** Deploying on a permissioned network with an identifiable operator is advisable,
because it permits a clear allocation of controller responsibility. On a permissionless network
there is a live argument that validators become joint controllers of the personal data in the state
and logs they process, which is a materially worse position for the subject and for the operator.

**The governing constraint.** If a deployment cannot render every on-chain artifact anonymous by
deleting off-chain material, then consent may not be an available lawful basis for it in some
jurisdictions. Implementers are advised to verify that this holds before relying on consent, rather
than after.

`subjectCommit` is retained in state so subject-authored acts can be authorized without publishing an
account address. The zero-subject profile in the Specification covers deployments with no on-chain
subject authentication.
