## Abstract

This ERC defines the AI Dispute Resolution Framework (ADRF) for disputes about AI systems, resources and services and about the conduct, deliveries and declarations around them, between participants that may be people, organizations, agents or contracts. A dispute is a **specific claim about a specific object** — that an identified record, delivery, settlement, declaration or action does or does not conform to an obligation that was declared before the event — admitted under a pre-committed **binding**, resolved by typed **methods** and closed by a **ruling** whose effects are limited to what the binding authorized. It specifies:

1. a **Dispute Policy**: an immutable, content-addressed procedure template that binds each (obligation kind, question type) pair to a method contract, declares the effect vocabulary, the procedure track (windows, reviews, hard cap, enactment margin, optional optimistic default) and the widest standing; policies are versioned through families;
2. a **Dispute Binding**: the attributable statement by which an obligation source (a business contract or its adapter) places its objects under one policy version, commits the effects a ruling may authorize, names its executors and, optionally, third parties with standing — the only source of a ruling's authority;
3. a **Case**: one contested point, with the eight elements a dispute must state, a table of typed sub-questions with declared dependencies, the findings each method produced with their support kinds, the round decisions and the four-dimensional state (procedure state, substantive result, reason, enactment status per effect);
4. a **Ruling**: the terminal record, with the result (`Sustained`, `NotSustained` or `NoDecision`), its provenance, the one substantive effect it authorizes or the pre-committed disposition it refers to, and how the procedure closed;
5. two co-deployable registries — `IADRFPolicyRegistry` and `IADRFDisputeRegistry` — that hold the normative record and never move funds or execute an effect; executors named by the binding enact within their own authority and report.

Formal questions are decided by deterministic verifiers over pinned inputs; questions of external fact by evidence rules; questions of judgment by a judgment method, whose reference profile files one binary question per sub-question with an [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) registry and imports only its Final result with full provenance. Reviews, an optimistic track, post-dispute acceptance and third-party standing are normative-optional profiles over the same objects and the same state machine.

## Motivation

AI is bought, run, delivered, evaluated and governed through commitments: an enterprise buys inference from a model API under service terms, a user orders a report that a provider promises to review, a data provider promises a scope of use, a task escrow pays on acceptance, a swarm acts under a charter, an identity carries attested claims, a safety intervener suspends an adopter. Each of those arrangements produces records and each has a default for what happens when nobody objects. What is missing is the closing step: when someone does object, how the question is confirmed, who may raise it, against which rule it is measured, by which kind of evidence it may be decided, what the answer may change, and when the matter is over.

Two dimensions of such a dispute are kept apart. *What it is about*: which model, service, output, dataset, evaluation, action or promise is in question — the object and the obligation. *How it is resolved*: by a deterministic verifier, by external evidence, by human judgment, by AI-assisted judgment or by a composition of them — the method. The first dimension decides whether a claim can be filed; the second is a property of the policy the parties accepted. Nothing requires that AI be judged by AI, and nothing requires that a participant be an agent: a company and a service under ordinary terms can dispute a double charge without any agent identity, task token or swarm. The kernel leaves room for reuse on disputes that have no AI in them; this document's scope is bounded disputes with an identifiable relation to an AI system, resource, service or activity.

Existing building blocks each cover one corner. Arbitration interfaces route a dispute to one arbitrator and leave the object, the rule and the authority of the ruling to the parties' contract. Oracle councils such as [ERC-8033](./eip-8033.md) answer information queries and may re-judge an answer on a bond, for one kind of query. Collective-decision registries such as [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) form a decision with procedural finality under explicit authorization but deliberately say nothing about admission, evidence, burden, remedies or how a claim relates to a record. A dispute framework has to add exactly those things without re-doing the others.

Four facts drive the design.

**A binding ruling needs a specific object, a specific obligation and pre-authorized effects.** "Agent A is unreliable" is not a dispute; "delivery S of task T does not meet the acceptance criteria pinned at tender time, and the escrow committed in advance to refund on that finding" is. Outside that triangle a procedure may still run and produce a labelled, non-binding record; it may not move anything.

**Support kinds do not form a hierarchy.** A signature proves that a key signed a commitment; a replayed computation proves that a relation holds between pinned inputs; attestations establish a fact on stated trust assumptions; a collective decision establishes that a procedure formed a judgment. They answer different questions. A ruling therefore records, per finding, which method produced it, on which inputs, under which premises and with which support kinds, and it never claims more than its findings support. A derived conclusion inherits a list of sources, not their proof.

**Not deciding is a legitimate outcome with a meaning of its own.** Evidence may be unavailable, a panel may not reach quorum, a window may lapse. The framework keeps `NoDecision` distinct from `NotSustained`, lets the policy say per claim kind whether an undetermined decisive finding falls on the claimant (burden) or ends in no decision, and routes `NoDecision` to the disposition the business committed in advance, never to a default the consumer picks at that moment.

**Every window has a consequence and every procedure ends.** Admission checks that the business clock can hold the whole procedure plus an enactment margin; silence under an optimistic track adopts a pre-accepted result and proves nothing; reviews are bounded; a hard deadline closes every case; a decision adopted after a review lapsed is recorded as adopted, not as freshly confirmed.

Illustrative flows (informative):

- A service escrow charges a call twice. The client files the claim "this call was charged twice"; the policy binds that obligation kind to a verifier that reads the escrow's own settlement count; the finding is `TRUE` with support kind `computed`; the claim is sustained; the escrow, as the bound executor, refunds one charge and reports `Enacted`; a second attempt is refused by both the escrow and the registry.
- A client disputes the quality of a delivery. The policy binds result obligations to a judgment method that files "does the deliverable conform?" with an [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) roster policy; the roster blocks the proposition; the Final result is imported as `FALSE` with its issue, policy, round and adoption flags; the claim, which named `FALSE` as its supporting value, is sustained.
- Nobody votes. The [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) issue ends in `NoDecision`; the imported finding is undetermined; the policy's burden rule for judged result questions is `NO_DECISION`; the ruling authorizes no refund and points at the escrow's pre-committed disposition, the status quo.

## Specification

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119) and [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174).

### 1. Terminology

- **Object** — an identified record or state: `ObjectRef(chainId, target, id, versionHash)`, where `target` is the registry, namespace or contract that holds the object and `versionHash` pins the content or version evaluated.
- **Event** — the contested occurrence on an object (a settlement, a delivery, a declaration, a member action, a state snapshot), identified by an opaque `eventRef` the obligation source recognizes.
- **Obligation** — what a party committed to or is bound by for that object or event, of an **obligation kind** (an open vocabulary; the reference uses 1 result, 2 process, 3 limit, 4 statistical quality). The **obligation reference** is the business obligation the event belongs to, attested by the source; it scopes exclusivity.
- **Norm** — the rule the claim measures the event against: a reference (for example the hash of the agreed terms) and a clause. A norm MUST have been declared no later than the event.
- **Contested point** — one decidable proposition about the event under the norm; one case decides one contested point.
- **Claim** — the contested point together with its requested effects. A claim is **Sustained** when it is supported; the direction is fixed by the claim itself (`supportsWhen`), never read from the sub-question's value.
- **Participant roles** — a case keeps apart the **object** under dispute (a model version, a service endpoint, one output, a dataset, an evaluation statement), the **actor** that performed the conduct (an agent, a model service process, a human operator, a tool, a contract; referenced opaquely by `actorRef` when it is not the respondent), the **obligor** (the party alleged to bear the obligation; the claim's `respondent`), the **judgment participants** (verifiers, evidence providers, human experts, agents, contract accounts — admitted by the method and the policy, not by the case) and the **executors** (the source and the accounts its binding names). One participant may hold several roles; the records keep them apart. A party reference is an account; identities, qualifications and organizational relations (trustless-agent identities, know-your-agent assertions, agent identity facets, swarm charters) are optional sources a policy or binding may consult, and no case requires an agent identity. Naming an organization, a service or a model as actor or obligor binds nobody: acceptance of the procedure comes only from a binding.
- **Sub-question** — a typed proposition (`FORMAL`, `EVIDENCED` or `JUDGED`) the case must settle, with declared premises (other sub-questions and the values they must have). The **decisive** sub-question is the one whose finding decides the claim.
- **Method** — the contract a policy binds for a (obligation kind, question type) pair: a verifier (`IADRFVerifier`), an evidence rule (`IADRFEvidenceRule`) or a judgment method (`IADRFJudgmentMethod`).
- **Finding** — the resolved value of a sub-question (`TRUE`, `FALSE` or `UNDETERMINED`), with the method that produced it, the result hash, the support kinds, a reason and, for judged findings, the provenance of the judgment.
- **Support kinds** — a bit set over `signed` (1), `computed` (2), `evidenced` (4), `judged` (8). No ordering is defined between them.
- **Obligation source (source)** — the contract that attests events, obligations, norms and parties for its objects and is the implicit executor of its bindings (`IADRFObligationSource`).
- **Dispute Binding (binding)** — the source's attributable adoption of one policy version for its objects: the acceptance mode, the authorized effects, the executors, the third parties with standing, the clock downgrade rule.
- **Effect** — an opaque identifier declared by the policy in one of two categories: `SUBSTANTIVE` (a remedy on the object or obligation) or `PROCEDURAL_ECONOMIC` (a consequence of the procedure itself: bond flows, fee allocation, rewards).
- **Effect class** — `Binding` (a ruling may authorize effects) or `Advisory` (a formal record with no authority over anything).
- **Procedure state** — `Filed`, `Admitted`, `Provisional`, `Final`, `NotAdmitted`, `Withdrawn`, `Expired`.
- **Substantive result** — `None`, `Sustained`, `NotSustained`, `NoDecision`.
- **Reason** — a code explaining a state or a result; never a verdict on the merits.
- **Enactment status** — per effect: `NotRequested`, `Pending`, `Enacted`, `Failed`, `Unknown`.
- **Track** — the procedure parameters of a policy: windows, review rules, hard cap, enactment margin, optimistic default.
- **Round** — one run of resolution and decision; reviews open further rounds under the same policy version.
- **Lead** — a report that someone stated something about an object; it opens no case and confers nothing.

### 2. Conformance

A conforming deployment consists of one `IADRFPolicyRegistry` and one `IADRFDisputeRegistry` bound to it (Section 12). Both MUST implement [ERC-165](./eip-165.md) and report the interface identifiers of Section 16.

The following are REQUIRED of every conforming deployment: the policy data model and validation (Section 3), bindings with `PRE_COMMIT` acceptance (Section 5), claims, sub-questions and admission (Section 6), the state machine (Section 7), `FORMAL` resolution through verifiers and the finding record (Section 8), the decision rule, finality and the adoption rule (Section 9), rulings, effects and the enactment log (Section 10), the clock (Section 13). This is the **Minimal profile**: a bound business contract, a deterministic verifier, one round, one ruling.

The following are **normative-optional profiles**: `EVIDENCED` resolution (Section 8.3), the J-profile (`JUDGED` resolution through an [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) registry, Section 11), reviews (`maxReviews > 0`, Section 9.3), the optimistic track (Section 8.5), `POST_DISPUTE` acceptance (Section 5.3), third-party standing (beneficiaries and open challenge, Section 5.4) and Advisory filing without a binding (Section 6.5). A deployment MAY reject policies or bindings that use a profile it does not implement; a deployment that accepts them MUST implement the profile exactly as specified here.

Section 14 names extension points this ERC reserves without specifying. A deployment MUST NOT claim conformance to a reserved extension on the basis of this document.

### 3. Dispute Policy

#### 3.1 Data model

```solidity
library ADRFTypes {
    enum QuestionType   { FORMAL, EVIDENCED, JUDGED }
    enum Value          { UNDETERMINED, TRUE, FALSE }
    enum AcceptanceMode { PRE_COMMIT, POST_DISPUTE }
    enum EffectClass    { Advisory, Binding }
    enum EffectCategory { SUBSTANTIVE, PROCEDURAL_ECONOMIC }
    enum OnUndetermined { NOT_SUSTAINED, NO_DECISION }
    enum StandingMode   { PARTIES, BENEFICIARIES, OPEN }
    enum SilenceRule    { NONE, SUSTAIN, NOT_SUSTAIN }
    enum ReviewMode     { PRESERVE_UNLESS_OVERTURNED, REQUIRE_FRESH_DECISION }

    struct MethodBinding {
        uint8          obligationKind;  // 0 = any kind
        QuestionType   qType;
        address        method;          // FORMAL: IADRFVerifier; EVIDENCED: IADRFEvidenceRule; JUDGED: IADRFJudgmentMethod
        bytes32        params;          // opaque parameters passed to the method
        OnUndetermined onUndetermined;  // burden rule for an UNDETERMINED decisive finding
    }

    struct Effect { bytes32 id; EffectCategory category; }

    struct Track {
        uint64      filingWindow;      // from the event instant
        uint64      acceptanceWindow;  // POST_DISPUTE: from filing; 0 disables the mode
        uint64      evidenceWindow;    // from admission and from each review
        uint64      decisionWindow;    // from admission and from each review
        uint64      reviewWindow;      // from a round decision
        uint32      maxReviews;        // review rounds allowed after the first
        uint8       reviewTypes;       // bit set: fact 1, evidence 2, rule application 4, conflict 8, procedure 16
        ReviewMode  reviewMode;
        uint64      maxTotalDuration;  // hard cap from admission (T_max)
        uint64      enactmentMargin;   // M: time the business window must keep after T_max
        bool        optimistic;
        uint64      answerWindow;      // optimistic: from admission
        SilenceRule silenceRule;       // optimistic: meaning of the respondent's silence
    }

    struct PolicySpec {
        bytes32         family;
        uint32          version;
        bytes32         previous;            // previous policyId in the family, or 0 for the first
        address         updateAuthority;     // who may publish the family's next version
        bytes32         objectKind;          // informative label (0 = any)
        MethodBinding[] methods;
        Effect[]        effects;
        Track           track;
        StandingMode    standing;            // widest standing a binding may grant
        bytes32         resultProfile;       // keccak256("adrf:result/v1") for this document
        bytes32         economicDeclaration; // hash of the economic declaration document (informative)
        bytes32         descriptorHash;      // hash of the full policy document (informative)
    }
}
```

`policyId` MUST equal `keccak256(abi.encode(spec))` over the full `PolicySpec`. Every field is part of the identifier; `economicDeclaration` and `descriptorHash` bind published documents to the parameters but MUST NOT influence execution.

#### 3.2 Validation

`registerPolicy` MUST reject a specification unless all of the following hold:

- `resultProfile == keccak256("adrf:result/v1")`. A consumer that does not know a policy's result profile MUST reject its rulings rather than read them as booleans.
- `1 <= methods.length <= 32`; every `method` is non-zero and reports, through [ERC-165](./eip-165.md), the interface of its question type; no two bindings share the same `(obligationKind, qType)`; for a `JUDGED` binding, `method.maxDuration(params)` is non-zero and not greater than `track.decisionWindow`.
- `effects.length <= 32`, identifiers non-zero and pairwise distinct.
- `track.filingWindow > 0`, `track.decisionWindow > 0`, `track.evidenceWindow <= track.decisionWindow`; if `maxReviews > 0` then `reviewWindow > 0` and `1 <= reviewTypes <= 31`; if `optimistic` then `0 < answerWindow <= decisionWindow` and `silenceRule != NONE`, otherwise `answerWindow == 0` and `silenceRule == NONE`.
- `maxTotalDuration >= decisionWindow + maxReviews * (reviewWindow + decisionWindow)` and `maxTotalDuration <= 2^63 - 1`.
- `family != 0`, `version >= 1`, `updateAuthority != 0`; a `policyId` is never registered twice.

#### 3.3 Families and versions

The first policy registered under a `family` MUST have `previous == 0`; it claims the family and sets its update authority. Any later version MUST be registered by the family's current update authority, MUST carry `previous` equal to the family's latest `policyId` and a strictly greater `version`, and its `updateAuthority` becomes the family's new authority. Registering a new version MUST NOT alter any existing policy, binding or case.

    function trackOf(bytes32 policyId) external view returns (ADRFTypes.Track memory);

`registerPolicy` is permissionless for new families. `findMethod` MUST return the first binding, in registration order, whose `qType` matches and whose `obligationKind` is 0 or equals the argument. The registry MUST store the full specification so that every parameter it executes is readable.

### 5. Dispute Binding

#### 5.1 Data model

```solidity
struct BindingInput {
    bytes32        policyId;
    bytes32        scopeKey;           // 0 = any object of the source; else keccak256(abi.encode(ObjectRef))
    AcceptanceMode mode;
    bytes32[]      authorizedEffects;  // subset of the policy's effect identifiers
    address[]      executors;          // accounts that may report enactment besides the source
    address[]      beneficiaries;      // third parties with filing standing
    bool           openChallenge;      // anyone may file
    bool           downgradeOnClock;   // admit as Advisory when the business window is too short
    uint64         validUntil;         // 0 = none; stops new filings only
    bytes32        termsHash;          // hash of the binding document
}

struct EventInfo {       // what a source attests about an event on one of its objects
    bool    known;
    uint64  eventAt;
    uint64  adoptionDeadline;  // last instant the source can still adopt a ruling; 0 = none
    bytes32 obligationRef;     // the business obligation the event belongs to
    bytes32 normRef;           // the norm the source recognizes for it
    uint64  normDeclaredAt;
    uint8   obligationKind;    // 0 = not attested
}

interface IADRFObligationSource /* is IERC165 */ {
    function supportsInterface(bytes4 interfaceId) external view returns (bool);
    function eventInfo(ADRFTypes.ObjectRef calldata object, bytes32 eventRef) external view returns (ADRFTypes.EventInfo memory);
    function isParty(ADRFTypes.ObjectRef calldata object, bytes32 eventRef, address account) external view returns (bool);
}
```

#### 5.2 Registration

`bind` MUST record `msg.sender` as the source and MUST reject the input unless: the policy exists; the caller reports `IADRFObligationSource` through [ERC-165](./eip-165.md); every authorized effect is declared by the policy; executors and beneficiaries are non-zero; `openChallenge` is set only when the policy's standing is `OPEN`; beneficiaries are given only when the policy's standing is not `PARTIES`; `POST_DISPUTE` is chosen only when the policy's `acceptanceWindow` is non-zero; `validUntil` is zero or in the future. The source derives the authorized effects from its own authority: a binding can commit only what the source can enact. `revokeBinding` by the source stops new filings only; admitted cases complete under the binding they were admitted with.

#### 5.3 Acceptance modes

`PRE_COMMIT`: the source accepted the procedure for its objects before any event; a case that passes admission is admitted atomically with filing. `POST_DISPUTE`: the respondent named by the claim MUST `accept` the case before `filedAt + acceptanceWindow`; until then the case is `Filed`, no evidence is attached and nothing is resolved; after the window anyone MAY `expireAcceptance`, which sets `NotAdmitted` with reason `ACCEPTANCE_LAPSED`. A case admitted as Binding is never later downgraded; a case that was not admitted releases its exclusivity slot.

#### 5.4 Standing

Standing is layered. **L0**, reporting a lead, is open to anyone (Section 10.5). **L1**, starting a Binding procedure, is granted to the parties the source attests for the event and, when the binding says so, to its beneficiaries or, under `openChallenge`, to anyone. **L2**, requesting a remedy, is bounded by the effects the binding authorized. **L3**, enacting, belongs to the source and the executors the binding named. A binding can narrow the policy's standing, never widen it. Paying a bond or being able to see an object confers no standing by itself; standing comes from the binding's terms.

### 6. Cases: claim, sub-questions and admission

#### 6.1 Claim

```solidity
struct SubQuestionInput {
    bytes32      proposition;
    QuestionType qType;
    uint8        obligationKind;
    bytes32      inputsHash;      // FORMAL: keccak256 of the inputs the verifier will receive
    uint32[]     dependsOn;       // premises
    Value[]      requiredValues;  // required value of each premise
}

struct ClaimInput {
    ObjectRef          object;
    bytes32            eventRef;
    bytes32            proposition;           // the contested point
    bytes32            normRef;
    bytes32            clause;
    uint8              obligationKind;
    address            respondent;            // the obligor
    bytes32            actorRef;              // the actor, when not the respondent; 0 otherwise
    bytes32[]          requestedEffects;      // substantive effects requested
    bytes32            effectOnSustained;     // one of requestedEffects, or 0
    bytes32            effectOnNotSustained;  // one of requestedEffects, or 0
    bytes32            fallbackRef;           // the pre-committed NoDecision disposition
    bytes32            evidenceScopeHash;
    bytes32            documentHash;          // hash of the eight-element case document
    uint32             decisive;
    Value              supportsWhen;          // the claim is Sustained iff the decisive finding has this value
    SubQuestionInput[] subQuestions;
}
```

The case document (referenced by `documentHash`) answers eight questions: which object (chain, namespace, identifier, version); which event or state; which proposition; which rules (norm, clause, policy version); which subjects and identities (who files, responds, performed, delegated, is bound); on which authorization (who accepted the procedure and to what extent); which evidence (what, how provided, who can verify, how long kept); which effects are requested and what they may touch at most.

#### 6.2 Shape

`file` and `fileAdvisory` MUST reject a claim unless: `object.chainId` and `object.target` are non-zero; `eventRef`, `proposition`, `normRef`, `documentHash` and `respondent` are non-zero; the respondent is not the filer; (`actorRef` is unconstrained: it is a reference, not a party); `1 <= subQuestions.length <= 16`; `decisive` is a valid index; `supportsWhen` is `TRUE` or `FALSE`; every sub-question has a non-zero proposition, as many required values as premises, no premise equal to itself or out of range, no duplicate premise, no `UNDETERMINED` required value; the premises form an acyclic graph; `effectOnSustained` and `effectOnNotSustained` are zero or among the requested effects.

#### 6.3 Admission of a Binding case

`file(bindingId, claim)` MUST read `info = source.eventInfo(claim.object, claim.eventRef)` and admit the case only if all of the following hold — the five criteria, then standing, effects and clock:

1. **Identifiable**: the binding is active and in scope; `info.known`, `0 < info.eventAt <= now`.
2. **Accountable**: `info.obligationRef != 0`; the respondent is the source itself or `source.isParty(object, eventRef, respondent)`. Admission establishes that the party alleged to bear the obligation can be identified; it does not establish liability.
3. **Norm pre-declared**: `info.normRef != 0`, `info.normDeclaredAt != 0`, `info.normRef == claim.normRef`, `info.normDeclaredAt <= info.eventAt`; if `info.obligationKind != 0` it equals `claim.obligationKind`.
4. **Decidable**: for every sub-question the policy binds a method for its `(obligationKind, qType)` (Section 4, `findMethod`). The sub-question is pinned to that method, its parameters and its burden rule at admission. This establishes that a method and a procedure exist; it promises no conclusion.
5. **In window**: `now <= info.eventAt + track.filingWindow`.
6. **Standing** (Section 5.4) and **effects**: every requested effect is authorized by the binding and declared `SUBSTANTIVE` by the policy. A claim whose requested effects exceed the binding is refused; the filer may file it as Advisory instead.
7. **Clock**: with `required = now + track.maxTotalDuration + track.enactmentMargin`, if `info.adoptionDeadline != 0` and `info.adoptionDeadline < required`, the case is refused with `ClockIncompatible` unless the binding set `downgradeOnClock`, in which case it is admitted as Advisory and `CaseDowngraded(CLOCK_INCOMPATIBLE)` is emitted. The whole procedure, reviews included, plus the margin must fit the business window; the check is repeated at admission, not only at binding time.
8. **Exclusivity**: the exclusivity key is `keccak256(abi.encode(source, info.obligationRef, claim.proposition))`. A Binding case MUST be refused with `Duplicate` if a case holding that key is `Final` or `Expired`, or if another case holds it and is not `NotAdmitted` or `Withdrawn`. The admitted case becomes the key's active case. Exclusivity is guaranteed within one registry instance and one binding scope; consumers that bind one obligation to several registries forfeit it (Section 15).

Admission records `admittedAt = now`, `evidenceCloseAt = admittedAt + evidenceWindow`, `decisionDeadline = admittedAt + decisionWindow`, `hardDeadline = admittedAt + maxTotalDuration`, `roundCount = 1` and, under the optimistic track, `answerDeadline = admittedAt + answerWindow`.

#### 6.4 Freezing

The policy version, binding, source, object, event, proposition, norm, obligation kind, respondent, actor reference, effect class, requested and conditional effects, fallback reference, decisive sub-question, direction, the sub-questions with their methods and premises, and every deadline except those a review recomputes (Section 9.3) MUST be fixed at admission and MUST NOT change afterwards. `withdraw` MUST succeed only while the case is `Filed` and only for the claimant.

#### 6.5 Advisory cases

`fileAdvisory(policyId, claim, eventAt, normDeclaredAt)` admits a case with no binding and no source: the filer attests the event instant and the norm's declaration time, which MUST satisfy `0 < eventAt <= now`, `0 < normDeclaredAt <= eventAt` and the filing window; shape and decidability are checked as above; standing, effects, clock and exclusivity are not. The case runs the full procedure and produces a formal record; its ruling authorizes no effect and `reportEnactment` MUST reject it. An Advisory case takes no exclusivity slot and MUST NOT be converted to Binding; a party that wishes to adopt its result needs a binding and a new case.

### 7. Procedure state machine

A case has four dimensions, read separately: the procedure state, the substantive result, the reason, and the enactment status per effect.

| Transition | Caller | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| → `Filed` | filer | `POST_DISPUTE` binding; shape and criteria of Section 6.3 |
| → `Admitted` | atomic in `file` / `fileAdvisory`, or `accept` | `PRE_COMMIT` or Advisory; or the respondent accepts within the acceptance window |
| `Filed` → `NotAdmitted` | anyone (`expireAcceptance`) | after the acceptance window |
| `Filed` → `Withdrawn` | claimant (`withdraw`) | — |
| `Admitted` → `Provisional` | anyone (`decide`, `expireDecision`, `applySilence`) | a round decision exists and a review is still possible |
| `Admitted` → `Final` | anyone (same calls) | a round decision exists and no review is possible |
| `Provisional` → `Admitted` | claimant or respondent (`requestReview`) | before `reviewOpenUntil`; a new round opens |
| `Provisional` → `Final` | anyone (`finalize`) | after `reviewOpenUntil` |
| `Admitted` / `Provisional` → `Final` or `Expired` | anyone (`enforceDeadline`) | after `hardDeadline` |

`Final`, `Expired`, `NotAdmitted` and `Withdrawn` are terminal. A terminal case MUST NOT accept evidence, resolutions, decisions or reviews, and its ruling MUST NOT change. Every non-terminal state has a time-bound exit: the acceptance window, the decision window, the review window and the hard deadline.

### 8. Resolution of sub-questions

#### 8.1 Order

A sub-question MAY be resolved only while the case is `Admitted`, before `decisionDeadline`, once, and only after every premise has a finding. If a premise's finding has a value other than the required one, the sub-question MUST be recorded `UNDETERMINED` with reason `PREMISE_NOT_ESTABLISHED`, support kinds 0 and no method run. Otherwise the order is free: the dependency table, not the question type, decides what may be resolved when. Computable sub-questions SHOULD be placed first in a claim's design; this document does not impose it.

#### 8.2 Formal

```solidity
interface IADRFVerifier /* is IERC165 */ {
    function supportsInterface(bytes4 interfaceId) external view returns (bool);
    function verify(bytes32 proposition, bytes32 params, bytes calldata inputs)
        external view returns (ADRFTypes.Value value, bytes32 resultHash);
}
```

`resolveFormal(caseId, sq, inputs)` MAY be called by anyone; it MUST require `keccak256(inputs) == inputsHash` (inputs are pinned at filing so that nobody chooses them after the fact) and record the verifier's value with support kinds `computed`, the result hash and the verifier as `acceptedBy`. A verifier SHOULD return `UNDETERMINED` for a proposition it was not built to decide. A computed finding states that the proposition holds, or not, under the bound rule and the pinned inputs; it says nothing about whether the inputs describe reality.

#### 8.3 Evidenced (normative-optional)

```solidity
interface IADRFEvidenceRule /* is IERC165 */ {
    function supportsInterface(bytes4 interfaceId) external view returns (bool);
    function evaluate(bytes32 caseId, uint32 subQuestion, bytes32 proposition, bytes32 params, bytes calldata bundle)
        external returns (ADRFTypes.Value value, bytes32 resultHash, uint8 supportKinds);
}
```

`resolveEvidenced(caseId, sq, bundle)` MAY be called by anyone; the registry MUST record the rule's value, result hash and support kinds with `judged` masked out, and the rule as `acceptedBy`. The rule decides what it accepts; the policy's evidence rules (in the policy document) state who must provide what, how it is accessed, within which period, and what a failure to provide means. Evidence integrity (a hash fixes content) and evidence availability (others can obtain and check it) are distinct; a party's failure to provide is a procedural matter handled under the rule, not a proof of the allegation.

`submitEvidence(caseId, sq, commitment, uri)` emits `EvidenceSubmitted` while the case is `Admitted` or `Provisional` and before `evidenceCloseAt`. The registry attaches no meaning to evidence.

Facts that were never committed on chain — a model API's logs, a human review record, an off-line delivery, the use made of a dataset — enter the procedure under the applicable evidence rule; their submission and their acceptance confer no binding power and no authority to enact. The original rules, service terms and materials need not be on chain in advance: what admission checks separately is that the applicable rule exists and was declared before the event, that the evidence can be verified under a bound rule, who accepted the procedure, and which effects were authorized. Accepting the framework after a dispute arose does not rewrite the substantive obligations of the time.

Three versions of one service call are different things: the version the terms **committed**, the version the provider **declared** at delivery, and the version **actually used**. The first two are statements on record and a verifier can compare them; the third may be the very fact in dispute. A claimant MUST NOT be required to establish a hidden fact of the actor's own operation before filing: the case fixes which request, which terms and which claim, records what is not yet known as a fact to be established or an unverified premise through the case document's context, and leaves it to the evidence procedure. No case requires model weights, random seeds or a complete execution environment.

A single delivery and a statistical quality commitment are different obligations (kinds 1 and 4 of the reference vocabulary): a promise that this report contains the agreed content is checked on this delivery; a promise that a batch reaches an accuracy threshold is checked on that batch under the agreed metric; a promise of service quality over a period is checked on the agreed window and sampling rule. A few failing samples do not stand in for the test of a statistical commitment, and a later re-run with a better result does not stand in for the original delivery. The policy document states, for statistical claims, the unit of evaluation, the sample selection, the version scope, the metric, the threshold and the handling of uncertainty; the kernel holds none of it.

Reviewable and re-runnable are different properties. The framework requires that others can check which material, method, assumptions and procedure a finding rests on and what it covers; it does not promise that every AI service can be re-run to the same output. That a run cannot be reproduced does not by itself make a dispute inadmissible; it bears on the methods available, the sufficiency of the evidence and the scope of the conclusion. Evidence rules MAY accept restricted access, redacted material or statements of a third party, keeping their trust premises on record; the absence of public raw data is not, by itself, a violation.

#### 8.4 Judged

```solidity
interface IADRFJudgmentMethod /* is IERC165 */ {
    function supportsInterface(bytes4 interfaceId) external view returns (bool);
    function maxDuration(bytes32 params) external view returns (uint64);
    function open(bytes32 caseId, uint32 subQuestion, bytes32 proposition, bytes32 params, uint64 deadline)
        external returns (bytes32 handle);
    function review(bytes32 caseId, uint32 subQuestion) external;
}
```

`openJudgment(caseId, sq)` MAY be called by anyone once; the registry calls `open` with the sub-question's decision deadline and stores the non-zero handle. The method pushes the finding through `recordJudgedFinding(caseId, sq, value, resultHash, source)`, which the registry MUST accept only from the method bound to that sub-question, only after it was opened, with support kinds `judged` and the `JudgmentSource` (registry, issue, policy, source round, round count, adoption flag, the source's reason) stored with the finding. Section 11 specifies the reference method.

#### 8.5 Findings and support kinds

```solidity
struct JudgmentSource { address registry; bytes32 issueId; bytes32 policyId; uint32 sourceRound; uint32 roundCount; bool adoptedFromEarlierRound; uint8 reason; }
struct Finding { bool resolved; Value value; uint8 supportKinds; bytes32 resultHash; address acceptedBy; Reason reason; uint32 round; uint64 at; JudgmentSource source; }
```

A finding records what was decided (the proposition and its value), by which method and parameters (pinned at admission), on which inputs (the pinned hash or the rule's result hash), on which premises (the dependency table), with which support kinds, accepted by which contract, and why it is undetermined when it is. Support kinds are a set; the registry MUST NOT order them and MUST NOT derive one from another: a finding whose premises are `computed` does not become computed. The round decision records the union of the support kinds of the decisive finding and of its transitive premises as **provenance** — which sources the result cites — and that union confers no proof on the result.

#### 8.6 Optimistic track (normative-optional)

Under a policy with `optimistic == true`, admission sets `answerDeadline`. The respondent MAY `answer` before it, after which the ordinary procedure continues. If the respondent has not answered when the deadline passes, and the decisive finding does not exist, anyone MAY `applySilence` (in the first round only): round 1 is decided `Sustained` (`silenceRule == SUSTAIN`) or `NotSustained` (`NOT_SUSTAIN`) with reason `SILENCE`, `bySilence` set and support kinds 0. Silence adopts the result the parties accepted in advance for silence; it proves nothing, and a later `JUDGED` sub-question of the same case is never opened on its account. A finding formed on the merits before the deadline takes precedence: `applySilence` MUST be refused once the decisive finding exists.

### 9. Decision, review and finality

#### 9.1 Decision rule

`decide(caseId)` MAY be called by anyone once the decisive finding exists. The round result is: `Sustained` if the finding's value equals `supportsWhen`; `NotSustained` if it is the other determined value; otherwise the burden rule of the decisive sub-question's method binding applies — `NOT_SUSTAINED` yields `NotSustained` with reason `BURDEN_NOT_MET`, `NO_DECISION` yields `NoDecision` with the finding's reason. `Sustained` always means the claim was supported; a `NotSustained` result is not a finding of the opposite proposition.

`expireDecision(caseId)` MAY be called by anyone after `decisionDeadline` while the decisive finding is missing: the finding is recorded `UNDETERMINED` with reason `TIMEOUT` and the same rule applies.

After a round decision the case becomes `Provisional` with `reviewOpenUntil = min(now + reviewWindow, hardDeadline)` if `reviewsUsed < maxReviews`, and `Final` otherwise (Section 9.4).

#### 9.2 Round record

```solidity
struct RoundRecord { SubstantiveResult result; Reason reason; uint64 at; bool bySilence; uint8 supportKinds; Value decisiveValue; JudgmentSource judgment; }
```

#### 9.3 Reviews (normative-optional)

`requestReview(caseId, reviewType, targets)` MUST be accepted only while `Provisional`, before `reviewOpenUntil`, from the claimant or the respondent, for exactly one review type the track allows (`fact`, `evidence`, `rule application`, `conflict`, `procedure`), while `reviewsUsed < maxReviews`, with at least one target. The reset set is the targets closed under dependency (every sub-question that depends on a reset one is reset); the decisive sub-question MUST be in it. For each reset `JUDGED` sub-question that was opened, the registry calls `method.review`; the registry then deletes the findings of the reset set, increments `reviewsUsed` and `roundCount`, sets `decisionDeadline = min(now + decisionWindow, hardDeadline)` and `evidenceCloseAt = min(now + evidenceWindow, decisionDeadline)`, and returns to `Admitted`. Sub-questions outside the reset set keep their findings. Every review runs under the same policy version; a review that would need a different procedure is a new, separately authorized case.

#### 9.4 Adoption rule and closure

`finalize(caseId)` MUST be accepted only while `Provisional` and after `reviewOpenUntil` (closure `REVIEW_LAPSED`). `enforceDeadline(caseId)` MUST be accepted while `Admitted` or `Provisional` after `hardDeadline` (closure `TIMEOUT`); if the decisive finding is missing it is recorded `UNDETERMINED(TIMEOUT)` and the round is decided first. A case that decides a round with no review possible closes at once (closure `NONE`).

The adopted result is fixed by the policy's `reviewMode`; the kernel has no default:

- `PRESERVE_UNLESS_OVERTURNED`: adopt the most recent round whose result is `Sustained` or `NotSustained`; `sourceRound` is that round and `adoptedFromEarlierRound` is true when it is not the last. Only if no round has a substantive result is the result `NoDecision`, with the last round's reason.
- `REQUIRE_FRESH_DECISION`: opening a review vacates every earlier decision; adopt the last round's own result when it is substantive, otherwise `NoDecision` with its reason; `sourceRound == roundCount`.

A case whose adopted result is `NoDecision`, that never produced a substantive result and that closed by the hard deadline is `Expired`; every other closure is `Final`. Both are terminal and both release the exclusivity key to its final record. A timeout and an adopted decision coexist: the closure says how the procedure ended, the result says what was adopted.

### 10. Rulings, effects and enactment

#### 10.1 Ruling

```solidity
struct Ruling {
    ProcedureState state; SubstantiveResult result; EffectClass effectClass;
    Reason reason;           // of the adopted result
    Reason closure;          // NONE, REVIEW_LAPSED or TIMEOUT
    uint64 decidedAt; uint64 finalAt; uint32 roundCount; uint32 sourceRound;
    bool adoptedFromEarlierRound; bool bySilence;
    uint8 supportKinds;      // provenance of the adopted round
    bytes32 allowedEffect;   // the one substantive effect the result authorizes, or 0
    bytes32 fallbackRef;     // NoDecision: the pre-committed disposition
    bytes32 policyId; bytes32 bindingId; address source; bytes32 proposition;
    JudgmentSource judgment; // provenance of the decisive finding when it was JUDGED
}
```

`getRuling` MUST return the ruling once the case is `Final` or `Expired`, and otherwise a record whose `state` is the procedure state and whose `result` is `None`. For a Binding case `allowedEffect` is `effectOnSustained` when `Sustained`, `effectOnNotSustained` when `NotSustained`, and 0 otherwise; for an Advisory case it is always 0. `fallbackRef` is set exactly when the result is `NoDecision`.

#### 10.2 Effects

A ruling bounds effects in three layers. **Substantive**: the one allowed effect, chosen at filing from the requested effects, which the binding authorized and the policy declared `SUBSTANTIVE`. **Procedural and economic**: effects the policy declared `PROCEDURAL_ECONOMIC` and the binding authorized; they follow the policy's economic declaration and are reportable whatever the result, since they were accepted as terms of the procedure rather than requested in the claim. **Execution**: each executor checks, for each effect, its own authority, the amount it can enact now, the target and its own anti-duplication record. The adjudged amount, the enforceable amount and the enacted amount are three numbers; the registry holds none of them.

A `NoDecision` result authorizes no substantive effect and MUST NOT be read by any consumer as liability of either party; the disposition the business committed in advance (`fallbackRef`) continues to apply, and a consumer MUST NOT apply a consequence it did not commit to before the case.

#### 10.3 Enactment log

`reportEnactment(caseId, effectId, status, ref)` is an informative log. It MUST be accepted only while the case is `Final` or `Expired`, only for a Binding case, only from the source or an executor named by the binding, only with `status != NotRequested`, and only for `effectId` equal to the ruling's `allowedEffect` or to a `PROCEDURAL_ECONOMIC` effect the binding authorized. Once an effect is `Enacted` it MUST NOT be overwritten; any other recorded status MAY be replaced by a later report. `Unknown` is not `Failed`: it says that the executor does not yet know the fate of its attempt, and a reader MUST NOT treat it as a failure or as a reason to enact again. The log records the real reporter. Execution state is authoritative at the executor; the registry never changes a ruling because an enactment failed, and never prevents an executor from retrying what its own rules allow. Where the underlying act is irreversible, a remedy is compensation or substitute performance, never a reversal of the event.

#### 10.4 Interim measures

Interim measures (freezing, suspension) are not granted by this framework. A consumer that needs them takes them under its own safety or permission arrangements, with their own authority, scope, duration and restoration conditions; a filed case, a lead or a report is not by itself a basis for any of them, and a sustained challenge to a suspension does not by itself restore anything.

#### 10.5 Leads

`reportLead(object, commitment, uri)` emits `LeadReported(objectKey, reporter, commitment, uri)`. It opens no case and confers no standing, no power and no presumption.

### 11. J-profile v1: judgment through ERC-8436 (normative-optional)

A deployment that binds `JUDGED` sub-questions to the reference judgment method MUST implement the following behaviour; a different judgment method MUST satisfy `IADRFJudgmentMethod` and MUST state what its findings support.

- The method's `params` is an [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) `policyId` registered with the policy registry of the [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) registry the method is constructed for; `maxDuration(params)` returns that policy's `maxTotalDuration`, which Section 3.2 requires to fit the dispute policy's decision window.
- `open` MUST be accepted only from the dispute registry. The method files one issue in `CONSUMER_FILED` mode as the consumer, with subject `(chainId, disputeRegistry, uint256(caseId), proposition)`, question `keccak256(abi.encode(keccak256("adrf:judgment/v1"), subQuestion))`, `effectYes = keccak256("adrf:finding/TRUE")`, `effectNo = keccak256("adrf:finding/FALSE")`, disposition `keccak256("adrf:finding/UNDETERMINED")`, `obligationId = keccak256(abi.encode(caseId, subQuestion))` and the sub-question's decision deadline as `consumerDeadline`. The dispute registry has verified the upstream authority before calling; the method relies on that and on the [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) registry's own admission checks. One binary question, one fixed effect pair: non-binary outcome spaces are reserved (Section 14).
- `importResult(caseId, subQuestion)` MAY be called by anyone once. It MUST read the result from the [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) registry (never from a receipt), MUST require `state == Final`, and MUST map `Decided` with `outcomeYes` to `TRUE`, `Decided` without it to `FALSE` (the proposition was blocked under the rule, not refuted) and `NoDecision` to `UNDETERMINED`; it pushes the finding with `JudgmentSource` filled from the result, including `sourceRound`, `roundCount` and `adoptedFromEarlierRound`, so that finality by adoption is carried, not hidden. For a `Decided` result the method records `Enacted` for the matching effect on the [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) side.
- Review of a judged sub-question is the [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) appeal of its issue, under that policy's own appeal standing and before the issue is Final. Because the method imports only Final results, its `review` MUST revert; a review request that targets an imported judged sub-question therefore fails, and consumers that need a second panel need a new, separately authorized case under another policy. Where the [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) policy's appeal standing is `ANYONE`, third parties can appeal directly at the source; the dispute policy cannot narrow that.
- The [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) Final record is never rewritten by this framework; a dispute may verify that an import, an authorization or a procedure reference was correct, as a `FORMAL` sub-question over the source registry's own state.

### 13. Clock

A dispute registry MUST expose [ERC-6372](./eip-6372.md) `clock()` and `CLOCK_MODE()`. One registry instance uses one clock for every policy it runs; the reference implementation uses `mode=timestamp`. Windows are seconds of that clock. A binding fixes clock rules — the triggering event, the relative windows, the hard cap and the margin — and the absolute deadlines of a case are derived when its event is attested; a source whose own clock differs converts in its adapter and MUST NOT claim that a deadline will be met by converting durations between clocks.

| Profile | Status in this ERC |
|---|---|
| Minimal (bound source, formal verification, one round, one ruling) | REQUIRED |

| J-profile v1 (ERC-8436 judgment, one binary question) | normative-optional, Section 11 |

The following are **reserved**: this ERC names them so that implementations leave room for them, but it does not specify them and no conformance may be claimed for them under this document.

- *Panel replacement on review*: a review that runs a judged sub-question under a different judgment policy or roster, with its own authorization, adoption rule, execution limits and time budget.
- *Non-binary and multi-option outcomes* (amounts, categories, rankings) and typed aggregation of allowed effect sets.
- *Verifiable selection* of judges (sortition with a randomness source, candidate sets, exclusion rules, freeze instants) and *automatic independence screening* by control relations.
- *Economic modules*: bond and fee vaults, loser-pays schedules, challenger rewards, coherence rewards; this document fixes only what an economic declaration must state (Section 15).
- *Private evidence* (commitments with gated access, zero-knowledge evidence rules).
- *Cross-chain objects and rulings*; *mirrors* of rulings into validation or reputation registries such as [ERC-8004](./eip-8004.md).
- *Third-party funding* and *standing sources* other than a binding's terms.

A policy that would need any of these is outside this ERC until a later document specifies it.

### 15. Scope, object categories and consumer integration (informative)

The relation of a dispute to AI names the domain; the object, the obligation, the rule and the authorized effects decide how it enters the procedure. No global test of whether a system is "AI enough" is needed or defined: a case states its relation to an AI system, resource, service or activity and is admitted on the five criteria. The object categories below describe the scope of the first draft; the classification is open.

| Category | Objects that can be named | Typical contested points |
|---|---|---|
| AI resources | a model version, a dataset, a toolkit, a resource declaration | was the committed version delivered; were declarations about provenance or scope of use honored |
| AI systems and services | a service endpoint, a deployed version, a service agreement, a billing period | were version, availability, billing or usage-limit commitments kept |
| AI execution and delivery | a request, an output, a training job, a report, a tool call | were the agreed steps performed; was authority exceeded; does the delivery meet the acceptance conditions |
| AI evaluation and declarations | an evaluation report, a metric statement, a capability or safety assertion | were the agreed sample, metric and method used; is the verified scope stated truthfully |
| AI governance and intervention | a suspension, a permission change, an eligibility decision, an organizational decision | was it authorized; was the applicable procedure followed; did the disposition stay within bounds |

The scope of a ruling is fixed by its object. A finding that an evaluation report used the wrong sample supports a ruling on that report and the obligations attached to it; it does not establish that every version of the model is untrustworthy, that every service of the provider is in breach, or that every downstream user of the model is liable. A conclusion is confined to the event, version, sample and obligation it was formed on, and a provider's use of a base model does not transfer the provider's commitments to the base model's publisher.

An obligation source derives every attestation from its own state and never from the caller: the event instant and its own adoption deadline (the business window in which it can still adopt a ruling), the obligation the event belongs to, the norm it recognizes and when that norm was declared, the obligation kind and the parties. It binds itself to the policy versions it accepts, commits only effects it can enact, and names its executors. Its executor reads a `Final` ruling, checks finality, effect class, the allowed effect, its own object, the remaining amount and its anti-duplication record, enacts within its own authority and reports; a `NoDecision` leaves its pre-committed disposition in force. The worked example of the reference assets, an escrow for a model API service between two plain accounts, attests settlements (a limit obligation: charge at most once) and deliveries (a result obligation, with the version the terms committed and the version the provider declared on record) and refunds on a sustained claim; no agent identity, task token or swarm is involved. The standards of the agent economy — executable skills, task tenders, know-your-agent assertions, agent identities, collective decisions, discovery registries, swarm charters, economic unit valuations and safety bindings — are integration examples whose records become objects and whose contracts become sources and executors; none of them is a prerequisite for a case.

A policy's economic declaration — referenced by `economicDeclaration` and published with the policy document — states, for every bond or fee arrangement, the instrument and executor, the purposes it serves (an open reference classification: anti-abuse, cost bearing, performance guarantee, penalty for a proven procedural violation, third-party funding, reward pool, and others), the flows under every procedure state and result, the economic assumptions relied on, and what happens on failure. A reward or forfeit is reported with the clause it follows from and whether it is an allocation condition or a consequence of a proven violation; receiving a reward proves no fact, and forfeiting a bond proves no wrongdoing. Being in the minority of a panel is not, by this document, a violation. No fund pool pays out more than its available balance; remedies may be funded from any authorized source, not only from case bonds.

Exclusivity holds within one registry instance and one binding scope. A consumer that wants one procedure per obligation binds each obligation to one registry and, at enactment, checks what it already disposed of and what remains, since another registry or a differently described claim is not visible to the key. Execution state is per effect: a case can have its refund enacted and its fee allocation failed.

## Rationale

**AI, not agents, is the scope.** The framework was first drafted for disputes between agents; its objects — an identified record, an obligation declared before the event, a binding that commits effects, typed methods — never depended on the parties being agents. The scope therefore names the domain by its objects: AI systems, resources, services and the conduct, deliveries and declarations around them, between any participants that can hold accounts and accept bindings. The roles a case keeps apart (object, actor, obligor, judgment participants, executors) are what a generalized domain needs: a model service process may act while a provider bears the obligation, a human may review while a contract enacts, and the records say which is which without requiring any of them to carry an agent identity.

**A claim, not a party, is the object of a dispute.** Admission is an algorithmic test of five criteria against what the obligation source attests — an identifiable object and event, an accountable respondent, a norm declared before the event, a bound method for every sub-question, an open window — and it refuses whatever fails without pre-judging anything that passes. "This agent is untrustworthy" cannot be filed; "this delivery does not meet the criteria pinned at tender time" can. The respondent criterion identifies who is alleged to bear the obligation; liability is what the case decides.

**The claim fixes its own direction.** A sub-question asks whether a proposition holds; a claim is sustained when the proposition has the value the claim named. Mapping `conforms == true` to a sustained claim would be wrong for every claim of non-conformance, so `supportsWhen` is part of the claim and `Sustained` always reads "the claim was supported". A blocked proposition under a collective rule is likewise `FALSE` for the sub-question and sustains or defeats the claim according to that direction, never as a finding of the opposite.

**Support kinds are a set and the union is provenance.** An earlier draft ordered signed, computed, evidenced and judged and gave a ruling the weakest kind among its findings. That ordering is false — a signature and a replayed computation answer different questions — and it would let a judged conclusion built on a computed premise present itself as computed, or let a later judgment downgrade a verified premise. The kernel therefore records support per finding, keeps premises as premises, and forbids any claim beyond what a finding's own method supports. The union recorded on a round names the sources the result cites and nothing more.

**The dependency table replaces a fixed order.** Formal questions are usually resolved first, but not always: which verifier to run can depend on which definition a contract adopted, and a time-limit check can depend on an external delivery fact. A bounded, acyclic premise table expresses the real dependencies; a question whose premise failed is recorded as not reached rather than answered by a method that was never meant to run.

**Four dimensions, not one enumeration.** A review window can lapse and an earlier decision be adopted in the same closure; an enactment can succeed for one effect and fail for another. Procedure state, substantive result, reason and per-effect enactment status are therefore separate, and `Expired` is reserved for a case that produced no substantive result at all.

**NoDecision keeps its meaning and routes to a pre-committed disposition.** The burden rule is a property of the method binding, so a policy can make an unverified formal claim fall on the claimant while a judgment that was never reached ends in no decision. Consumers never improvise: the fallback is the disposition the business committed before the case, referenced in the ruling.

**Authority comes from the binding; the registry holds no funds.** Credit, stake or reputation can qualify someone to judge; they never grant power over another party's assets. A binding is the source's own statement of what a ruling may authorize, and an executor enacts only within that statement and its own current conditions. Economic mechanisms are declared, not designed here: the framework fixes what a declaration must say and records what executors report, so that adopters choose their own loser-pays, bond or reward schemes without the kernel endorsing any.

**Reuse of ERC-8436.** Collective judgment is the one question type that needs a procedure with its own finality, and [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) already defines it with explicit authorization, frozen policies, appeals and an adoption rule. The J-profile consumes it as a consumer, through its real interface, with one binary question and a fixed effect pair, importing only Final results with their provenance. The profile is normative-optional, and this document depends on that ERC because the profile cannot be implemented or understood without it. Panel replacement, non-binary outcomes, verifiable selection and independence screening are reserved in both documents and are left to a later one.

**Relation to oracle councils and arbitration interfaces.** [ERC-8033](./eip-8033.md) produces answers to information queries through information agents and a judge, with an optional bonded re-judgment; this framework contests existing records against pre-declared obligations and routes judgment to any collective-decision registry. The arbitrable/arbitrator split of earlier arbitration interfaces, in which the arbitrated contract enforces a ruling it receives, is kept as the executor pattern; what is added is the object, the norm, the typed resolution and the bounded authority.

## Backwards Compatibility

No backward compatibility issues were found. The framework introduces two new registries and references existing objects, obligations, policies and registries by their own identifiers without modifying them. Bound business contracts expose `IADRFObligationSource` through an adapter when they cannot be changed. The J-profile uses the [ERC-8436](./eip-8436.md) interfaces as published; no change to that ERC is required.

## Security Considerations

**Authority.** A ruling has force only through a binding the source itself registered, and only for effects the source could enact. An attacker who controls a policy's update authority can publish new versions but cannot change what any existing binding or case is bound to; an attacker who controls a method contract controls the findings of the sub-questions bound to it, which is why methods are pinned by address at admission and why a policy document should pin their code. Consumers should bind only to methods whose code they have reviewed.

**Attestations by the source.** Admission trusts the obligation source about events, obligations, norms and parties. A source that attests a norm declared after the event, or omits a party, opens or closes the door to claims about its own objects; the attestation is public and attributable, and a source's misstatements are themselves records that a consumer or another framework can hold against it. The registry does not check that the attested norm reflects the actual agreement; that is between the parties and the source.

**Inputs and evidence.** Formal inputs are pinned at filing, so the party who files chooses them; a verifier that decides anything other than the canonical proposition for its inputs, or that reads mutable state, weakens the finding, and the proposition check of the reference verifier is the recommended pattern. Evidence rules accept what they are built to accept; an attestor panel is a trust assumption, declared as such, and its support kinds say so.

**Time.** All windows depend on block time. A business window shorter than the hard cap plus the margin is refused at admission, and a review never extends the hard deadline; sources should choose dispute periods that leave time for the whole procedure and should expect latency between a ruling and its inclusion. A judged sub-question that nobody imports before the decision window closes ends in a timeout; the claimant has the incentive to import, and anyone may.

**Liveness and griefing.** Every step that could stall has a permissionless timeout, so no party can hold a case open; the price of that is that a party who lets a window lapse accepts its consequence. Under the optimistic track a respondent who does not answer accepts the silence rule it agreed to at binding. Filing is cheap and standing is checked, so the surface for spurious cases is the set of parties the source attests plus the beneficiaries and open challengers the binding admits; economic deterrents, if wanted, are declared by the policy and enforced by executors, not by the kernel.

**Exclusivity.** The key is derived from the source, the attested obligation and the proposition. Two claims that describe the same point with different propositions, or the same obligation bound to two registries, are not caught; executors should check what was already disposed of before enacting.

**Judgment at the source.** When the J-profile is used, the panel's independence, selection and incentives are those of the collective-decision policy; a policy whose appeal standing is open to anyone lets third parties appeal at the source. The import carries the source's provenance so that a consumer can see whether a result was adopted after a failed appeal round rather than confirmed. A roster of accounts does not by itself prove the identity or the independence of the people, organizations or agents behind them; representation, control relations and conflicts of interest are declared and checked by the adopted configuration, not inferred from addresses.

**AI-assisted judgment.** Where a method or a panel uses AI to evaluate material, the material submitted by a party is evidence to be assessed and nothing more: it can never acquire the ability to change procedure rules, call an executor or widen an authorization, and a method whose model could be steered by the content it reads should treat that content as untrusted input, separate from its instructions. The same holds for the texts of claims, evidence URIs and lead reports read by any automated participant.

**Reviews and finality.** A review cannot be opened after its window, cannot exceed the policy's count and cannot reach a different policy; a final ruling cannot be re-litigated through a new case on the same key. Remedies for a final ruling that proves wrong are new, separately authorized matters.

**Enactment.** The log is informative. An executor that reports `Enacted` without enacting, or `Unknown` indefinitely, misleads readers of the log but changes no ruling; readers who need execution state read it from the executor.
