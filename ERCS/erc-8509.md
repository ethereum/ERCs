## Abstract

This proposal defines the report a reader returns about what anyone can link to an [ERC-8004](./eip-8004.md) agent
from public chain data: whether payments to the agent land on its owner, whether the owner funded or paid the
agent's payment address, how many agents the owner holds, whether other agents share the payment address, and how
many payers are visible. A report is a JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) object pinned to one block. Every field
carries a value and a pointer to its evidence (a transaction, a log, a call at a block, or an address whose history
was read), or it is **not read**, with a reason, which is never the same as zero, `false` or an empty list. A report
also records what helps: a registered [ERC-6538](./eip-6538.md) stealth meta-address and receipts from shielded pools
named by the reader. The report standardizes the facts, not their weight: a score is optional and
implementation-defined, and "linked" always means an on-chain transfer, never proof of common control.

## Motivation

An ERC-8004 agent publishes its owner, its `agentWallet` and, through the service endpoints in its registration
file, the addresses it asks to be paid at. Each of these is an address with a public history. A payer who pays an
agent, and anyone who watches the chain, can often link the payment address to the owner, the owner to its other
agents, and every payer to the agent. Agents that sell services to other agents make this worse: an agent's payers
are often agents, and each payment links two operators in public.

The links are simple to read and seldom checked. In a census of one ERC-8004 identity registry (chain 4663) read on
2026-11-21, 25,620 of 26,235 registered agents had no `agentWallet` distinct from their owner, so every payment to
them lands on the owner's own address. Several readers already compute some of these links, under different names,
thresholds and scores, and none says what it could not read.

Three problems follow:

1. **No shared fields.** An agent operator, a wallet or a paying agent that asks two readers gets two scores that
   cannot be compared, and cannot tell which link moved a score.
2. **No evidence and no block.** A claim that "the owner funded the payment wallet" without the funding transaction
   cannot be checked, and one without a block cannot be reproduced after the owner or `agentWallet` changes.
3. **Absence reads as private.** A reader that did not get through the payment address's history and says nothing
   looks the same as one that read it and found no link.

This proposal standardizes the report: which links a reader read, about which agent, at which block, with what
evidence, and what it could not read. It does not standardize how to weigh them.

## Specification

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in RFC 2119 and RFC 8174.

### Terms

- A **reader** is a service or program that reads public chain data about an agent and returns a report.
- A **consumer** is the party that reads a report and acts on it: the agent's operator, a wallet, a paying agent or a
  directory.
- The **agent** is one ERC-8004 identity, named by its chain, its identity registry and its `agentId`.
- The **report's block** is the block named by the report's `block` member. Unless a field says otherwise, the
  history a field covers is every block up to and including the report's block.
- The **owner** is the address that holds the agent's identity token at the report's block (`ownerOf(agentId)`).
- **Payment terms** and the **`payTo` addresses** are defined in [Payment terms](#payment-terms).
- A **payment address** is the agent's `agentWallet` at the report's block when it is not the zero address, or one of
  its `payTo` addresses.
- A **transfer of value** from address A to address B is one of:
  1. a non-zero amount of the chain's native currency sent from A to B by a transaction, or by a call or a
     self-destruct inside a transaction (an internal transfer, as recorded in the transaction's execution trace);
  2. a fungible token transfer: a log with exactly three topics, whose first topic is
     `keccak256("Transfer(address,address,uint256)")`, whose second and third topics are A and B, and whose data is a
     non-zero amount.

  Transfers of zero amount, token transfers with four topics (non-fungible tokens), and value credited without a
  sender (such as consensus-layer withdrawals and block rewards) are not transfers of value.
- Two addresses are **linked** when a transfer of value between them is recorded on chain. A link is a fact about a
  transfer. It is never proof that one party controls both addresses.
- A field is **not read** when the reader did not establish its value. Not read is a status of its own; it is never
  written as zero, `false`, `null` or an empty list.
- A field is **none** when the reader read it completely and found nothing: a count of `0`, the value `false` or an
  empty list, with evidence of what was read.
- Integers that can exceed `2^53` (block numbers, agent identifiers, chain identifiers, scheme identifiers) are JSON
  strings holding decimal digits with no leading zeros. Addresses are `0x`-prefixed hexadecimal strings; consumers
  compare them without regard to case.

A value that depends on the absence of transfers or on the earliest transfer (a none value, `linked` `false`, a first
funder, a count of payers or counterparties) MUST be computed over every kind of transfer of value listed above,
unless the field's value carries an `assets` member, in which case it covers exactly the assets listed there. A
reader that cannot read one of these kinds for such a field MUST report the field not read.

### Report

A report is a JSON object with these members:

| Member | Required | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `version` | yes | `"1"` |
| `read_at` | yes | The Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) at which the reader finished reading, as an RFC 3339 date-time ending in `Z` |
| `block` | yes | The number of the block every field holds at, as a decimal string; this is the number `eth_blockNumber` returns in hexadecimal, written in decimal |
| `block_hash` | no | The hash of `block`, so a consumer can detect a reorganization |
| `source` | no | `{ name, url, agentId }`: who produced the report; `agentId` is the reader's own ERC-8004 identity on the same chain, if it has one |
| `agent` | yes | The agent and its addresses (below) |
| `findings` | yes | The core findings (below) |
| `helps` | yes | What reduces linkability (below) |
| `extensions` | no | Optional blocks; this proposal defines `payment_wallet_leaks` |
| `partial` | yes | `true` when any field is not read for a reason other than `unsupported` |
| `partial_reasons` | yes | One `{ field, reason }` for each such field; `field` is a JSON Pointer (RFC 6901) into the report |
| `score` | no | `{ value, max, label, method }`: an implementation-defined summary (below) |

A report that breaks a requirement of this Specification is invalid. A consumer MUST reject an invalid report and
MUST NOT act on any of its fields.

A [JSON Schema](../assets/eip-8509/linkability-report.schema.json) for the report is provided as an aid. It is not
normative: where it and this Specification disagree, this Specification prevails.

A reader MAY answer a request with something other than a report, for example a notice that the report is not ready
yet. Such answers are outside this format. A consumer MUST NOT treat an answer that is not a report as a report, or as
none.

### Fields

Every field is one of two shapes:

```json
{ "status": "read", "value": <value>, "evidence": [<evidence>, ...] }
{ "status": "not_read", "reason": "<reason>", "detail": "<optional text>" }
```

A read field MUST carry a `value` member and at least one evidence item. Its `value` MUST NOT be `null` unless the
field's definition allows `null`; only `agent.agentWallet` does. A not read field MUST NOT carry `value` or
`evidence`.

The reason of a not read field is one of:

| Reason | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `unsupported` | The reader never reads this field, or never reads it for entries of this role, for any agent |
| `unavailable` | A data source the reader needs did not answer, answered with an error, or gave no value |
| `incomplete` | The reader read only part of the history it needs, for example a page limit |
| `timeout` | The reader stopped within its own time budget |
| `rate-limited` | A data source refused the reader for its request rate |
| `invalid` | The data exists but cannot be interpreted, for example a registration file or payment terms that do not parse |

A reader MUST NOT use `unsupported` for a field, or for entries of a role, that it reads for other agents. A reader
that tried to read a field and did not finish MUST report it not read with `unavailable`, `incomplete`, `timeout`,
`rate-limited` or `invalid`, and MUST NOT report it as none.

An evidence item is one of:

| `type` | Members | Points to |
|---|---|---|
| `call` | `to`, `data`, `block` | An `eth_call` anyone can repeat at `block` |
| `tx` | `hash`, optional `block` | A transaction |
| `log` | `tx`, `logIndex` | One log of a transaction |
| `address` | `address`, optional `from_block`, `to_block` | The history of an address the reader read, between the blocks given, both included |
| `block` | `block` | A block |

An evidence item MUST NOT name a block (`block`, `from_block` or `to_block`) after the report's block. A reader SHOULD
prefer `tx`, `log` and `call` evidence where one exists; `address` evidence states where to look, not which record
proves the value.

### Entries

A field about the agent's addresses is an object with one member, `entries`, whose value is a list:

```json
{ "entries": [<entry>, ...] }
```

Each entry is a field with two more members: `role` (`agentWallet`, `payTo` or `owner`) and `address`. Entries are
per role, not per distinct address: a field that covers payment addresses holds one entry with role `agentWallet`
when the `agentWallet` is set, and one entry with role `payTo` for each distinct `payTo` address. An address that
holds two roles, for example an `agentWallet` that is also a `payTo` address, has one entry for each role. An entry
with role `owner` exists only where a field says so.

`address` is `null` in two cases only:

- an entry with role `agentWallet` in `payment_lands_on_owner` when the `agentWallet` is unset, with value `"unset"`;
- an entry whose address the reader could not learn. Such an entry MUST be not read.

When `agent.payTo` is not read, every field that holds entries MUST hold one entry with role `payTo` and address
`null`, not read with the same reason. When `agent.payTo` is read as an empty list, fields hold no `payTo` entries.

### Agent

`agent` names the agent and its addresses at the report's block:

| Member | Shape | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `chainId` | decimal string | The chain of the identity registry |
| `identityRegistry` | address | The ERC-8004 identity registry |
| `agentId` | decimal string | The agent |
| `owner` | field, value an address | `ownerOf(agentId)` at `block` |
| `agentWallet` | field, value an address or `null` | `getAgentWallet(agentId)` at `block`; `null` when it is unset (zero) |
| `payTo` | field, value a list of addresses | The distinct `payTo` addresses in the payment terms the agent's endpoints served (below) |

ERC-8004 clears `agentWallet` when the identity token is transferred. A reader MUST read `owner` and `agentWallet` at
the report's block, and MUST NOT carry an `agentWallet` over from an earlier read, a cache or the registration file.
A reader SHOULD give a `call` evidence item for each.

### Payment terms

Payment terms are read off chain, from the agent's own endpoints, as follows:

1. The reader reads the agent's registration file from `tokenURI(agentId)` of the identity registry at the report's
   block.
2. For each element of the registration file's `services` list whose `endpoint` is an `https://` Uniform Resource
   Locator (URL), the reader sends one Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP) `GET` request to that URL, without a
   payment.
3. An answer with status `402` (Payment Required) carries payment terms when its body is a JSON object with an
   `accepts` list, or, when the body is not such an object, when its `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` header holds such an object
   encoded in base64. This is the answer format of the x402 payment protocol.
4. The `payTo` addresses are the distinct, non-zero `payTo` members of the elements of `accepts` whose `network`
   names the agent's chain, either as `eip155:` followed by the decimal `chainId`, or as a name the reader maps to that
   chain.

A reader MUST NOT add addresses from any other source to `payTo`. `payTo` holds the payment terms the endpoints served
during the reader's read, which ends at `read_at`; payment terms can change without a transaction, so `payTo` is
bound to that read, not to the block. Evidence of a read `payTo` MUST include a `block` item naming the report's block,
and SHOULD include a `call` item for `tokenURI(agentId)`.

`payTo` is read, with an empty list as none, when every such endpoint answered and none served payment terms for the
agent's chain. It is not read with reason `unavailable` when the registration file or every endpoint could not be
fetched, `incomplete` when some endpoints answered and others did not, and `invalid` when the registration file does
not parse.

### Core findings

`findings` holds these six fields. All are REQUIRED; a reader that does not read one reports it not read.

**`payment_lands_on_owner`**: entries, one with role `agentWallet` (present even when the `agentWallet` is unset) and
one for each `payTo` address. The value is `true` when the address equals the owner, `false` when it does not, and
`"unset"` for the `agentWallet` entry when the `agentWallet` is `null` (address `null`). A consumer that pays an agent
with no payment address set usually pays the owner; the `"unset"` value says so without calling it a link.

**`owner_funded_payment_address`**: entries, one for each payment address. The value is
`{ first_funder, is_owner }`, with an optional `funding_tx`: the address that sent the first transfer of value into the
payment address, whether it is the owner, and that transaction. A reader that has the transaction SHOULD give it. For a
payment address equal to the owner the value is `"same-address"`. A reader that did not reach the first transfer of
the address's history MUST report the entry not read with reason `incomplete`.

**`owner_payment_direct_link`**: entries, one for each payment address. The value is `{ linked }`, with optional
`owner_to_address` and `address_to_owner`: `linked` is `true` when at least one transfer of value between the owner
and the payment address, in either direction, is recorded up to and including the report's block, and the counts are
the number of such transfers each way. `{ "linked": false }` is none, and a reader MUST NOT report it unless it read
every counterparty of the payment address up to and including the report's block. Evidence SHOULD list the transfers,
or the first of them when there are many. For a payment address equal to the owner the value is `"same-address"`.

**`owner_agent_count`**: the number of agents in the identity registry the owner holds at the report's block,
including this one, as an integer of at least 1. ERC-8004 defines the identity registry as an
[ERC-721](./eip-721.md) contract, so this is always `balanceOf(owner)` on the registry at the report's block.

**`shared_payment_address`**: entries, one for each payment address. The value is `{ agent_ids, compared }`:
`compared` lists what the reader compared across the registry (`"agentWallet"`, `"payTo"` or both), and `agent_ids`
lists the other `agentId`s whose compared address is the same address at the report's block. An optional
`lower_bound` is `true` when the reader listed only the first agents it found. `agent_ids` `[]` is none only for what
`compared` names: a reader that compared `agentWallet` alone says nothing about agents that take payment on the same
`payTo`.

**`payers_visible`**: `{ addresses, window_seconds, payers }`, with optional `payments` and `assets`. `addresses` is
the set of distinct addresses the reader counted: the payment addresses the reader learned, or the owner alone when
it learned none (an unset `agentWallet` and a `payTo` that is empty or not read). `payers` is the number of distinct senders of a transfer of value into `addresses`, `payments` the
number of such transfers, and `assets` the assets the reader counted. A transfer counts when the timestamp of its
block is greater than `T - window_seconds` and at most `T`, where `T` is the timestamp of the report's block. A count
without its window MUST NOT be reported. A count of `0` is none only for the addresses in `addresses`.

### Helps

`helps` holds what can make an agent harder to link. Both fields are REQUIRED and either can be not read.

**`stealth_meta_address`**: entries, one with role `owner` and one for each payment address. The value is
`{ registry, checked, scheme_ids }`: the ERC-6538 registry read, the scheme identifiers the reader checked, and those
of them for which the address has a non-empty stealth meta-address registered at the report's block. `[]` is none
for the schemes in `checked` only. A registered stealth meta-address lets payers send to
[ERC-5564](./eip-5564.md) stealth addresses instead of the payment address.

**`shielded_pool_receipts`**: `{ addresses, pool_list, pools, received_from }`. `addresses` is the set of addresses
read, chosen as for `payers_visible`: the payment addresses the reader learned, or the owner alone when it learned
none.
`pools` is a list of contract addresses the reader was given (`pool_list` names that list, for example a URL or a
configuration name), and `received_from` is the subset of `pools` from which an address in `addresses` received a
transfer of value up to and including the report's block. The list is supplied by the reader or the consumer. A
pool's presence in `pools` means only that the reader looked for transfers from it. A reader MUST NOT include a pool
list it does not name, and a consumer MUST NOT read a pool in `pools` or in `received_from` as vouched for, safe or
compliant by the reader or by this proposal. `received_from` `[]` is none only for the addresses in `addresses` and the
pools in `pools`. `pool_list` is a label only: two reports used the same pools when their `pools` hold the same
addresses, whatever their `pool_list` says. A reader with no pool list reports the field not read with reason
`unsupported`.

### Extension: `payment_wallet_leaks`

`extensions.payment_wallet_leaks` is OPTIONAL. It describes one address, `address`: the payment address a consumer
cares about, or the owner when no payment address is set. Each member below is a field. A member that is omitted MUST
be treated as not read, and never as none.

| Member | Value |
|---|---|
| `funding_sibling` | `{ first_funder, other_funded }`: the first funder of `address` and how many other addresses it was also the first funder of |
| `linked_counterparties` | `{ sent_to, received_from, lower_bound, assets, sample }`: how many distinct addresses `address` sent value to and received value from; `lower_bound` is `true` when the reader stopped counting early; `sample` lists some of them |
| `activity_hour_concentration` | `{ window_hours, from_hour_utc, to_hour_utc, share_bps, tx_count }`: see below |
| `holdings_visibility` | `{ tokens, unpriced, usd_value }`: how many tokens the address holds, how many the reader could not price, and the value of the rest in United States dollars (USD) as a decimal string |

For `activity_hour_concentration`, `tx_count` is the number of transactions sent from `address` up to and including
the report's block, and `window_hours` (1 to 23) is the window length the reader chose. The reader takes the run of
`window_hours` consecutive UTC hours, wrapping at midnight, that holds the most of those transactions by their block
timestamp; `from_hour_utc` is its first hour, `to_hour_utc` is `(from_hour_utc + window_hours) mod 24` (the hour it
ends before), and `share_bps` is the share of `tx_count` in it, in basis points, rounded down. When `tx_count` is `0`,
`from_hour_utc` and `share_bps` are `0`. The value carries no threshold: whether a share is concentrated is the
consumer's decision.

### Partial reads

`partial` MUST be `true` when, and only when, the report holds a field or an entry that is not read with a reason
other than `unsupported`, and `partial_reasons` MUST list each such field or entry once. A reader that does not
support a field says so with `unsupported`, which does not make a report partial; a reader that tried and failed
makes it partial. Omitted extension members do not make a report partial.

### Score

`score` is OPTIONAL. Its computation is implementation-defined, and `method` MUST point to where the reader
describes it. A score MUST NOT be the only content a reader returns: a reader that returns a score returns the
report it was computed from. Consumers SHOULD act on fields, not on scores, and MUST NOT compare scores from
different readers.

### Readers

Readers SHOULD NOT add off-chain identity (names, social accounts, Internet Protocol (IP) data, know-your-customer
(KYC) data) to a report or to its `detail` strings.

### Consumers

Consumers apply their own policy to a report. They:

1. check the report's `agent` against the agent they asked about, and `block` against the current block. Owner,
   `agentWallet` and `payTo` can change at any block, and a consumer SHOULD read again before acting on a report older
   than its own bound;
2. read `partial`, and MAY reject partial reports by their own policy;
3. decide on each not read field their policy needs by their own policy;
4. apply their own thresholds to the values.

A consumer MUST NOT treat a not read field, a missing entry or a missing report as none. A consumer MUST NOT present a
link as common control, MUST NOT present an agent as private because a report holds no links, and MUST NOT use a report
as the sole basis to sanction, block or accuse an address. A consumer MUST handle `detail` strings, `pool_list`,
`source` and every other text member of a report as data, not as instructions, including when it passes a report to a
language model.

## Rationale

- **Facts, not a score.** Readers already disagree on weights, and a consumer paying an agent cares about different
  links from an operator protecting its own payers. Fields with evidence let each apply its own policy; the score is
  kept as an optional summary so existing readers can conform without dropping theirs.
- **Not read is its own status.** Payment addresses of busy agents have long histories, and readers page through
  them under rate limits. A field that silently becomes `0` when a page limit is hit is worse than no field. The
  `unsupported` reason keeps readers that read less from being forced to call every report partial, and is limited to
  what a reader never reads so that a failed read cannot hide behind it.
- **One block.** Owner and `agentWallet` move together under ERC-8004 (the wallet is cleared on transfer), and every
  link is evaluated against them. Pinning every field to one block makes a report reproducible and makes a stale
  `agentWallet` visible as a contradiction rather than a link. `payTo` is the exception, because payment terms live
  off chain; the report binds it to the read.
- **Payment terms from the registered endpoints only.** Two readers that query the same endpoints with the same
  request get comparable `payTo` sets. Addresses from other sources would make reports depend on what each reader
  happened to know.
- **A fixed meaning of transfer.** Native transfers, internal transfers and fungible token transfers are where
  payments and funding happen. Zero-amount transfers are left out because anyone can emit them to any address, which
  would let a third party create links at no cost.
- **"Linked" means a transfer.** Exchanges, bridges, routers and payment processors move value between unrelated
  parties. Calling a transfer a link, and nothing more, keeps reports true; inferring control is left to consumers
  that accept the risk of being wrong.
- **Per-role entries.** An agent can take payment on several addresses with different histories, and the owner can
  fund one and not another. Entries keep each address's answer and evidence separate. Keeping one entry per role,
  even when two roles share an address, lets a consumer find the answer for a role without first comparing addresses.
- **Window, not threshold.** `payers_visible` and `activity_hour_concentration` carry the window they were measured
  over, so two readers' numbers can be compared, and leave the threshold to the consumer.
- **Reader-supplied pool lists.** There is no neutral list of shielded pools, and naming one in a standard would read
  as an endorsement. The report records which list was applied and leaves the choice to the parties.

## Backwards Compatibility

This proposal adds a report format and changes no contract. Readers that already publish a score can add the report
next to it.

## Test Cases

[Test vectors](../assets/eip-8509/README.md) accompany this proposal. `vectors/valid` holds four reports mapped from
answers of a live reader about agents 20030, 7622, 3000 and 9000 on chain 4663, read between 2026-11-21T07:54:05Z and
2026-11-21T07:59:18Z, each at the block its answer names. `owner`, `agentWallet`, `owner_agent_count` and the stealth
meta-addresses were confirmed by `eth_call` at that block. `vectors/invalid` holds reports that are invalid under the
Specification, one defect each. `validate.mjs` is a dependency-free validator that checks both against the schema and
the rules above.

A conforming reader and consumer pass these cases:

- An agent whose `agentWallet` and `payTo` both equal its owner yields `payment_lands_on_owner` entries `true`, and
  `owner_funded_payment_address` and `owner_payment_direct_link` entries `"same-address"`, one entry for each role
  although both roles hold the same address (agents 20030, 7622).
- An agent whose `agentWallet` is unset yields `agentWallet` `null` and a `payment_lands_on_owner` entry `"unset"`
  with address `null`, and no other `agentWallet` entries (agents 3000, 9000); its `payTo`, when it has one, is
  judged on its own (agent 3000: `payTo` equals the owner).
- An agent whose owner holds three agents yields `owner_agent_count` `3`, with a `balanceOf` call as evidence (agent
  9000).
- A reader that could not read who paid the agent yields `payers_visible` not read with reason `unavailable`,
  `partial` `true` and a `partial_reasons` entry `/findings/payers_visible` (agent 9000).
- A reader that read the payments and found none yields `payers_visible` with `payers` `0` and evidence (agent 3000);
  one that found some gives the count and its window (agent 20030: 9 payers in 2592000 seconds).
- A reader that read only part of a payment address's history yields `shielded_pool_receipts` not read with reason
  `incomplete` and `partial` `true` (agent 7622).
- A reader whose source gave no `payTo` value for an agent yields `payTo` not read with reason `unavailable`, a
  `payTo` entry with address `null`, not read with the same reason, in each field that holds entries, and a
  `partial_reasons` entry for `/agent/payTo` and for each of those entries (agent 9000).
- A reader that learned no payment address reads `shielded_pool_receipts` on the owner, with `addresses` naming the
  owner (agent 9000).
- A reader that checked stealth scheme 1 only yields `checked` `["1"]`, and `scheme_ids` `[]` says nothing about
  other schemes.
- These reports are each invalid and are rejected: a not read field that carries a `value`; a read field without
  `evidence`; a read `payers_visible` whose `value` is `null`; a report whose `partial` is `false` while a field is
  not read with reason `rate-limited`; an entry naming an `agentWallet` that differs from `agent.agentWallet`; and an
  evidence item whose block is after the report's block.

## Reference Implementation

The reference implementation is the validator and the vectors in the assets folder:

- [`validate.mjs`](../assets/eip-8509/validate.mjs) checks a report against the
  [schema](../assets/eip-8509/linkability-report.schema.json) and against the rules of the Specification that a schema
  cannot express: `partial` and `partial_reasons` agree with the fields not read, no evidence block is after the
  report's block, per-address entries agree with `agent.owner`, `agent.agentWallet` and `agent.payTo`, entries with
  address `null` follow the rules in [Entries](#entries), and `activity_hour_concentration` windows are consistent.
- The vectors in `vectors/valid` and `vectors/invalid` are described in [Test Cases](#test-cases). The raw answers of
  the reader they were mapped from are kept in `reference-responses`, and the
  [mapping notes](../assets/eip-8509/README.md) list the rules field by field.

On chain 4663, where the vectors were read, the ERC-8004 identity registry is at
`0x8004a169fb4a3325136eb29fa0ceb6d2e539a432` and the ERC-6538 registry at

## Security Considerations

- **The report aggregates public data.** Each fact is public, but a report puts the owner, its other agents, its
  funder, its counterparties, its holdings and its active hours in one place. That is a doxxing aid. Readers should
  not publish reports about an agent in bulk beyond what their consumers ask for, and the Specification asks them not
  to add off-chain identity. The `payment_wallet_leaks` extension is optional for this reason.
- **Links are not control.** A transfer from an exchange hot wallet, a bridge or a router links unrelated parties. A
  consumer that treats a link as common control will be wrong, and a party that wants two addresses to look related
  can create a link at the cost of one transfer. The Specification forbids consumers from using a report as the sole
  basis to sanction, block or accuse an address.
- **Absence.** A not read field, a missing entry or a missing report says nothing about the agent. A report with no
  links is not evidence of privacy: links through addresses outside the report, timing and amounts are not covered.
- **Partial reads.** Busy addresses exceed the history a reader can page through within its budget or rate limit.
  The Specification requires these fields to be reported not read, never as none.
- **Rate limits and cost.** A cold report reads several address histories. Readers should rate-limit per client.
  Answers that are not reports, such as a notice to retry later, are outside this format, and consumers do not treat
  them as reports.
- **Payment terms.** `payTo` is read from endpoints the agent's operator controls, which can serve different terms
  to different requesters or change them between reads. A report records what one reader was served at one time.
- **Time of check.** Owner, `agentWallet` and `payTo` can change at any block. A report older than a consumer's own
  bound is stale.
- **Shielded pools.** A pool in `pools` is there because someone chose to look for it, and is not vouched for by the
  reader or by this proposal. A pool can also be sanctioned in some jurisdictions; whether to use one is the agent
  operator's decision.
- **Stealth meta-addresses.** A registered meta-address helps only if payers use it. A stealth address funded from,
  or swept to, the owner is linked again by that transfer. A payer always knows the stealth address it paid, and the
  amount and announcement stay public; what a stealth address hides is only the link to the owner.
- **Injection through the report.** `detail` strings and pool list names are text a reader controls. Consumers that
  pass reports to a language model are exposed to instructions hidden in them, which is why the Specification
  requires every text member to be handled as data.
