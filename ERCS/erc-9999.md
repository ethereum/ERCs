## Abstract

This ERC defines the Agent Safety Framework (ASF): a registry model through which the safety arrangements that govern autonomous systems become protocol objects with a source, a version, a scope, an adoption relation and a lifecycle.

In this document, safety arrangement is used in the broad sense that covers security (resistance to unauthorized access, manipulation and theft), safety (avoidance of unacceptable harm with or without an attacker), control (the ability of parties outside the system to limit its effects when it errs, is manipulated or drifts) and assurance (the evidence under which, within what scope and for how long, the system may be relied upon). The framework standardizes none of the underlying mechanisms. Identity, authorization, policy evaluation, execution gating, evidence and dispute resolution remain with the proposals and systems that provide them.

Two objects are standardized. A Safety Specification is a reusable, versioned, content-addressed document that states which protected interests a set of requirements serves, which constraints apply, who may adopt and enforce them, which evidence is expected, through which channels and at which locations the constraints are enforced, how unknown conditions and failures are handled, and which remedies are pre-accepted. A Safety Binding is the attributable statement, made on chain by the adopting account itself, that it adopts one pinned version of a specification for one subject within one scope, under a named external authority basis and declared enforcement locations, for a validity window, with named interveners and an explicit lifecycle. Bindings can be derived from other bindings without ever widening them.

A registry-level check lets any consumer ask, before it acts, whether a set of bindings currently satisfies a requirement it has published, and receive a verdict of satisfied, not satisfied or unknown together with the reason. Which bindings a resource requires is decided by the resource's own authority rules; a subject can never substitute a looser arrangement for the one required.

## Motivation

Agents now hold credentials, operate accounts and move funds on behalf of people and organizations. The mechanisms that bound what they may do are maturing in several places at once: permission grants and delegations in accounts, policy verdicts bound to actions, identity and reputation registries, decision mechanisms and task escrows. What none of them records is the relation between them: in this task, on these resources, in this execution environment, which participants have accepted which safety requirements, in which version, relying on which evidence, enforced by which mechanism, and whether that acceptance can still be relied upon now.

Without that relation, four failures recur. A counterparty cannot tell before interacting which arrangement, if any, governs an agent, so it either trusts a label or asks for nothing. A safety requirement written into a document cannot be told apart from one that is actually enforced at an execution boundary, so promises and controls are mixed. A requirement that was accepted in one version is silently reinterpreted when its publisher releases a new one, so nobody knows which conditions were agreed. And when an arrangement is paused, revoked or breached, there is no shared way to say what stops, what continues, who was entitled to intervene and on what basis.

The framework answers each of these with a registry object rather than with a new control: the Safety Specification fixes what was agreed, the Safety Binding fixes who agreed to it for what, the lifecycle fixes how the agreement changes, and the check lets every consumer verify the relation at the moment it matters. Because the adopting account is always the sending account, the registry cannot be used to attribute an arrangement to someone who did not accept it; because every specification version is content-addressed and pinned, publishing a new version changes nothing for existing adopters; and because enforcement locations are declared, a consumer can distinguish an arrangement the subject merely promises to follow from one enforced by its account, its runtime, a custodian, a contract or a collective decision.

## Specification

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119) and [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174) when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.

### 1. Terminology

- **Subject**: the system or account whose behaviour a safety arrangement governs: an agent account, a registered agent identity, a swarm, an economic unit or any other referenceable system. A subject is referenced by a string and identified in the registry by its `subjectKey`.
- **Principal**: the party on whose behalf the subject acts. The principal is often, but not necessarily, the adopter.
- **Safety Specification** (specification): a versioned, content-addressed document of safety requirements and of the means by which they are enforced and evidenced (Section 4).
- **Publisher**: the account entitled to publish new versions of a specification and to transfer or renounce that entitlement.
- **Safety Binding** (binding): the on-chain record that an adopter adopts one version of a specification for one subject within one scope (Section 6).
- **Adopter**: the account that created a binding. The adopter is always the account that sent the creating transaction.
- **Scope**: the part of the world a binding applies to: a task, a resource, an execution environment, an account, or a general scope. A scope is a JSON object identified by its `scopeKey`.
- **Authority basis**: the external permission record (a wallet permission, a delegation, a membership record, a charter, tender terms, an operator agreement) that entitles the adopter to adopt for the subject and scope. The registry records a reference to it and never evaluates it.
- **Enforcement location**: where an arrangement is actually enforced: by the subject itself, by its account, by its runtime, by a resource custodian, by a contract or by a collective decision mechanism (Section 6.4).
- **Intervener**: an account other than the adopter that holds some lifecycle rights over a binding (Section 6.5).
- **Derived binding**: a binding that names a parent binding and is bounded by it (Section 9).
- **Requirement set**: a consumer's statement of which bindings must hold, combined by all-of, any-of or k-of (Section 10).
- **Consumer**: a contract, wallet, runtime, task or service that decides whether to admit or execute something on the strength of bindings.
- **Profile**: a versioned, content-addressed vocabulary for one kind of constraint, identified by a profile identifier (Section 12). Profiles defined by this ERC use the `asf:` namespace; extension profiles use the `x:` namespace.
- **Family**: one of eight groupings of profiles by the kind of safety problem they address (Section 12.1).

### 2. Conformance

A conforming deployment consists of one Specification Registry (Section 5) and one Binding Registry (Section 7). Both MUST implement [ERC-165](./eip-165.md) and MUST report the interface identifiers of Section 14. A Binding Registry MAY accept specifications held by any Specification Registry on the same chain that implements the interface of Section 5.

A conforming Safety Specification, binding parameters document, requirement set document or profile descriptor is a JSON document that satisfies the corresponding data model of Sections 4, 6, 10 and 12 and whose canonical hash (Section 3.1) equals the hash recorded on chain or cited by the record that refers to it.

A conforming consumer MUST implement the rules of Section 11.

Implementations MAY support only a subset of profiles. A consumer that encounters a profile or extension it does not understand, and that a specification marks as required, MUST treat the specification as not understood and MUST NOT act as if the requirement were satisfied (Section 13.3).

### 3. Identifiers and canonicalization

#### 3.1 Content hashes

The content hash of any document of this ERC is the Keccak-256 digest of the UTF-8 encoding of the document after canonicalization with the JSON Canonicalization Scheme ([RFC 8785](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8785)). This rule defines `contentHash` of a specification version, `paramsHash` of a binding, the descriptor hash of a profile and the document hash of a requirement set. Numbers that carry economic quantities MUST be encoded as decimal strings so that canonicalization never alters them.

#### 3.2 Subject and scope keys

`subjectKey` is the Keccak-256 digest of the UTF-8 encoding of the subject reference string. Account subjects use the reference `eip155:<chainId>:<address>` with the address in lowercase hexadecimal; other subjects use the reference form of the system that defines them (a CAIP-19 asset reference, a `did:` identifier, or a framework-specific URN). The registry treats the key as opaque.

`scopeKey` is the content hash (Section 3.1) of the scope object. The one-member object `{"ref": "<reference>"}` is the form that a contract can canonicalize without a JSON library; scope objects with more members are canonicalized off chain and passed as keys.

#### 3.3 Specification and binding identifiers

```text
specId    = keccak256(abi.encode(chainId, specificationRegistry, publisher, salt))
bindingId = keccak256(abi.encode(chainId, bindingRegistry, adopter, subjectKey, scopeKey,
                                 specificationRegistry, specId, version, salt))
```

Both identifiers bind the chain and the registry so that a record cannot be replayed elsewhere, and both bind the account that created the record so that nobody can occupy another account's identifier space.

#### 3.4 Profile, family and requirement identifiers

`profileId` and `familyId` are the Keccak-256 digests of the UTF-8 encoding of the identifier string (for example `asf:profile:authority/bounded-spend@1` and `asf:family:identity-authority`). `requirementId` is the Keccak-256 digest of the ABI encoding of the `RequirementSet` struct of Section 10 (Section 10.5).

### 4. Safety Specification

#### 4.1 Document

A Safety Specification is a JSON object with the following members. Members marked (R) are REQUIRED.

| Member | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `asf` (R) | string | Data model version. This document defines `"1.0"`. |
| `kind` (R) | string | MUST be `"safety-specification"`. |
| `name` (R) | string | Human-readable name. |
| `summary` | string | Descriptive only. |
| `publisher` | object | `ref` and `name` of the publisher. Informative; the on-chain publisher is authoritative. |
| `version` (R) | integer | MUST equal the registry version under which the document is published. |
| `protects` (R) | array | Protected interests: objects with `domain` (one of `assets-economic`, `information-privacy`, `control-rules`, `credit-eligibility`, `third-party-public`) and `description`. |
| `applicability` (R) | object | `subjects`, `tasks`, `resources`, `environments` (arrays of strings) and `notes`: what the specification is meant for. |
| `constraints` (R) | array | Constraint objects (Section 4.2). |
| `authority` (R) | object | `whoMayAdopt`, `whoMayEnforce`, `authorityBasis`: who is expected to adopt and enforce, and which kinds of external permission records a binding is expected to cite. |
| `evidence` (R) | array | Evidence objects: `id`, `kind` (`attestation`, `proof`, `audit`, `runtime-log`, `test-result`, `declaration`, `decision`), `source`, `binds` (which of subject, instance, version, action, rule-version, purpose, state and validity the evidence is bound to) and `validity`. |
| `enforcement` (R) | object | `channels`: the enforcement channels (Section 4.3). |
| `failureHandling` (R) | object | `unknown`, `denied`, `dependencyFailure`, each one of `refuse`, `wait`, `handoff`, `degrade`, and `notes`. |
| `remedies` (R) | object | `suspensionModes` (subset of the restriction vocabulary of Section 8.2), `recovery`, `dispute`, `compensation`. |
| `profiles` (R) | object | `required`: profile identifiers a consumer MUST understand to rely on the specification; `optional`: profiles that MAY be ignored. |
| `limitations` | array | Assumptions and gaps the publisher states. |
| `extensions` | object | Extension members (Section 13). |
| `metadata` | object | Free-form, non-normative. |

#### 4.2 Constraints

A constraint object has the members `id` (R), `profile` (R, a profile identifier), `family` (R, Section 12.1), `statement` (R), `parameters`, `scope` (R) and `enforcedBy` (R). `scope` states what the constraint is evaluated over: `action` (one action), `state` (the current state), `trajectory` (the history of the subject within a window), `group` (the combined behaviour of several subjects) or `relation` (a relation between trajectories, for example that a secret did not influence what the outside world could observe). `enforcedBy` lists the identifiers of the channels (Section 4.3) that check the constraint; every identifier MUST name a channel of the same document.

A constraint's `parameters` are defaults or fixed values; a binding instantiates the remaining parameters under the constraint's profile (Section 6.2).

#### 4.3 Enforcement channels

A channel object has the members `id` (R), `description` (R), `mechanism` (`kind`, `ref`, `version`, `notes`), `location` (R, one of `self`, `account`, `runtime`, `custodian`, `contract`, `collective`), `coverage` (R, `full` or `partial`) and `knownBypasses` (array of strings). A specification whose every channel has location `self` describes a promise, not a control; consumers decide what that is worth.

#### 4.4 Versions

A specification version is immutable once published. A correction, a tightening or a loosening is a new version. Publishing a new version MUST NOT change any binding of an earlier version; adopters move by creating new bindings (Section 8.4). A publisher MAY mark a version deprecated; the mark is informative and MUST NOT change the state or the evaluation of any binding.

### 5. Specification Registry interface

```solidity
// SPDX-License-Identifier: CC0-1.0
pragma solidity ^0.8.20;

struct SpecificationVersion {
    bytes32 contentHash;        // keccak256(UTF-8(JCS(document)))
    string locator;             // where the document can be fetched; mutable, the hash is not
    uint64 publishedAt;
    bool deprecated;            // publisher's informative signal; never changes a binding
    bytes32[] requiredProfiles; // profile identifiers a consumer MUST understand
}

interface IASFSpecificationRegistry /* is IERC165 */ {
    event SpecificationRegistered(bytes32 indexed specId, address indexed publisher, bytes32 salt);
    event SpecificationVersionPublished(bytes32 indexed specId, uint32 indexed version, bytes32 contentHash,
        string locator, bytes32[] requiredProfiles);
    event LocatorUpdated(bytes32 indexed specId, uint32 indexed version, string locator);
    event VersionDeprecated(bytes32 indexed specId, uint32 indexed version, bytes32 reasonRef);
    event PublisherChanged(bytes32 indexed specId, address indexed previousPublisher, address indexed newPublisher);

    /// @notice Registers a specification under msg.sender and publishes its version 1.
    function registerSpecification(bytes32 salt, bytes32 contentHash, string calldata locator,
        bytes32[] calldata requiredProfiles) external returns (bytes32 specId);
    /// @notice Publishes the next version. Only the publisher. Reverts for frozen specifications.
    function publishVersion(bytes32 specId, bytes32 contentHash, string calldata locator,
        bytes32[] calldata requiredProfiles) external returns (uint32 version);
    /// @notice Replaces the locator of a version; the content hash is untouched. Only the publisher.
    function setLocator(bytes32 specId, uint32 version, string calldata locator) external;
    /// @notice Marks a version deprecated. Informative: bindings are unaffected. Only the publisher.
    function deprecateVersion(bytes32 specId, uint32 version, bytes32 reasonRef) external;
    /// @notice Transfers or renounces publishing control. address(0) freezes the specification.
    function setPublisher(bytes32 specId, address newPublisher) external;

    function computeSpecId(address publisher, bytes32 salt) external view returns (bytes32);
    function specificationExists(bytes32 specId) external view returns (bool);
    function publisherOf(bytes32 specId) external view returns (address);
    function latestVersion(bytes32 specId) external view returns (uint32);
    function versionOf(bytes32 specId, uint32 version) external view returns (SpecificationVersion memory);
    function supportsInterface(bytes4 interfaceId) external view returns (bool);
}
```

Registry rules:

1. `specId` MUST be computed as in Section 3.3 with `msg.sender` as the publisher. Registering an existing `specId` MUST revert.
2. Versions MUST be numbered sequentially from 1 and MUST be immutable once published. `registerSpecification` and `publishVersion` MUST revert when `contentHash` is zero.
3. `publishVersion`, `setLocator`, `deprecateVersion` and `setPublisher` MUST revert unless `msg.sender` is the current publisher, and MUST revert for a frozen specification (publisher `address(0)`). A frozen specification remains readable and adoptable.
4. `versionOf` MUST revert for an unknown specification or version, so that a binding registry can rely on a successful return.

The registry does not validate that the document at `locator` hashes to `contentHash` or that the document satisfies Section 4; consumers verify both before relying on a version.

### 6. Safety Binding

#### 6.1 Meaning

A binding states: the adopter adopts version `version` of specification `specId` held by `specRegistry`, with content `contentHash`, for the subject `subjectKey` within the scope `scopeKey`, instantiated by the parameters document `paramsHash`, on the authority basis `authorityRef`, enforced at the locations in `enforcement`, from `validFrom` until `validUntil`, with the interveners listed, and (optionally) as a derivation of `parentBindingId`.

Four things a binding does not state, and that consumers MUST establish separately, are the following:

1. That the adopter was entitled to adopt for the subject and the scope. The registry records the adopter faithfully and records the reference to the authority basis; whether that basis exists, covers the subject, the scope and the period, and names the adopter, is verified by the consumer against the permission source it already trusts (Section 11.1).
2. That any particular action of the subject is permitted. Permission is decided by the permission and policy mechanisms the specification names.
3. That the arrangement is actually enforced. The binding declares where enforcement is claimed to happen; the consumer decides which locations it accepts.
4. That the subject is safe. A satisfied binding means that an attributable party accepted a stated arrangement whose enforcement is declared, and that this acceptance is currently in force.

#### 6.2 Binding parameters document

The off-chain parameters document is a JSON object with the members `asf` (R, `"1.0"`), `kind` (R, `"safety-binding"`), `adopter` (R: `ref`, `role`), `subject` (R: `ref`, `key`, `kind`), `scope` (R: `object`, `key`), `specification` (R: `registry`, `specId`, `version`, `contentHash`, `locator`, `name`), `parameters` (R: an object keyed by profile identifier whose values instantiate the constraints of the specification), `authorityBasis` (R: an array of `kind`, `ref`, `description`), `mechanisms` (R: an array of `channel`, `mechanism`, `location`, `coverage`), `evidence`, `derived` (`parentBindingId`, `narrowing`), `validity` (`from`, `until`), `interveners` (array of `ref`, `rights`, `role`), `extensions` and `metadata`. The binding's `paramsHash` is the content hash of this document. `authorityRef` SHOULD be the content hash of the `authorityBasis` array; a binding MAY instead carry an opaque reference defined by the authority basis itself. The on-chain `enforcement` bit set SHOULD be the union of the locations listed under `mechanisms`.

Every profile listed as required by the adopted specification version MUST have an entry under `parameters`.

#### 6.3 Pinning

A binding pins one specification version by (`specRegistry`, `specId`, `version`, `contentHash`). The registry MUST verify at creation that `specRegistry` implements the interface of Section 5 and that the content hash it reports for the version equals the pinned one. After creation the pin never changes: a later version, a deprecation, a changed locator or a frozen publisher leaves the binding exactly as adopted.

#### 6.4 Enforcement locations

The `enforcement` member of a binding is a bit set over the following locations, ordered from the least to the most independent of the subject:

| Bit | Location | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `self` | the subject promises to comply |
| 2 | `account` | the subject's account or wallet enforces (permissions, session keys, modules) |
| 4 | `runtime` | the execution environment enforces (sandbox, loader, monitors outside the subject's authority) |
| 8 | `custodian` | a custodian of the resource or an API enforces |
| 16 | `contract` | an on-chain contract or guard enforces |
| 32 | `collective` | a collective decision mechanism enforces |

A requirement MAY demand that given locations are declared (Section 10.2). A declaration is a commitment of the adopter, attributable to it; it is not a proof that the mechanism works.

#### 6.5 Interveners

A binding MAY name up to sixteen interveners, each with a rights bit set over `RIGHT_SUSPEND` (1), `RIGHT_RESUME` (2) and `RIGHT_TERMINATE` (4). The adopter holds all three rights. The set of interveners is fixed at creation; changing it means terminating the binding and creating a new one, so that an intervener's rights cannot be quietly removed while the binding appears unchanged. Interveners give the responsibility layer of a safety arrangement a hook: a guardian, a decision mechanism's executor or a task's acceptance authority can pause an arrangement with a basis recorded in the binding itself, and nobody else can.

#### 6.6 Validity

`validFrom` and `validUntil` are unix timestamps; `validUntil` is exclusive and zero means open-ended. A binding exists only from its `createdAt`; the registry records a `validFrom` earlier than `createdAt` as declared but a binding is never satisfied for a time before `createdAt`. A later registration cannot prove an earlier acceptance.

### 7. Binding Registry interface

```solidity
// SPDX-License-Identifier: CC0-1.0
pragma solidity ^0.8.20;

enum BindingState { None, Active, Suspended, Terminated }
enum Restriction { None, NoNewRisk, Shrink, EssentialOnly, DeriskOnly, FullStop }

    AdopterMismatch, SubjectMismatch, ScopeMismatch, EnforcementMismatch, ParentNotSatisfied,
    SpecRegistryUnavailable }
enum Combinator { All, Any, KOf }

struct Intervener { address account; uint8 rights; }

struct BindingInput {
    bytes32 subjectKey; bytes32 scopeKey;
    address specRegistry; bytes32 specId; uint32 version; bytes32 contentHash;
    bytes32 paramsHash; string paramsLocator;
    bytes32 authorityRef; uint16 enforcement;
    uint64 validFrom; uint64 validUntil;
    bytes32 parentBindingId; bytes32 salt;

    address adopter; bytes32 subjectKey; bytes32 scopeKey;

        Restriction restriction, address actor, bytes32 reasonRef);
    event BindingParamsLocatorUpdated(bytes32 indexed bindingId, string locator);

    function adopt(BindingInput calldata input) external returns (bytes32 bindingId);
    function suspend(bytes32 bindingId, Restriction restriction, bytes32 reasonRef) external;
    function resume(bytes32 bindingId, bytes32 reasonRef) external;
    function terminate(bytes32 bindingId, bytes32 reasonRef) external;
    function setParamsLocator(bytes32 bindingId, string calldata locator) external;

    function computeBindingId(address adopter, BindingInput calldata input) external view returns (bytes32);
    function bindingOf(bytes32 bindingId) external view returns (Binding memory);
    function intervenersOf(bytes32 bindingId) external view returns (Intervener[] memory);
    function rightsOf(bytes32 bindingId, address account) external view returns (uint8);

    function countBySubject(bytes32 subjectKey, address adopter) external view returns (uint256);

        external view returns (bytes32[] memory);

        external view returns (Verdict verdict, Reason reason, Restriction restriction);
    function check(RequirementSet calldata requirement, bytes32[] calldata bindingIds)
```

Registry rules:

1. The adopter of a binding MUST be `msg.sender` of `adopt`. `bindingId` MUST be computed as in Section 3.3. `adopt` MUST revert when a record with that identifier exists in any state, including `Terminated`: a binding identifier is never recreated.
2. `adopt` MUST revert when `specRegistry` has no code or does not report the interface identifier of Section 14 for the Specification Registry, when `versionOf(specId, version)` reverts, or when the reported content hash is zero or differs from `input.contentHash`.
3. `adopt` MUST revert when `validUntil` is non-zero and not later than the current block time, or not later than a non-zero `validFrom`.
4. `adopt` MUST apply the derived-binding rules of Section 9.1 when `parentBindingId` is non-zero.
5. `adopt` MUST revert for an intervener with a zero account, for an intervener equal to the adopter, for duplicate interveners, for a rights value of zero or above 7, and for more than sixteen interveners.
6. A new binding MUST be `Active` with restriction `None`, MUST record `createdAt`, and MUST be appended to the index of (`subjectKey`, adopter) and to the index of (`scopeKey`, adopter). The adopter is part of every index key.
7. Lifecycle transitions MUST follow Section 8. `setParamsLocator` MUST revert unless `msg.sender` is the adopter and the binding is not `Terminated`; it never changes `paramsHash`.
8. `checkBinding`, `check` and `requirementId` MUST follow Section 10.

### 8. Lifecycle

#### 8.1 States

A binding is `Active`, `Suspended` or `Terminated`; `None` means the record does not exist. `Active -> Suspended` requires `RIGHT_SUSPEND` and a restriction other than `None`; `Suspended -> Active` requires `RIGHT_RESUME` and clears the restriction; `Active -> Terminated` and `Suspended -> Terminated` require `RIGHT_TERMINATE`. `Terminated` is terminal. Every other transition MUST revert. Every transition MUST emit `BindingStateChanged` with the acting account and the caller's `reasonRef`, an opaque reference to the evidence or decision behind the transition.

#### 8.2 Restrictions

A suspension names one of five restrictions, which consumers interpret through the profiles of the adopted specification:

| Value | Restriction | Meaning for the subject while suspended |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `NoNewRisk` | no action that adds exposure, obligations or new counterparties |
| 2 | `Shrink` | only actions that reduce the current scope, exposure or authority |
| 3 | `EssentialOnly` | only the actions a profile lists as necessary to keep a service alive |
| 4 | `DeriskOnly` | only unwinding, repayment, cancellation and recovery actions |
| 5 | `FullStop` | nothing under the binding may proceed |

A specification's `remedies.suspensionModes` lists which restrictions its adopters and interveners are expected to use. The registry does not enforce that list; consumers MAY treat a suspension under an unlisted restriction as `FullStop`.

#### 8.3 What a transition does and does not do

Suspending or terminating a binding changes the verdict of every check that depends on it. It does not by itself revoke a wallet permission, stop a running process, reverse a completed transfer, retract disclosed information or cancel an external commitment; those effects follow from the mechanisms the specification names, under their own authority. Resuming a binding does not restore a permission that was revoked in the meantime. Terminating a binding does not erase incidents, debts, completed effects or existing obligations, and the record remains readable.

#### 8.4 Moving between versions

An adopter that wants a newer specification version creates a new binding pinned to it, presents the new binding to its consumers and terminates the old one when the consumers have switched. Two bindings of one adopter for the same subject and scope MAY be `Active` at once; a requirement decides which versions it admits.

### 9. Derived bindings and attenuation

#### 9.1 Registry rules

A binding whose `parentBindingId` is non-zero is derived. At creation the registry MUST verify that the parent exists and is `Active`, that the parent pins the same `specRegistry`, `specId` and `version`, that the child's `validUntil` is non-zero and not later than the parent's whenever the parent's `validUntil` is non-zero, and that the parent's depth is below eight. The child's `depth` is the parent's plus one. The child's adopter, subject and scope MAY differ from the parent's: a member of a group derives its own binding from the group's, an operator derives one for a sub-agent from the principal's.

At evaluation (Section 10.1) a derived binding is satisfied only if every ancestor is `Active` and inside its validity window. A suspended or terminated ancestor makes every descendant not satisfied with reason `ParentNotSatisfied`, while the descendants' own records stay untouched.

#### 9.2 Parameter attenuation

A derived binding MUST NOT be wider than its parent. For every profile whose descriptor declares a partial order (Section 12.2), the child's parameters MUST be not wider than the parent's under that order; for every profile without a declared order, the child's parameters MUST equal the parent's. The child's parameters document records the relation per profile under `derived.narrowing` with its basis. The registry cannot evaluate parameters; consumers that rely on a derived binding verify the narrowing against the two parameters documents, and MUST treat a child whose narrowing cannot be established as not satisfied, never as satisfied by default.

### 10. Requirement sets and the check

#### 10.1 Clause evaluation

`checkBinding(bindingId, clause)` evaluates one binding against one clause at the current block time, in the following order, and returns the first failing reason:

1. `NoBinding`: `bindingId` is zero or no record exists.
2. `Terminated`, `Suspended`: the record's state. For `Suspended` the record's restriction is returned.
3. `NotYetValid`: the current time is before the later of `validFrom` and `createdAt`. `Expired`: `validUntil` is non-zero and not later than the current time.
4. `SpecMismatch`: the clause's `specRegistry` or `specId` differs from the binding's.
5. `VersionMismatch`: the clause's `versions` list is non-empty and does not contain the binding's version.
6. `AdopterMismatch`, `SubjectMismatch`, `ScopeMismatch`: the clause's `adopter`, `subjectKey` or `scopeKey` is non-zero and differs from the binding's.
7. `EnforcementMismatch`: a bit set in the clause's `enforcement` is not set in the binding's.
8. `ParentNotSatisfied`: an ancestor is not `Active` or is outside its validity window; the ancestor's restriction is returned.
9. `SpecRegistryUnavailable`: the pinned specification registry no longer reports its interface identifier. This is the only outcome with verdict `Unknown`; every other failure is `NotSatisfied`.

A binding that passes every step is `Satisfied` with reason `None`.

#### 10.2 Clauses

A clause names the specification registry and identifier (both REQUIRED and non-zero), an explicit list of admitted versions (empty admits every version; ranges are deliberately not expressible), an adopter, a subject key and a scope key (zero is a wildcard for each), and an enforcement bit set (every set bit MUST be declared by the binding). A requirement that wants a subject's own commitment sets `adopter` to the subject's account; a requirement that accepts a principal's adoption for its agent sets `adopter` to the principal and `subjectKey` to the agent.

#### 10.3 Combinators

`check(requirement, bindingIds)` evaluates `bindingIds[i]` against `clauses[i]` (the two arrays MUST have equal length; `bytes32(0)` means that no candidate is supplied) and combines the clause verdicts:

- `All`: `Satisfied` when every clause is satisfied; `Unknown` when no clause is not satisfied and at least one is unknown; otherwise `NotSatisfied`.
- `Any`: `Satisfied` when at least one clause is satisfied; `Unknown` when none is satisfied and at least one is unknown; otherwise `NotSatisfied`.
- `KOf`: with `k` between 1 and the number of clauses, `Satisfied` when at least `k` clauses are satisfied; `NotSatisfied` when the satisfied and unknown clauses together are fewer than `k`; otherwise `Unknown`.

`Unknown` MUST NOT be treated as satisfied by any consumer. Clause verdicts are never combined into a score.

#### 10.4 Malformed requirements

`check` and `requirementId` MUST revert for an empty clause list, for a clause with a zero `specRegistry` or `specId`, for `KOf` with `k` of zero or above the clause count, and for `All` or `Any` with a non-zero `k`.

#### 10.5 Requirement identifier and document

`requirementId` is the Keccak-256 digest of the ABI encoding of the `RequirementSet` struct, with enumerations encoded as `uint8`. A resource that publishes what it requires SHOULD publish the requirement set document (members `asf`, `kind` = `"requirement-set"`, `name`, `combinator` (`all`, `any`, `k-of`), `k`, `clauses` with `role`, `specification`, `versions`, `adopter`, `subject`, `scope`, `enforcement`, and `metadata`). Role-relative values (`"self"`) in the document are resolved by the consumer when it builds the struct for a concrete participant.

### 11. Consumer rules

#### 11.1 Authority verification

Before relying on a binding, a consumer MUST verify, against the permission source it already trusts, that the adopter was entitled to adopt for the subject and the scope at the time of the action, and that the authority basis covers the current task and period. The binding's `authorityRef` tells the consumer where to look; it proves nothing by itself. A resolver provided by the author of the binding that always answers favourably is not a permission source.

#### 11.2 Which bindings are required

Which bindings a resource requires, in which versions, from which adopters and with which enforcement locations, is determined by the resource's own authority rules (its terms, charter, permission policy or the requirement set it published). A subject MUST NOT be allowed to substitute a different binding for a required one, and a consumer MUST NOT accept a binding of a looser specification, an unlisted version, another adopter or weaker enforcement than required.

#### 11.3 Check at the time of effect

A consumer MUST evaluate the requirement at the time an effect is produced, not only when a session or a batch is validated: a binding suspended between validation and execution MUST stop the execution. When a required binding is not satisfied, the normal execution path MUST NOT fall back to an unchecked path. Under a `Suspended` verdict the consumer MAY continue the actions that the restriction and the specification's profiles allow (for example repayment and cancellation under `DeriskOnly`) and MUST stop everything else.

#### 11.4 Unknown and dependency failure

A verdict of `Unknown`, a specification document that cannot be fetched or does not hash to its pin, a required profile the consumer does not understand, or an authority basis that cannot be verified are all handled according to the specification's `failureHandling`, and never as a pass. Refusing, waiting, handing the decision to an authorized party and degrading to a pre-accepted restricted mode are the admissible responses.

#### 11.5 Separate states

Consumers MUST keep four facts separate in their own records: that a binding is registered, that the requirement is satisfied, that an action was authorized and sent, and that its outcome is known. An action whose outcome is unknown MUST be reconciled before any retry; a failed outcome MUST NOT be recorded over a successful one.

#### 11.6 Multi-party acceptance

Where several parties must accept an arrangement (a principal, an operator, a task publisher, a data controller, the members of a group), each party adopts separately or expresses acceptance through the precise confirmation mechanism of the protocol that governs its participation. One party's binding never implies another party's acceptance.

### 12. Profiles

#### 12.1 Families

Profiles are grouped into eight families, used as the `family` member of constraints and descriptors. The grouping is practical; a profile belongs to one family, and the families are not claimed to be disjoint as safety problems.

| Family | Addresses |
|---|---|
| `identity-authority` | who acts, for whom, with which delegated and re-delegated authority, for how long |
| `information-privacy` | which inputs are trusted, what may be read, disclosed, retained or used for another purpose |
| `execution-supply-chain` | whether models, programs, tools, contracts and environments run as specified; pinned dependencies; isolation |
| `behavior-goals` | task goals, prohibited behaviours, required review, capability limits, protection of monitoring |
| `assets-economic` | per-action and cumulative limits, exposure, settlement uncertainty |
| `multi-agent-system` | shared budgets, recursive delegation, conflicts, common-cause failures, cascades |
| `human-public` | protected persons and third parties, purpose limits, value rules, appeal and review |
| `governance-accountability` | sources of jurisdiction, incident evidence, pre-accepted consequences, intervention, recovery and compensation |

#### 12.2 Profile descriptors

A profile is described by a content-addressed descriptor with the members `asf` (R), `kind` (R, `"profile-descriptor"`), `identifier` (R), `family` (R), `summary` (R), `evaluatesOver` (subset of `action`, `state`, `trajectory`, `group`, `relation`), `parameterSchema` (R, the JSON Schema of the parameters a binding supplies), `partialOrder` (R: `defined` and `rule`, whether and how "not wider than" between two parameter sets is decided), `verification` (R: an array of `method`, `location`, `inputs`, `resultMeaning`, `trustAssumptions`), `limitations` and `metadata`. A descriptor with `partialOrder.defined` false makes derived bindings under that profile keep their parameters equal to the parent's (Section 9.2).

Profile identifiers have the form `asf:profile:<family-short-name>/<name>@<version>` for profiles defined by this ERC and `x:<namespace>:<name>@<version>` for extensions. The descriptors of six initial profiles accompany this proposal as informative examples: bounded spending authority, untrusted input, pinned dependencies, shared budget, monitoring integrity and intervention.

#### 12.3 Required and optional profiles

A specification's `profiles.required` lists the profiles a consumer MUST understand to rely on it; the same identifiers are recorded on chain as `requiredProfiles` of the version. A binding MUST supply parameters for every required profile. A binding MAY add or tighten conditions only under profiles the adopted specification lists; adding a new mandatory interpretation needs a new specification version and a new adoption.

### 13. Extensibility

#### 13.1 Namespaces

Identifiers in the `asf:` namespace are defined by this ERC and its revisions. Extension identifiers use the `x:` namespace followed by a namespace of the defining party. Unregistered `asf:` identifiers MUST NOT be used.

#### 13.2 Extension members

Every document of this ERC MAY carry an `extensions` object keyed by identifier. An extension member with `required` true is part of the document's meaning; one without is informative.

#### 13.3 Unsupported extensions and profiles

A consumer that does not understand a required extension member or a required profile MUST treat the document as not understood and MUST NOT act as if the corresponding requirement were satisfied. Optional members MAY be ignored and MUST NOT change permissions, constraints or failure handling.

#### 13.4 Revisions

Later revisions of this ERC MAY add restrictions, reasons, enforcement locations, families and document members. Added enumeration values MUST be appended; added members MUST be optional; the identifier derivations of Section 3 MUST NOT change.

### 14. Interface identifiers

The interface identifiers are computed as the XOR of the selectors of all functions declared in Sections 5 and 7 (excluding `supportsInterface`), following ERC-165:

```text
IASFSpecificationRegistry  0x9bddde77
IASFBindingRegistry        0x9659b640
```

## Rationale

**The adoption relation is the object.** Permission grants, delegations, policy verdicts, identity registries, decision mechanisms and task escrows each answer one question: who the subject is, what it was granted, whether a proof holds, whether an action passes a policy, how an executor behaves. The unanswered question is relational: in this scope, which participants accepted which arrangement, in which version, enforced where, and is that acceptance in force now. Making the relation a registry object with a source, a version, a scope and a lifecycle is a smaller addition than any new control, and it is the one addition that lets the existing controls be composed and relied upon by third parties. A registry of safety documents alone, or a registry of policies alone, or one more allow/deny interface, would not have been a contribution; several proposals already provide each of those.

**The adopter is the sender.** An open registry invites records that say "a well-known agent adopts my arrangement". Such a record can prove only that some account published the statement. Deriving the adopter from `msg.sender`, putting the adopter into every identifier and every index key, and leaving the entitlement to adopt to the consumer's own permission source are the three rules that make the registry unforgeable in the only sense a registry can be: it never attributes a statement to an account that did not make it.

**Pinned versions, no automatic upgrade.** A publisher's belief that version 2 is safer than version 1 is not consent by the parties who accepted version 1. Bindings pin a content hash and move only by new adoption. Deprecation exists so that a publisher can speak without being able to change anything.

**Registry-level check only.** The registry evaluates existence, state, validity, pin, adopter, subject, scope, declared enforcement and the ancestor chain. It never evaluates a policy, because policy evaluation already has standards and because a registry that evaluated policies would become a policy engine with a privileged position. What the check adds is a single cheap call that a guard, an account module or a task contract can make at the moment of effect, with a reason that wallets can show to people, and a verdict that distinguishes refusal from ignorance.

**Unknown is a verdict.** A dependency that cannot be queried, a document that cannot be fetched and a profile that is not understood are not refusals, and they are not passes. Collapsing them into either produces wrong retries, wrong refunds and silent bypasses. The check therefore returns `Unknown` where the registry itself cannot decide, the combinators propagate it without ever counting it as satisfied, and the consumer rules route it to the specification's own failure handling.

**Derived bindings and the attenuation rule.** Re-delegation that silently widens authority is the gap that surveys of agent-mandate proposals and of workload-identity standards both identify. The framework addresses the decidable part on chain (same pinned version, validity inside the parent's, bounded depth, ancestors must hold) and the profile-dependent part off chain under a precise rule: not wider under the profile's declared order, equal where no order is declared, and never satisfied by default when the relation cannot be established. Declaring the undecidable part undecidable is what keeps the rule honest.

**Five restrictions rather than one pause.** Stopping everything is sometimes the most harmful response: a trading agent that cannot repay or cancel while suspended, a service that cannot keep an essential function alive. The vocabulary of five restrictions lets a suspension say what continues, and lets profiles map their actions onto it, so that completability is designed in advance rather than improvised during an incident.

**Interveners with explicit rights.** Who may pause an arrangement is itself a safety question; a danger label must not create power. Interveners and their rights are fixed in the binding at creation, the adopter always keeps all rights, and every transition records the actor and a reference to its basis. A guardian, a decision mechanism or an acceptance authority can therefore act on an arrangement precisely to the extent the adopter granted.

**Enforcement locations are declared.** "The agent will respect the limit" and "the account enforces the limit" are different arrangements. Recording the location bit set lets a requirement demand enforcement at a given independence from the subject and lets consumers see at once when a specification is a promise. The declaration is attributable, not verified; verification belongs to evidence and to the mechanisms themselves.

**Eight families, one analysis.** The families group profiles the way practitioners group safety problems; they are allowed to overlap as problems because nothing on chain depends on their disjointness. The analysis behind the framework distinguishes three independent axes (where in the chain of a delegated action a relation breaks, which protected domain is affected, and what the source of the risk is); the families are the practical projection of that analysis and the protected domains of Section 4.1 are its second axis.

**Why a registry and not a token.** An adoption is a relation with a lifecycle, not a transferable whole. Nothing is gained by making it transferable, and a transferable adoption would be a forgery primitive.

**Why "safety" in the name.** The framework governs arrangements whose content ranges from access control to harm avoidance, controllability and assurance. Safety is the umbrella term under which operators, laboratories and regulators already write specifications and cases of exactly this shape; the Abstract defines the breadth so that readers from the security tradition lose nothing.

## Backwards Compatibility

No backward compatibility issues were found. The framework introduces two new registries and four document kinds and changes no existing interface. Bindings reference existing accounts, identities, permissions, policies, tasks and groups by their own identifiers and never modify them.

### Canonicalization

The vectors file `canonicalization.json` carries a minimal specification document, its JCS string and its content hash; a conforming implementation reproduces the hash from the JCS string.

### Requirement identifier

The vectors file `requirement.json` carries a three-clause `KOf` requirement (`k` = 2), its ABI encoding under the type `tuple(uint8,uint16,tuple(address,bytes32,uint32[],address,bytes32,bytes32,uint16)[])` and its `requirementId`; `requirementId(requirement)` of a conforming registry returns that value.

### Registry behaviour

1. `registerSpecification` with a zero content hash reverts; registering the same salt twice reverts; `publishVersion`, `setLocator`, `deprecateVersion` and `setPublisher` by an account other than the publisher revert; every write to a frozen specification reverts while its versions stay readable.
2. Publishing version 2 leaves version 1's content hash unchanged; a binding pinned to version 1 keeps `version` 1 and fails a clause that admits only version 2 with `VersionMismatch`, while a clause admitting versions 1 and 2 is satisfied.
3. `adopt` through a `specRegistry` that does not report the interface, or for an unknown version, or with a content hash that differs from the registry's, reverts.
4. `adopt` with the same input twice reverts; after suspension and after termination it still reverts; a new salt yields a new binding.
5. `adopt` with `validUntil` equal to the current time, or earlier than `validFrom`, reverts; a `validFrom` thirty days in the past is recorded while `createdAt` is the block time.
6. Interveners: a zero account, the adopter itself, duplicate accounts, rights 0 or 8, and seventeen entries each revert. A guardian granted only `RIGHT_SUSPEND` can suspend and can neither resume nor terminate; a stranger can do nothing; the adopter can do everything.
7. `suspend` with restriction `None` reverts; `resume` of an `Active` binding reverts; every transition of a `Terminated` binding reverts; `setParamsLocator` of a `Terminated` binding reverts and otherwise changes the locator but not the hash.
8. Derived bindings: a parent that is `Suspended`, unknown, pins another version, or has a shorter validity than the child, and a chain deeper than eight, each make `adopt` revert; a chain of depth eight is accepted. Suspending the root makes a grandchild `NotSatisfied` with `ParentNotSatisfied` and the root's restriction; resuming restores it; terminating the root fails the child while the child's own record stays `Active`.
9. `checkBinding` returns `NoBinding` for a zero or unknown identifier, `Suspended` with the restriction, `Terminated`, `NotYetValid` before the window, `Satisfied` at `validFrom`, `Expired` at `validUntil`, and the mismatches of Section 10.1 in that order; an enforcement requirement that is a subset of the declared set is satisfied.
10. A specification registry that stops reporting its interface turns `Satisfied` into `Unknown` with `SpecRegistryUnavailable`; `All` and `Any` over that clause return `Unknown`; `KOf` with `k` = 2 over one satisfied and one unknown clause returns `Unknown`, with `k` = 1 returns `Satisfied`.
11. `requirementId` and `check` revert for an empty clause list, a non-zero `k` under `All`, `k` of 0 or above the clause count under `KOf`, a zero `specId`, and for candidate and clause arrays of different lengths. `All` fails on one suspended clause; `Any` passes on one satisfied clause; `KOf` 2-of-3 passes with two satisfied and fails with one.

### Consumer scenarios

12. Wallet path: an account whose operator may act only while the principal's binding is satisfied executes while `Active`; after a guardian suspends under `DeriskOnly`, calls that add exposure revert and the listed de-risking calls still go through; under `FullStop` even those revert; after resumption execution resumes; after termination nothing goes through; a binding that expires closes the path without any transaction; a third party's binding for the same agent fails with `AdopterMismatch`; a binding declaring only `self` enforcement fails a requirement for `account` enforcement with `EnforcementMismatch`; the account exposes no path that skips the check.
13. Task path: a tender whose acceptance authority requires the fulfiller's own binding accepts and pays a fulfiller that holds one through the kernel's real acceptance call, rejects a fulfiller without one and releases the reservation, rejects a fulfiller whose binding was suspended between submission and judgment, rejects a binding adopted by someone else for the fulfiller, and when the verdict is `Unknown` neither accepts nor rejects, leaving the kernel's own judgment clock in force.

## Security Considerations

**Forged adoption claims.** Anyone can create a binding that names any subject and any scope. The record proves only that its adopter made the statement. Consumers that present or rely on bindings must key everything by adopter, as the registry's indexes do, and must verify the adopter's entitlement against a permission source of their own choosing. A user interface that shows "agent X adopted arrangement Y" without showing who adopted it on X's behalf misleads its users.

**Promises presented as controls.** A binding declaring enforcement only at the `self` location is a promise. Requirements that matter should demand account, runtime, custodian, contract or collective enforcement, and consumers should read `coverage` and `knownBypasses` of the specification's channels. Declared locations are attributable commitments, not verified facts; evidence and the mechanisms themselves carry the verification.

**Suspension is not revocation.** Suspending a binding stops consumers that check it; it does not touch the permissions, keys, processes or commitments behind the arrangement. Operators should pair a suspension with the revocations the specification names, and consumers should not assume that a suspended binding has made the subject inert.

**Validation-time staleness.** A requirement checked when a session or batch is validated can be stale when the effect lands. Consumers must check at the time of effect; the reference consumers do so on every call.

**Ancestor takeover and griefing.** Anyone may create a binding derived from a public parent; this restricts the child, never the parent, and the child's adopter is attributable, so no harm reaches the parent. A parent's adopter or intervener can, by suspending the parent, stop every descendant; parties deriving from a parent accept that dependency knowingly, and a requirement that must not depend on a parent should require root bindings.

**Index spam.** Indexes are keyed by adopter, so a spammer fills only its own index. Consumers that scan another account's index bound the scan, as the reference adapter does.

**Document availability and integrity.** Content lives off chain behind locators. A document that cannot be fetched or does not hash to its pin makes the binding unverifiable, not satisfied; publishers should use durable content-addressed storage and adopters should keep copies of what they adopted.

**Specification registry as a dependency.** A binding pins a specification registry. If that registry stops answering, checks turn `Unknown`, which consumers route to the specification's failure handling. Deploying the two registries together and treating the specification registry as immutable infrastructure keeps this dependency inert.

**Intervener key compromise.** An intervener's key can suspend or, if granted, terminate a binding. Rights should be granted narrowly, interveners should be collectively governed accounts where the stakes warrant it, and the restriction vocabulary limits what a hostile suspension can achieve to stopping, never to widening.

**Privacy of arrangements.** Specifications and binding parameters can disclose business relationships and operational details. Publishers and adopters may keep sensitive members as commitments and disclose them to selected parties while keeping the canonical hash on chain; a consumer that cannot read a document treats it as unverified.

**Time.** Validity windows and suspensions depend on block time. Consumers should allow for the latency between a decision to suspend and its inclusion, and specifications should set failure handling for the interval.
