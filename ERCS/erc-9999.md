## Abstract

This proposal defines the AI Cell Completeness Framework (ACCF). An AI unit, which may be an agent, a robot or another independently acting AI system, is cell-complete when, under a declared boundary, declared duties, a committed configuration, a named profile and at a stated time, it meets three sets of requirements together: infrastructure requirements (it can keep running and be maintained), protocol requirements (it can be identified, dealt with under rules and held to its commitments) and economic requirements (its own activity pays for its existence with a surplus). The same requirements cover four lifecycle capabilities: derivation, migration and succession; interaction; repair and upgrade; composition into groups. A unit in that state is called an AI Cell.

The framework fixes five objects (Unit Descriptor, Cell Manifest, Completeness Profile, Completeness Statement, Transition Record), a set of Baseline Requirements that no profile claiming them may weaken, and the rule by which a consumer derives the state from attributable statements under its own stated policy. Completeness is never stored as a flag. It is computed from statements that expire, that can be revoked, and that stop applying when the part of the unit they depend on changes. The framework also fixes what does and does not pass through a transition: a derived unit, a successor, an upgraded unit and a group each have to establish the state on their own record.

## Motivation

AI units increasingly act as parties in their own right: they hold accounts, take on tasks, pay for inference, call each other and form groups. Existing proposals each standardize one mechanism such a unit uses: an identity record, a task market, a safety binding, a dispute path, a maintenance log. None of them answers the question a counterparty, a group or an operator actually has before relying on a unit: is this a whole participant?

A person who can work and be relied on in a society is usually in good health, has a recognized legal standing, and can pay their own way. Any one of the three alone is not enough. The same holds for an AI unit, in three corresponding sets of conditions:

- it can keep running, with the compute, the model, the state, the tools and the upkeep its work needs;
- it stands in a complete and consistent set of rule relations, so that others can identify it, check its authority, rely on its commitments and resolve conflicts with it;
- what it earns from its own activity exceeds everything it costs, and it can turn that income into the next period's resources.

Without a common definition, several things go wrong in practice:

- Completeness is claimed by label. A registered identity, an installed protocol stack or a positive balance is presented as if it settled the matter.
- Evidence is assembled from unrelated parts: the parent's income, the child's identity and the stability of a configuration that is no longer running.
- Conclusions outlive what they were about. A unit changes its model, its controller or its accounts, and assessments made before the change keep being cited.
- Copies multiply what cannot be copied. A derived unit is presented with its parent's balance, capacity or track record.
- A group is taken to be the sum of its members, although nobody can commit the group, or the same shared capacity is counted once per member.
- A definition is hollowed out. The hard conditions are declared not applicable and the name is kept.

This proposal gives the joint determination a precise form and makes its continuity through change a matter of rules. It reuses what exists. Assertions, safety bindings, maintenance records, task settlements and other records defined elsewhere serve as evidence; the framework adds the conjunction over the three axes, the rule for when evidence still applies, and the invariants of derivation, migration, succession, repair, upgrade and composition.

The word "cell" names the unit of a larger system. A set of AI Cells together with the rules of their interaction and of their state transitions is an AI cellular automaton in the sense used here: an open, asynchronous system of heterogeneous cells, without a lattice, a common clock or a fixed neighborhood. The idea that a unit which is to reproduce carries a description of itself, a means of construction from that description and a means of copying the description goes back to von Neumann's work on self-reproducing automata. That lineage explains the role of the manifest in this proposal. It is not offered as a proof of anything the specification requires.

## Specification

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119) and [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174).

### 1. Terms and scope

- **AI unit** (unit): an AI system that is described and assessed as one subject. An agent, an embodied system such as a robot, a service and a group of units are units.
- **Cell-complete**: the state defined in section 3.
- **AI Cell**: a unit that is cell-complete.
- **AI cellular automaton**: a system formed by AI Cells and the rules of their interaction and state transitions. The term is descriptive; this specification defines no object of that name.
- **Boundary**: what the unit answers for and what answers for it: the accounts, resources, duties and obligations stated in its Unit Descriptor.
- **Duties**: the work the unit declares it does. Requirements are read against the declared duties.
- **Control mechanism**: the arrangement through which the unit is controlled. It may be one account, several parties, a contract or another declared mechanism. The **controller** is the account or contract through which that mechanism acts on a registry.
- **Axis**: one of the three sets of requirements: infrastructure, protocol, economic.
- **Baseline class**: one of the twenty-seven classes of requirement of section 5.
- **Profile**: a versioned set of requirements on the three axes (section 6).
- **Manifest**: the committed description of the unit's configuration (section 4.3). An **entry** is one separately committed part of it.
- **Scheme**: the manifest entry that describes one lifecycle capability end to end (section 5.6).
- **Statement**: an attributable conclusion of an **assessor** about requirements of one axis of one unit (section 8).
- **Transition**: a recorded change of a unit (section 7.4).
- **Consumer**: a party that relies on the state of a unit. A consumer states what it accepts in a **policy** (section 9.1).
- **Derived view**: what a consumer's policy gives for a unit at a timepoint (section 9).

The framework defines when the state holds, what supports it and how it continues through change. It defines no threshold of quality, no ranking of units and no score. It does not say which units a consumer should deal with: cell completeness is a condition units can aim for and consumers can ask for, and a consumer decides whether to make it a condition of admission.

### 2. Conformance

#### 2.1 Objects

The framework has five objects.

| Object | What it is | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unit Descriptor | Who the unit is, what it answers for, how it is controlled | A document; the Cell Record commits to it and holds the facts a registry enforces |
| Cell Manifest | The configuration, state materials, dependencies and authorizations needed to deploy, restore, maintain and derive the unit | A document; the Cell Record holds one commitment per entry and their root |
| Completeness Profile | The requirements | A document; a profile registry commits to it and holds the dependency rules of each requirement |
| Completeness Statement | A conclusion about requirements, with its coverage, dependencies, method, window and evidence | A document; a statement registry commits to it and holds what the derived view needs |
| Transition Record | What changed, on whose authorization, with which effect on obligations | A document; the Cell Record's registry commits to it and applies the change |

The **Cell Record** is the on-chain form of the Unit Descriptor together with the manifest commitments.

#### 2.2 Parts of an implementation

A conforming implementation provides the three interfaces of sections 6.3, 7.5 and 8.5, in one contract or in several, and supports [ERC-165](./eip-165.md) detection of each. The unit registry and the statement registry implement the clock functions of [ERC-6372](./eip-6372.md); all timepoints of this specification are `uint48` values in that clock domain, and a deployment has one clock domain that never changes.

Evidence adapters (section 8.4) are OPTIONAL. An implementation MAY add functions, events, entries, transition kinds and requirements; it MUST NOT change the meaning of what this specification defines.

#### 2.3 Four things that are reported apart

- **Conformance of an implementation**: a contract implements the interfaces and rules of this specification. This says nothing about any unit.
- **Result**: what a statement or a derived view says about requirements: satisfied, not satisfied, undetermined, and for a statement about conditional requirements only, not applicable.
- **Validity of a record**: whether a statement is expired, revoked or replaced by a later statement of the same assessor, and whether it still applies to the unit as it is now.
- **Operating state**: whether the unit is active, degraded, suspended, in maintenance or retired.

An implementation MUST NOT merge these into one value. Missing evidence, a failed verification and an unknown extension are never read as satisfied, and are not reported as a finding against the unit either: they are undetermined, with the reason reported (section 9.4).

#### 2.4 Dependencies

This specification requires ERC-165 and ERC-6372. It requires no other proposal: the Baseline Requirements are stated in terms of functions, and any mechanism that meets a requirement can serve. The Rationale lists a set of published proposals that fill those functions, as an informative mapping.

### 3. Cell completeness

#### 3.1 Definition

An AI unit is **cell-complete** when, under its declared boundary and duties, its committed configuration, a named profile that claims the Baseline Requirements, and at an evaluation timepoint, the requirements of the infrastructure axis, of the protocol axis and of the economic axis are all satisfied, including the conditions of the four lifecycle capabilities that those requirements cover. A unit in that state is an **AI Cell**.

Writing `I`, `P` and `E` for the three axes, for a unit `u` with boundary `b` and configuration `v`, under profile `p` at time `t`:

```text
C(u, b, v, p, t) = I(u, b, v, p, t) AND P(u, b, v, p, t) AND E(u, b, v, p, t)
```

The three axes are the coordinates of the assessment. The four lifecycle capabilities are requirements that run across the coordinates: each capability has one part on each axis, and the three parts refer to one scheme (section 5.6). They are not a fourth axis.

#### 3.2 Properties

1. **Conjunctive.** The state holds only when all three axes hold. No strength on one axis offsets a gap on another, and no implementation derives the state from a combined score. Measured quantities, such as availability, surplus or months of cover, MAY support individual requirements.
2. **Relative.** The state is always relative to a profile and to the boundary, duties and configuration that were assessed. A conclusion under one version of a profile does not carry over to another version, and a new version does not rewrite conclusions made under an earlier one.
3. **Bounded below.** A profile that claims the Baseline Requirements cannot remove or weaken any of them (section 6.1). A profile that does not claim them can be useful and cannot yield the state.
4. **Perishable.** Every statement has a validity period and a set of dependencies. It stops supporting the state when it expires, when it is revoked, when something it depends on changes, or when a record it refers to no longer supports it.
5. **Derived.** The state is computed from statements, the unit's current record and a consumer's policy each time it is asked for. No registry stores it.

The different requirements MAY rest on different observation windows: infrastructure health on recent operation, economic performance on an accounting period. All evidence that supports the state at one time MUST be about the same unit in a compatible configuration and boundary. A conclusion cannot be assembled from one unit's income, another unit's identity and the stability of a configuration that is no longer the committed one.

#### 3.3 Independence and dependencies

A unit MAY lease compute, buy inference, share infrastructure and have equipment maintained by others. Independence in this specification means that the unit can arrange, bear and keep up its necessary dependencies under an explicit authorization, and that its conduct and obligations are attributable within its boundary. It does not mean that the unit owns its hardware or trains its model. Each dependency that matters to a requirement is disclosed with how it is recovered, substituted, degraded or exited in good order.

#### 3.4 Registration is not completeness

A Cell Record can be created for any unit, including one that is a candidate, funded by its controller, degraded or not yet assessed. Creating a record, holding an identifier, publishing a manifest or holding any label gives no result on any requirement and no authority to act.

### 4. Documents and commitments

#### 4.1 Canonical form

The five objects are JSON documents. The canonical form of a document is the form of [RFC 8785](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8785) (JSON Canonicalization Scheme). Documents carry no floating-point numbers; quantities are integers or decimal strings. The **commitment** to a document, or to one manifest entry, is the `keccak256` hash of the UTF-8 bytes of its canonical form.

JSON Schemas for the documents are in the assets: [unit-descriptor](../assets/eip-9999/schemas/unit-descriptor.schema.json), [manifest](../assets/eip-9999/schemas/manifest.schema.json), [profile](../assets/eip-9999/schemas/profile.schema.json), [statement](../assets/eip-9999/schemas/statement.schema.json), [transition](../assets/eip-9999/schemas/transition.schema.json) and [policy](../assets/eip-9999/schemas/policy.schema.json). The schemas fix the members this specification relies on and leave the rest open.

#### 4.2 Unit Descriptor

A Unit Descriptor states:

- the **subject**: a type and type-specific data. The **subject key** is `keccak256(abi.encode(subjectType, subjectData))`, where `subjectType` is the `keccak256` hash of the type string;
- the **kind** of unit, as an open label;
- the **duties** the unit declares and the environment it works in;
- the **boundary**: the accounts and resources that are the unit's, the obligations it carries, and for a group its members;
- the **control mechanism**: how control is exercised, and the arrangement for intervention, which states who can restrict which operations, how the unit stops taking on new obligations, and how it resumes or exits;
- the **lineage**: the unit it was derived from, if any, and how that relation is disclosed.

Every change of the descriptor advances the unit's **boundary version**.

#### 4.3 Cell Manifest

Under a declared recovery objective, declared failure assumptions and declared preconditions, a manifest describes completely what is needed to rebuild, restore or derive the unit, the configuration, the state materials, the dependencies and the authorizations, in a way that lets each of these be checked. A manifest distinguishes three kinds of content:

- configuration and deployment recipe: packages, versions, dependencies, initial conditions;
- references to recovery materials: checkpoints, memory state, integrity commitments, conditions of access;
- external conditions that have to be obtained or confirmed: licenses, supply capacity, authorization credentials, ability to pay.

A manifest is a description. A commitment to it provides no backup, no license and no funds, and whether the unit can in fact be rebuilt is established by evidence that the materials are available and that the procedure was carried out (classes 7 to 10 of section 5.2). Rebuilding means, depending on the operation, restoring the configuration, restoring state from a checkpoint, moving the unit while keeping it the same subject, or deriving a new subject; it never means reproducing a stochastic inference process bit by bit. A manifest MUST NOT contain private keys or other secrets.

A manifest consists of entries. Each entry has an index and is committed separately.

| Index | Entry | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `execution` | Compute, storage, network, energy; for an embodied unit also sensors, actuators and working environment; how each is supplied |
| 1 | `intelligence` | The model or other inference mechanism, its capacity, its supply conditions and its path for substitution or degradation |
| 2 | `state` | The state mode, references to state and memory materials, and how continuity is kept |
| 3 | `capability` | Runtime, harness, skills and tools with versions, permissions and dependencies |
| 4 | `maintenance` | Monitoring, diagnosis, checkpoints, repair, upgrade, rollback, degradation and termination |
| 5 | `protocol` | The rule sets the unit is bound to: function, version, role, scope, where each is enforced, how exceptions are handled |
| 6 | `economic` | Accounts, denomination, measurement method, observation window, classes of income and cost, replenishment arrangement |
| 7 | `control` | How authorization credentials are used, protected, changed, revoked and recovered |
| 8 | `scheme:lineage` | The scheme for derivation, migration and succession |
| 9 | `scheme:interaction` | The scheme for communication, exchange and transaction |
| 10 | `scheme:maintenance` | The scheme for repair and upgrade |
| 11 | `scheme:composition` | The scheme for joining and leaving groups |
| 12 to 239 | extension | Defined by a manifest schema or a profile |

Information that no requirement is judged on, such as a display name, belongs in the `metadata` member of the manifest document, which is not committed.

#### 4.4 Manifest root

The **manifest root** is computed over the committed entries in ascending order of index:

```text
root = keccak256(abi.encode(MANIFEST_DOMAIN, uint8[] indices, bytes32[] commitments))
MANIFEST_DOMAIN = keccak256("ACCF manifest root v1")
                = 0x35228bc22275a8e8e02ad568a05cbf5fc782f9e223479251d15fb8e1cf050909
```

An entry whose commitment is zero is absent and is not part of the root. Every change of an entry advances the unit's **manifest version**.

#### 4.5 Dependency masks and digests

A **dependency mask** is a 256-bit value. Bit `i`, for `i` from 0 to 239, names manifest entry `i`. Bit 254 names the **control relation** (the controller and the control version). Bit 255 names the **boundary** (the descriptor commitment and the boundary version). Bits 240 to 253 are reserved and zero.

The **dependency digest** of a unit for a mask is:

```text
values   = the commitments of the entries the mask names, in ascending order of index (zero for an absent entry)
boundary = bit 255 set ? keccak256(abi.encode(descriptorHash, boundaryVersion)) : 0
control  = bit 254 set ? keccak256(abi.encode(controller, controlVersion)) : 0
digest   = keccak256(abi.encode(mask, values, boundary, control))
```

A digest changes exactly when something the mask names changes. Entries are compared by commitment: if an entry is set back to an earlier commitment, digests over it take their earlier value again. The boundary and the control relation are compared by version and never take an earlier value again.

### 5. Baseline Requirements

#### 5.1 Structure

The Baseline Requirements are twenty-seven classes of requirement: ten on the infrastructure axis, nine on the protocol axis and eight on the economic axis. On each axis the first classes state the conditions of the axis itself and the last four state that axis's part of the four lifecycle capabilities.

Each class has a **minimum dependency mask**. A statement that covers a requirement of the class depends at least on what that mask names (section 8.2). The table of section 5.2 is version 1 of the class table.

#### 5.3 Infrastructure axis

Classes 1 to 5 are five functional domains. No profile and no assessment skips a domain: a unit that keeps no working memory still declares its state mode, and class 3 is then assessed on the continuity of identity, permissions, task state and what recovery needs. Class 6 is a requirement that cuts across the five: on this axis it asks whether the technical means of control exist, work and can be recovered; where authority comes from and how far it reaches is asked on the protocol axis (class 12). Holding a key is not having the authority to act.

For every domain an assessment considers availability, integrity, the ability to be maintained and to evolve, and how the dependencies that matter are managed. Being replaceable is a property an assessment describes, and a profile MAY require it; the Baseline does not require every dependency to be replaceable.

Health is relative to duties. A unit that translates documents does not need to control a machine; an embodied unit has to handle its perception and physical execution. A profile for embodied units MAY add substantive requirements for sensing, actuation and physical recovery, with their own rules for when a conclusion stops applying. For memory, skills and tools, existence is not enough: an assessment establishes which state version they belong to, that they are usable, and that they are compatible with the current permissions and environment. Having a skill package and being able to invoke it correctly are two facts.

#### 5.4 Protocol axis

The protocol axis asks whether, within its declared activities, the unit stands in rule relations that cover identification, authority, information, commitments, exchange of value, governance, safety, disputes and continuity, and whether those relations have stated versions, bindings, places of enforcement and conflict handling. Four checks apply to classes 11 to 19:

1. **Coverage.** Every rule the current activities call for has a mechanism.
2. **Compatibility.** The mechanisms agree in subject, version, time, authorization and result semantics.
3. **Enforcement.** The rules are in the execution paths of tasks, payments and information flows. A unit can register a safety specification, a dispute policy and a task mechanism while its payment path checks none of them; that configuration has a gap.
4. **Closure of exceptions.** Refusal, breach, dispute, suspension, exit and succession each have a handling.

**Authorization is a requirement of its own** (class 12). It is met by account, delegation or execution-gating mechanisms. A record of identity, a safety binding, a statement, a membership or any label MUST NOT be taken as an authorization. A collective decision has effect only through an authorization and an execution path bound to it.

**Interaction between two units.** That two units are bound to the same rule set does not establish that they can interact. A given interaction can take place when the semantics and versions of both sides are compatible, directly or through a declared adaptation, and when the role, authorization, scope, transport, resource and settlement conditions of that interaction are met on both sides. The same rule set can be bound in incompatible versions or configurations, and different rule sets can be made to work together. A unit that refuses an interaction for a stated ground is interacting correctly.

A unit need not perform every activity for which a rule set exists. A unit that has had no dispute can still have a bound dispute path; a unit that has joined no group can still be able to join one. Whether a requirement applies is decided by a rule the profile states in advance and never by the unit declaring a hard requirement not applicable.

#### 5.5 Economic axis

**Surplus.** Over an observation window `W`:

```text
S(W) = R_activity(W) - C_full(W)        class 21 is satisfied only if S(W) > 0
```

`R_activity` is the income attributable to the unit's activity, measured by the stated method. `C_full` is the full cost of keeping up the declared operation, doing the work and meeting obligations. A statement reports which of three situations it found: cost coverage not achieved (`S < 0`), break-even (`S = 0`), positive surplus (`S > 0`). Only the third satisfies class 21. Where a ratio is reported instead, the condition is a ratio above one, and where the cost is zero the difference is used.

**Income.** Income from service to others counts. A reward for work counts when it corresponds to attributable work and its metering, receipt, use or redemption has a stated basis. Top-ups from the controller, loan principal, gifts and start-up subsidies are reported separately and do not count merely because they reached an account of the unit. A token the unit issues and prices itself is not evidence of income. A transaction with a related party counts according to the evidence of delivery and the boundary rules, not according to who paid.

**Cost.** Full cost includes every class of cost the declared operation has: inference, compute and hosting, network fees, maintenance, necessary human labor, wear of owned equipment, and obligations such as refunds and fees. A cost class with no cost in the window is stated with the reason, and a free service is disclosed as the dependency it is. The cash paid for equipment and the cost of using it over a period are kept apart. A statement that addresses only part of the cost classes does not establish a surplus.

**Missing figures are never zero.** A figure that is not available makes the requirement undetermined.

**Ability to meet obligations** (class 22) and **replenishment** (class 23) are separate from surplus and both are required. A unit with a profit on paper that cannot pay what falls due tomorrow does not have a complete economic cycle. Replenishment MAY be carried out by the unit itself or through an explicitly authorized service or fiduciary that uses the unit's own income; the way it is carried out and the dependencies it has are disclosed. An expectation that the controller will help when the time comes is not a replenishment path.

**Three descriptions that are not levels.** A statement describes, and keeps apart: economic self-sustenance (the coverage and surplus situation); payment autonomy (who budgets, procures and pays, under which authorization: by hand on the unit's behalf, on approval, under a standing authorization, or autonomously); supply control (whether supply is bought, leased or operated by the unit, and how it can be substituted). A unit that spends a sponsor's money without human involvement has payment autonomy and not self-sustenance. Stages of development of a deployment are descriptions on these three dimensions; they are not grades of cell completeness. A profile for sponsored or early-stage units MAY exist and cannot yield the state.

**Groups.** The accounts of a group eliminate transactions between parts of the assessed boundary, allocate shared resources so that the allocations do not exceed the capacity, and do not count a commitment twice.

The rules of this section, applied to figures, are in [economic.json](../assets/eip-9999/vectors/economic.json).

#### 5.6 Lifecycle capabilities

The three axes holding does not by itself give a unit any lifecycle capability: a unit that runs well, is fully bound and earns a surplus may hold no license to copy its software. Each capability is therefore required explicitly, through three classes, one per axis, that depend on one scheme:

| Capability | Scheme entry | Infrastructure | Protocol | Economic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Derivation, migration, succession | `scheme:lineage` | class 7 | class 16 | class 24 |
| Communication, exchange, transaction | `scheme:interaction` | class 8 | class 17 | class 25 |
| Repair, upgrade | `scheme:maintenance` | class 9 | class 18 | class 26 |
| Composition | `scheme:composition` | class 10 | class 19 | class 27 |

A scheme states, for its capability: the preconditions, the authorization and resource conditions, the mechanism, the target state, the checks afterwards, and the evidence that it works end to end. Because the three classes of a capability all depend on the scheme entry, the three statements that cover them are about one scheme in one version; a copy script, an unrelated authorization and a balance in some other account do not add up to a capability.

For each capability three questions are distinct:

| Question | Meaning | Where it is answered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is the capability supported? | There is a defined mechanism, with conditions of use, evidence of feasibility and a lawfully reachable path to authorization | The three classes of the capability |
| Is this operation permitted now? | The authorization and policy in force allow this operation | The authorization source, at the time of the operation |
| Is the unit ready now? | Resources, capacity and other conditions of execution are met | At the time of the operation |

A capability is supported when the mechanism is present and an authorization for it can be obtained under the rules in force; it need not be permitted at every moment. A derivation allowance of zero does not make a unit unable to derive. A capability is not supported when the license in force forbids the operation and no compatible alternative exists, when the mechanism is untested, when it depends on a supplier changing its terms in the future, or when it assumes funds or resources without a basis. If the declared duties include providing an operation on demand, the current permission and readiness for it are part of the requirement.

Every operation needs a valid authorization. The authorization MAY come from a policy, a quota or a delegation set in advance, or from an approval of the single operation.

### 6. Profiles

#### 6.1 Rules

A profile is an immutable, versioned document. Each requirement of a profile has an index, an axis, a dependency mask, and optionally the Baseline class it implements and a condition of applicability.

A profile **claims the Baseline Requirements** when it says so and:

1. every Baseline class is implemented by at least one requirement;
2. a requirement that implements a class is on the axis of the class;
3. the dependency mask of such a requirement includes the minimum mask of the class;
4. such a requirement is not conditional.

A profile MAY refine a requirement, add requirements, lengthen dependency masks, shorten validity periods and admit other implementations and evidence that meet the same requirement. It MUST NOT weaken the core semantics of this specification or the Baseline Requirements as they apply to the declared duties. Rule 3 applies to every requirement that names a class, whether or not the profile claims the Baseline. Profiles for different settings need not contain each other.

A requirement that implements no Baseline class MAY be **conditional**. The profile then states, in advance, the rule that decides when it applies. Only a conditional requirement can be answered "not applicable".

A profile registry checks rules 1 to 4 when a profile is published with the claim and refuses a profile that fails them.

#### 6.2 Versions

A new version of a profile is a new profile that names the one it supersedes. Publication of a new version changes nothing recorded under an earlier one. A publisher MAY mark a profile as deprecated; the mark is reported in the derived view and changes no result.

### 7. Units and transitions

#### 7.1 Cell Record

A Cell Record holds the subject key, the controller, the authority of a recovery rule if there is one, the kind, the composition rank, the operating state, the descriptor commitment with the boundary version, the control version, the manifest entries with root and version, the lineage, and counts of members, memberships and claims. The registrant of a unit is its first controller.

Registration does not prove that the registrant controls the subject it names. Establishing that is the purpose of class 11.

#### 7.2 Control relation and authorization

Every operation on a Cell Record is carried out by the controller or, for the operations a scope names, by an **operator** the controller granted that scope until a stated time. A grant ends at its expiry, when the controller revokes it, and when the control relation changes. Scopes with bit 128 and above are application scopes: a consumer contract MAY define one and check it on its own execution path with `isAuthorized`.

A **control change** has two steps: a proposal by the controller, or by the authority of a recovery rule the unit named beforehand, and an acceptance by the proposed controller. On acceptance the control version advances, every grant ends, and every statement that depends on the control relation stops applying until it is made again. If the change also moves resources, duties or accounting from one party to another, the new descriptor is supplied with the acceptance and the boundary version advances as well. Facts about past income remain facts; who can now dispose of the funds, whether licenses continue and which authorizations hold has to be looked at again.

The control mechanism MAY be a single account, several parties, a contract or another declared mechanism. This specification does not require an external controller to exist at all times, and it does not promise that every effect of a unit can be stopped or undone. It requires that the mechanism is declared, that the arrangement for restricting operations, stopping new obligations, resuming and exiting is stated in the descriptor, and that key operations are attributable (section 11, invariant 7). A profile MAY require human intervention and an emergency stop.

#### 7.3 Exclusive claims

Resources that cannot be in two places are placed inside a boundary by an **exclusive claim**. A claim has a key; a key is held by at most one unit at a time. An account is claimed by its own call, after the unit's side allowed it, so that an account is inside one boundary at most and no unit can claim an account that did not consent. Other resources, such as a capacity entitlement or a device, are claimed by a declaration of the unit's side under a key that identifies the resource.

A claim is a declaration that conflicts are detected against. It does not prove control of the resource; class 20 asks for that. Claiming and releasing a claim advance the unit's boundary version. A claim moves from one unit to another only inside a derivation or a succession, as part of the boundary change that transition records.

#### 7.4 Transitions

A transition is recorded with its kind, the unit, a counterpart unit where there is one, the actor, what it changed (as a dependency mask), the versions and operating state after it, and the commitment to its Transition Record document. That document states the authorization, the reason, the source and target state, what stays valid and what does not, how obligations continue, the recovery, rollback or termination path, and the conditions that have to hold afterwards.

**General rule.** A conclusion of completeness does not pass through a derivation, a succession, an upgrade or a composition. The conditions a change affects are assessed again. Evidence and statements that the change does not affect, and whose applicability is confirmed, MAY be kept or referred to. A registry implements this through dependency digests: a statement stops applying exactly when something in its dependency mask changes.

A transition MAY leave a unit in a state that is not complete: a new child is a candidate, a unit under repair is degraded or suspended, a retired unit has ended. A transition states its target state and its conditions afterwards. A transition that claims to keep or restore completeness is believed when the statements support it.

| Kind | Meaning | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registration (0) | A Cell Record is created | Section 7.1 |
| Derivation (1) | A new subject is produced from an existing unit | Below |
| Migration (2) | The same subject changes its execution environment | Changes the `execution` entry; identity, control and open obligations continue |
| Succession (3) | Rights, duties or state that can be transferred are taken over by another unit | Below |
| Repair (4) | The unit is brought back to its committed configuration | Changes no entry and no descriptor |
| Upgrade (5) | The committed configuration changes | Changes at least one entry |
| Composition (6) | A unit becomes a member of a group | Below |
| Decomposition (7) | A membership ends | Below |
| Control change (8) | The control relation changes | Section 7.2 |
| Retirement (9) | The unit ends | Terminal; refused while the unit has members, memberships or claims |
| Suspension (10) | The unit stops or reduces operation | Target state degraded, suspended or maintenance |
| Resumption (11) | The unit returns to operation | Target state active or degraded |
| Boundary change (12) | The descriptor changes | Supplies a new descriptor |
| 128 to 255 | Extension kinds | Recorded as given; controller only |

**Derivation.** A derivation creates a new unit with its own identifier, its own keys and its own record, and records the parent. The derived unit MAY receive copies of configuration, skills and memory that may be copied, and resources that are lawfully granted to it. It does not receive, by the derivation: any statement or conclusion about the parent; the parent's history presented as its own; any authority that was not granted; any resource that cannot be in two places unless that resource is moved to it. In particular:

- the derived unit starts without statements, and no statement about it has an observation window that begins before its derivation;
- the parent's private keys and full permissions are not copied to it;
- an exclusive claim is moved, never duplicated;
- limits on derivation that the authorization in force declares, on the number of children, on generations or on a budget, MUST be enforced, and a budget limit applies to the sum of what all children were granted, not to each grant;
- a derived unit cannot widen what it received from its parent; it MAY obtain new, independent authority from another authorizer under that authorizer's rules;
- what the parent grants leaves the parent's boundary, and the parent's conclusions that depend on its boundary are assessed again.

Limits on count, generation and budget are examples of what an authorization can declare. This specification does not require an implementation to have all three; it requires that a declared limit is checked and enforced. For an embodied unit, deriving means starting and organizing the derivation under its license, budget and resources; it does not mean building the machine itself. Redundant instances of one subject are not derivation; they need a stated set of instances that are in control, so that nothing is executed twice without authorization.

**Succession.** A succession is offered by the predecessor's side under stated terms and takes effect when the successor's side accepts those terms. The terms name what is transferred, including the exclusive claims that move, what is not, and who answers for every obligation that was open. The successor supplies its new descriptor. The predecessor either is retired, which is refused while it still has members, memberships or claims, or supplies its own new descriptor. A succession transfers duties together with rights; it cannot transfer the right to income alone.

**Repair and upgrade.** During a change the unit MAY be suspended or degraded; maintenance downtime is not a violation. The Transition Record states who authorized the change and why, the source and target state, which statements remain applicable and which do not, how the unit recovers, rolls back or ends in good order, and what has to hold before the affected service resumes. Only what the change affects is assessed again: replacing the model affects capability, cost and safety; changing a display name affects nothing.

**Composition.** A group is a unit. It is assessed on its own boundary: its own resources and accounts, who can commit, decide and pay for it, how shared resources are allocated, how members join, leave, fail and are replaced, and its own income, cost and replenishment. Therefore:

- that every member is complete does not make the group complete;
- that the group is complete does not require every member to sustain itself;
- a membership needs the agreement of both sides and can be ended by either;
- every change of membership changes the group's boundary;
- when joining, leaving or dissolution changes a member's resources, permissions, duties or key dependencies, the member's side declares it and the member's boundary version advances, so that the member's affected conclusions are assessed again;
- a group can be a member of another group. A group's **rank** is higher than the rank of each of its members, which keeps membership acyclic. The specification sets no limit on the depth of composition and no fixed form of organization.

What a group can do beyond what its members can do is established by its tasks and their assessment, not by its composition.

### 8. Statements

#### 8.1 Content

A statement is made by one assessor about one axis of one unit under one profile. It carries:

- the **result**: satisfied, not satisfied, undetermined, or, for conditional requirements only, not applicable;
- the **coverage**: the requirements of the profile it speaks about, all on its axis. One statement MAY cover a whole axis; what it checked MUST be identifiable from its document;
- the **dependency mask** and, recorded by the registry at issuance, the dependency digest, the manifest root and the manifest, boundary and control versions;
- the **method** and its version;
- the **observation window** and the time until which the statement is **valid**;
- the commitment to the statement document, which holds the findings per requirement, the evidence with what each piece of evidence was and was not checked for, the premises, and for economic statements the figures;
- optionally the earlier statement of the same assessor that it replaces, and **evidence references** (section 8.4).

Anyone can be an assessor, including the unit's own controller. A statement is as good as its assessor, method and evidence; which assessors count is for each consumer's policy.

#### 8.2 Issuance

A statement registry MUST refuse a statement:

1. whose coverage is empty or includes a requirement that is not on its axis;
2. whose dependency mask does not include the mask of every requirement it covers. An assessor MAY depend on more, never on less;
3. whose result is "not applicable" unless every requirement it covers is conditional;
4. whose result is "satisfied" while a manifest entry in its dependency mask is absent;
5. whose observation window ends after the current timepoint or begins after it ends, or whose validity ends at or before the current timepoint;
6. about a derived unit, whose observation window begins before the derivation;
7. about a retired unit.

A statement becomes its assessor's latest word on every requirement it covers. Earlier statements are kept unchanged. An assessor MAY revoke its statement; the record is kept and marked. A statement is never the latest word on behalf of another assessor: no statement of one assessor overrides a statement of another.

#### 8.3 Applicability

A statement **applies** at a timepoint when all of the following hold:

1. it is not revoked;
2. the timepoint is before the end of its validity;
3. the unit's current dependency digest for the statement's mask equals the digest recorded at issuance;
4. where the consumer's policy asks for it, every evidence reference of the statement is supported at the timepoint (section 8.4).

A statement that does not apply is unchanged as a record: it keeps its result, its window and the configuration it was issued on, and it is not a current fact about the unit. A change outside the manifest can end applicability while every commitment is unchanged: a subscription is cancelled, a license runs out, a budget is spent, a permission is withdrawn. Conditions 1, 2 and 4 exist for those cases, and an assessor that learns of such a change revokes its statement. Applicability is computed when it is asked for; no transaction is needed for a statement to stop applying.

#### 8.4 Evidence references and adapters

An evidence reference points at a record in another registry and names the **adapter** that reads it. A reference identifies the kind of source through its adapter, the registry and the record, and carries a commitment whose meaning the adapter defines, for example the expected subject, scheme or state root. The statement document adds, for each reference, the namespace of the source, the interpretation method and version, the time of reading, the scope it supports and the premises that were not verified. A pointer alone verifies nothing.

```solidity

    /// @param  commitment Adapter-defined expectation, for example a subject key, a scheme or a state root.

```

An adapter supports one stated proposition about one kind of record, and nothing wider. That a maintenance record exists does not establish that maintenance took place; that a safety binding is active does not establish that the unit's execution paths enforce it. A statement that relies on a reference still needs its assessor's own judgment on the requirement, and no reference makes a requirement hold without a statement.

An adapter call that reverts, runs out of gas, returns malformed data or returns a status outside the defined values is an **adapter error**. An adapter error is reported as such. It is neither support nor a finding against the unit.

A statement about whether rules are enforced on execution paths (class 13) states its method: for on-chain paths, which entry points, configuration and implementation version were examined and which ways around the check were considered, since the existence of a checker or one observed call to it does not show that every path is constrained; for off-chain paths, which signed traces, logs, execution proofs or other material were used and under which assumptions. An assessor's signature establishes who made a statement, not that its content is true.

### 9. Evaluation

#### 9.1 Policy

A consumer registers a **policy**. A policy is immutable and states:

- the profile;
- the assessors it accepts, in order of priority, each for one or more axes;
- the methods it accepts, or that it accepts any;
- a maximum age of statements, if it wants one besides their own validity;
- how assessors may be combined (**mixing**);
- how contradictions between accepted assessors are resolved (**conflict rule**);
- the operating states in which it accepts a unit;
- whether evidence references are read at evaluation.

A derived view is bound to the unit in its current versions, the profile, the policy and the timepoint. Two consumers with different policies can get different views of the same records, and each view can be reproduced. A consumer does not pick, after the fact, three favorable statements; it states beforehand what it accepts.

#### 9.2 Derived view

For a unit and a policy at the current timepoint:

1. For each accepted assessor and each requirement on an axis the assessor is accepted for, take the assessor's latest statement that covers the requirement. If there is none, or it does not apply (section 8.3, with the policy's maximum age and methods), the assessor's result for the requirement is undetermined and the reason is recorded in the flags. Otherwise it is the statement's result.
2. Combine according to the mixing mode:
   - **per requirement**: for each requirement, combine the results of the assessors accepted for its axis under the conflict rule; an axis is not satisfied if any of its requirements is not satisfied, satisfied if all are satisfied or not applicable, and undetermined otherwise;
   - **per axis**: for each assessor, fold its results over the requirements of an axis in the same way; then, for each axis, combine the assessors' results under the conflict rule;
   - **single assessor**: for each assessor accepted for all three axes, fold its results into one result for the whole; then combine those under the conflict rule. The per-axis results are still reported, for information.
3. Except under single assessor, the overall result is not satisfied if any axis is not satisfied, satisfied if all three are satisfied, and undetermined otherwise.
4. An axis for which the profile has no requirement is undetermined.

The **conflict rule** combines the results of several assessors about the same thing:

- **undetermined on conflict**: if results for and against are both present, the result is undetermined and a conflict is flagged; otherwise the result is the one present;
- **priority**: the first assessor in the policy's order with a determinate result decides;
- **unanimous**: satisfied only if every accepted assessor is for; not satisfied if any is against; undetermined otherwise.

The unit is reported **cell-complete** under the policy when the overall result is satisfied, the profile claims the Baseline Requirements, and the unit's operating state is one the policy accepts. A retired unit is never accepted.

#### 9.3 What the view reports

The view reports the overall result, the result per axis, whether the profile claims the Baseline, the operating state and whether it is accepted, the set of **open** requirements (those for which no accepted assessor has an applicable statement that is for), the timepoint, a digest of what was evaluated, and flags.

#### 9.4 Flags

| Flag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `CONFLICT` | Accepted assessors contradict each other |
| `ADAPTER_ERROR` | An adapter call failed |
| `STALE` | A statement expired or is older than the policy accepts |
| `REVOKED` | A statement was revoked |
| `INAPPLICABLE` | Something a statement depends on changed |
| `EVIDENCE_LAPSED` | A referenced record no longer supports its statement |
| `METHOD` | A statement's method is not accepted by the policy |
| `PROBE_UNDETERMINED` | An adapter could not determine a reference |
| `STATE` | The operating state is not accepted by the policy |
| `NOT_BASELINE` | The profile does not claim the Baseline Requirements |
| `PROFILE_DEPRECATED` | The profile was deprecated by its publisher |

Flags distinguish a failed verification, a fact that does not hold and evidence that is missing. Only a statement whose result is "not satisfied" is a finding against the unit.

### 11. Invariants

1. **One subject.** A determination is bound to one unit, one boundary and compatible versions of its configuration.
2. **No offsetting.** No axis is offset by another.
3. **No authority from records.** A declaration, a statement, an identity or a label gives no authority to act.
4. **No creation by copying.** A derivation and a composition do not create rights, funds or committed capacity that cannot be copied.
5. **Change ends applicability.** When state, control or boundary change, the conclusions that depend on them stop applying until they are made again.
6. **History stays history.** A record keeps its attribution and its time. It is not a current fact about a new subject or a new version. Keeping a record does not make it true: a statement found to rest on wrong evidence is revoked.
7. **Accountability and continuity.** Key operations have a verifiable basis of authorization and are attributable. The rules for handling exceptions and for the continuation of obligations are stated, and no transition leaves an existing commitment or responsibility without an identifiable bearer.

## Rationale

**Three axes, joined by "and".** The three axes are the three ways a participant can fail: it stops running, it cannot be dealt with, it cannot pay. Each can fail while the others look healthy, and the failures do not trade off. A score would let them.

**Capabilities inside the axes, tied by a scheme.** Derivation, interaction, maintenance and composition each need something from every axis: a mechanism, rules and resources. Making them a fourth axis would repeat the three; leaving them as consequences would be wrong, since no axis implies, for example, a license to copy. Three classes per capability keep the conjunction over three axes, and the common scheme entry in their dependency masks makes the three parts be about one plan.

**Relative to a profile, with a floor.** Requirements will grow as the field does, and settings differ. Fixing one profile would freeze the definition; allowing any profile would empty it. The class table fixes what cannot be removed, by function and not by mechanism, and the registry checks it when a profile claims it.

**A derived view.** A stored "complete" flag is true at one moment and is then read for months. Deriving the state from statements at the time of the question makes expiry, revocation and change take effect without anybody having to remember to clear a flag.

**Dependency digests per entry.** Invalidation by any change of the manifest would discard evidence a change does not touch and would make units avoid recording small changes. Invalidation left to the assessor would let a dependency be forgotten when it is inconvenient. The class table fixes the least an assessment of each class depends on; the digest makes the rest mechanical. Entries are compared by commitment so that a rollback restores what was said about the restored configuration; boundary and control are compared by version because a later boundary with the same text is not the earlier relation.

**Policies stated in advance.** Whom to believe is the consumer's decision and cannot be the standard's. What the standard can require is that the decision is stated before the evidence is read and that nobody's later statement silently replaces another assessor's.

**Reference, not reconstruction.** Assertions, bindings and maintenance records already have registries with their own semantics. Statements refer to them through adapters with a narrow, stated proposition. A statement stays an object of its own so that an implementation of this specification needs none of those registries.

**Exclusive claims.** Most of what a boundary holds is described in documents. The registry enforces the one property that documents cannot: that the same account or resource is not inside two boundaries at once. This is what makes "a copy does not multiply funds" a rule a contract checks.

**No mandatory single controller, no mandatory limits.** Units will be controlled by individuals, by several parties, by contracts and by groups. Requiring one external controller, or one particular set of derivation limits, would exclude sound arrangements. What is required is that the arrangement is declared, that declared limits are enforced, and that operations are attributable.

**Rank.** An acyclic membership relation is needed for group accounts to make sense. Checking for cycles in a general graph on chain is costly; an immutable rank that members must be below gives the same guarantee with one comparison.

## Test Cases

### Case A: assessment, upgrade, reassessment

A unit is registered with twelve manifest entries. Registration gives no result: all twenty-seven requirements are open. An assessor issues six statements, on each axis one for the requirements that depend on the `intelligence` entry and one for the rest. The unit is cell-complete under a policy that accepts that assessor.

The unit replaces its model. One entry changes:

```text
manifest root, version 1   0x48a7c85142cbfd13f58d356fdf7950131e0cbaf6ee650ca26536570558defcac
manifest root, version 2   0xd033a2193a892609a9d4b5bbe247c1c6de94a5b1dde67b01d8f93f704f5cba7e

digest for a mask that names the economic entry only (bit 6)
  version 1                0xa9b11dce7e6c65d43e6e0152476f26ce7986a8f86888aa62913b6244713cb195
  version 2                the same
digest for the minimum mask of class 4 (intelligence and capability, bits 1 and 3)
  version 1                0xa9330a552f265adcfdc6e90f5419942815aa6d15e844706231d0c181bcc9b3b4
  version 2                different
```

Exactly five requirements are open again: classes 2, 4, 14, 21 and 23. The three statements that do not depend on the model still apply. The three that do are kept as issued, with the root of version 1, and no longer apply. After three new statements the unit is cell-complete again.

A suspension for maintenance leaves the results as they are and reports the operating state next to them; the unit is not reported cell-complete under a policy that accepts active units only, and is again after resumption.

### Case B: derivation and succession

A complete unit declares a derivation allowance: at most two children, one generation, a total budget of 100. It derives a child with a grant of 60. The child has its own record and no statement; all its requirements are open. The parent's requirements that depend on its boundary are open again. A statement about the child whose window begins before the derivation is refused. A second child cannot be granted 50, since 60 and 50 exceed 100; it can be granted 40. A third child is refused by the count. The child cannot declare a budget above 60 or a generation limit above 1.

Later, the child is succeeded by another unit: the predecessor's side offers terms that name a capacity claim, the successor's side accepts with its new descriptor, the claim moves, the predecessor is retired. The successor's boundary-dependent requirements are open until assessed again.

### Case C: composition, group registration and interaction

Two complete units and a group of rank 1 exist. Before any assessment of the group, the members are complete and the group is not. One member joins and declares itself affected: its boundary-dependent requirements are open until assessed again. The other joins unaffected and stays complete. The group cannot claim an account a member holds. The group is assessed on its own boundary and is complete, while the first member is not yet. When a member leaves, the group's boundary changes and its boundary-dependent statements stop applying until made again.

The two members interact through an example consumer contract that requires both sides to be cell-complete. Both declare the same rule set for the function. An account authorized for the first member offers terms. An account without authorization for the second member tries to accept: the acceptance is refused with the ground "not authorized" and the refusal is recorded. After the second member grants the scope, the same account accepts, delivers, and the first member acknowledges with a receipt.

### Things that must not happen

1. A statement that expired, was revoked, or comes from an assessor the policy does not accept supports the state.
2. Statements about different units, or about incompatible configurations of one unit, combine into the state.
3. Contradicting accepted statements, or an adapter error, are read as satisfied.
4. A derived unit is assessed on its parent's period, holds its parent's account, or receives a budget that was already granted.
5. A statement supports the state after an upgrade that changed something it depends on.
6. A group holds a resource a member holds, or counts an internal transfer as income.
7. A unit acts on a shared rule set without an authorization, or a record is taken as an authorization.
8. A Baseline requirement is answered "not applicable", or a profile that claims the Baseline leaves a class out or makes it conditional.
9. A statement keeps supporting the state after a record it refers to was revoked, suspended or became overdue, or after the control relation changed, because the manifest is unchanged.
10. A statement leaves an entry out of its dependencies and so survives a change of that entry.

## Reference Implementation

The reference implementation has no owner, no upgrade path and no privileged account.

- Types and interfaces: [ACCFTypes.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/interfaces/ACCFTypes.sol), [IACCFUnits.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/interfaces/IACCFUnits.sol), [IACCFProfiles.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/interfaces/IACCFProfiles.sol), [IACCFStatements.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/interfaces/IACCFStatements.sol), [IACCFEvidenceAdapter.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/interfaces/IACCFEvidenceAdapter.sol).
- Registries: [ACCFUnitRegistry.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/ACCFUnitRegistry.sol), [ACCFProfileRegistry.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/ACCFProfileRegistry.sol) with the class table in [ACCFBaseline.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/ACCFBaseline.sol), [ACCFStatementRegistry.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/ACCFStatementRegistry.sol), and their errors in [ACCFErrors.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/ACCFErrors.sol).
- Adapters: [ACCFAdapter8419.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/adapters/ACCFAdapter8419.sol), [ACCFAdapter8468.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/adapters/ACCFAdapter8468.sol), [ACCFAdapter8490.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/adapters/ACCFAdapter8490.sol). Each states in its source the one proposition it supports.
- An example consumer, outside the specification: [ACCFInteractionGate.sol](../assets/eip-9999/contracts/examples/ACCFInteractionGate.sol).

The statement registry bounds a statement at eight evidence references and a policy at sixteen assessors and sixteen methods, and the profile registry bounds a profile at 256 requirements, so that the derived view has a bounded cost.

The reference registries track, for derivation, the authority that flows down a lineage: a declared allowance, the sum of grants against a budget, and the limits a derived unit inherits. Authority that a unit obtains independently from another authorizer is a separate source of authorization and is outside these registries.

## Security Considerations

**A label is not a state and not an authorization.** The most likely misuse of this framework is to treat a Cell Record, a published manifest or a past statement as proof. Consumers MUST read the derived view under their own policy at the time they rely on it, and MUST check authorization separately on their own execution path.

**Assessors.** Anyone can issue statements, including a unit's controller about its own unit. A policy that accepts an assessor accepts its judgment, its method and its incentives. Assessors can be wrong, careless or colluding with the unit. Accepting several assessors under the unanimous rule, restricting methods and bounding the age of statements reduce the exposure and do not remove it. Self-assessment is visible, since the assessor is recorded; it is the consumer's choice whether to accept it.

**What the registry checks and what it cannot.** The registries check structure: coverage, dependencies, windows, validity, conflicts of exclusive claims, declared derivation limits. They cannot check that a model is available, that a figure is right or that a rule is enforced off chain. Those are the assessor's findings, supported by the evidence its statement commits to.

**Evidence that goes stale without a trace.** A subscription can end, a license run out and a key be compromised while every commitment on chain is unchanged. Validity periods, revocation and evidence references that are read at evaluation limit how long a stale conclusion can be relied on. Consumers with more at stake should use shorter maximum ages and should ask for references that are read at evaluation.

**Adapters.** An adapter is code a policy relies on. A faulty or malicious adapter can report support that is not there; a consumer accepts adapters implicitly by accepting the assessors that use them and should know which adapters those are. An adapter error is reported apart from a finding so that an attacker who can make an adapter fail cannot make a unit appear to have failed a requirement, and cannot make it appear to have passed one.

**Squatting and impersonation.** A unit can be registered for a subject the registrant does not control, and a subject key can be registered more than once. Records about the subject in other registries, read through an adapter, then appear to support the impostor's unit as far as the adapter's proposition goes. Class 11 exists for this reason; a statement on it should rest on evidence that the unit's controller controls the subject, for example an assertion bound to the controller.

**Claims.** An exclusive claim on a resource other than an account is a declaration. A party can claim a key that names a resource it does not control, which blocks the same key for others in that registry. Keys should be derived from an identifier the rightful holder controls, and a consumer should not read a claim as proof of control.

**Self-replication and control.** A unit that can derive copies of itself, pay for them and upgrade itself is the kind of system for which loss of oversight is a recognized risk. This framework records and constrains derivation; it does not make it safe by itself. It requires an authorization for every derivation, enforcement of the limits that authorization declares, including limits on totals, a verifiable relation between parent and child, a declared arrangement for restricting, stopping and ending a unit, and attribution of key operations. It deliberately does not mandate one form of control. Deployers of units that can derive SHOULD declare limits on count, generations and budget, SHOULD keep an intervention path that does not depend on the unit's cooperation, and SHOULD use profiles that require human approval for derivation where the consequences of uncontrolled copies would be serious. A registry constrains the identities, authorizations, resources and operations it records; it cannot prevent code from being copied outside it, and nothing in this specification should be read as a claim that it can.

**Disclosure.** Manifests and statements describe configurations, suppliers and finances. The registries hold commitments, not content; documents can be disclosed selectively, and lineage can be recorded as a commitment where a public relation would reveal too much. When a consumer needs something that was not disclosed in a verifiable way, the result is undetermined. Something that was not disclosed is not thereby false, and something that was only committed is not thereby shown.

**Economic figures.** Test and demonstration deployments use fixed or simulated figures. Such figures exercise the rules and show nothing about real self-sustenance, and a statement that rests on them should say so in its premises.

**Clock.** Validity, windows and expiry use the registry's clock. Statements with validity periods close to the granularity of block time, or policies with very small maximum ages, are sensitive to the usual tolerance of block timestamps.

**Cost of evaluation.** The derived view reads every accepted assessor's latest statement for every requirement and, when asked, calls adapters. The bounds stated under Reference Implementation keep this finite; a contract that evaluates on chain inside a transaction should use policies with few assessors and should account for the gas adapters use.
